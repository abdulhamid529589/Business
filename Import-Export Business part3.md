## 1. What is Import-Export? The Simple Definition

> *"Import-Export is not some unique or special business. It is like any other business with its own pros and cons."*

**Export:** Any product made in your country that you want to sell abroad

**Import:** Any product made abroad that you bring into India to sell

**The business model is simple:**
- Source a product
- Find a buyer in another country (or find a product from another country for Indian buyers)
- Handle documentation and logistics
- Earn the price difference as your margin

**However:** 90% of people who enter this field fail — not because the business is hard, but because of **wrong mindset and wrong approach**.

## 2. Why 90% of People Fail — The Root Cause

The single biggest reason: **They try to sell what THEY want to sell, not what the MARKET wants to buy.**

### The Water Glass Example

> *"If you want to sell a water glass to Tanzania, you need to know what size the Tanzanian market uses. If you insist on selling the Indian size, the market will reject it. 90% of people fail because they are adamant about selling their preferred product."*

### The Consumer Psychology Principle

- Customers buy what THEY want, not what YOU want to sell
- Generating demand for a new product requires enormous capital
- Always sell what is already in demand or in **rising demand**

**The correct approach:**
1. First identify the market
2. Understand consumer psychology in that market
3. Find what is in demand there
4. Then procure and sell that product

## 3. How to Research Which Product to Sell

Go to the e-commerce platforms of the target country and observe:

**Examples:**

### Method 2: Trade Data Research

- Port data (import-export records from customs)

### Method 3: On-Ground Research

- Visit trade fairs in India (EPCH, CEPC exhibitions)
- Talk to exporters in your target product category
- Visit the manufacturing hubs of products

### Important Note

> *"You will always get estimates, never exact figures. Use those estimates to plot your roadmap. Business is always about estimation and risk management."*

## 4. Import vs. Export — Where is the Bigger Money?

### The Key Difference

| Parameter | Export | Import |
|---|---|---|
| **Capital flow** | Money comes TO you (you receive) | Money goes FROM you (you pay upfront) |
| **Government support** | Extensive — GST refunds, schemes, incentives | Minimal support |
| **Safety** | Exporter is protected | Importer takes most of the risk |
| **Competition** | High but manageable | Extremely high — everyone tries to source from China |
| **Ease for beginners** | More recommended | Riskier, more capital needed |
| **Profit margins** | Both are comparable overall | Can be higher but risk is higher |

### The Competition Reality in Import

> *"In import, everyone is trying to procure from China and sell in India. Even a local shopkeeper can now directly order from China with just a phone call. The competition is at the micro level."*

**Result:** Import margins are being squeezed because:
- Direct B2C sourcing from China via Alibaba/1688 is possible for anyone
- Local retailers bypass middlemen by ordering themselves
- Price competition is intense

### Government Support Comparison

| Export | Import |
|---|---|
| GST refund on exported goods | Almost zero government support |
| Export promotion schemes | No incentives |
| DGFT support | No equivalent |
| LUT facility | None |

**Conclusion:** For beginners, **export is significantly safer and better supported** than import.

## 5. Essential Commodities — Why Beginners Must Avoid Them

### What Are Essential Commodities?

Products like:
- Wheat (atta), pulses (dal), rice, vegetables
- Onions, sugar, edible oils
- Any staple food product

### Why These Are a "Suicide Formula" for Beginners

**Problem 1: Government Bans**

India regularly imposes export bans on essential commodities to control domestic prices:

| Product | Current Status (as of recording) |
|---|---|
| Wheat | Export banned |
| Some rice varieties | Banned |
| Onions | Previously banned; then export duty imposed |
| Sugar | Restrictions applied periodically |

> *"Bans can come and go overnight. What's allowed today may be banned tomorrow. The government does this to protect domestic market prices."*

**Problem 2: Mandibhav (Market Rate) Pricing**

Essential commodities are priced like a mandi (wholesale market):
- Today's price ≠ tomorrow's price
- Prices are volatile and beyond your control
- You have to sell at whatever the market rate is that day

**Problem 3: The Averaging Problem**

Explained through example:
- 3 containers/month × ₹10 lakh each = ₹30 lakh/month turnover
- Some months: profit
- Some months: loss
- Some months: break even
- Only your **annual average** tells if you made money

Beginners cannot handle this mental and financial stress of averaging because:
- Insufficient capital buffer
- Weak mindset (can't handle month-to-month losses)
- No experience to predict when prices will recover

**Verdict:** Essential commodities = avoid unless you have ₹1–2 crore capital and 2+ years of experience.

## 6. FCL vs. LCL — Understanding Shipment Types

### FCL — Full Container Load

- You hire the **entire container** (20ft, 40ft, or High Cube 40ft)
- Your goods fill the whole container
- More cost-effective per unit for large quantities
- Better for established exporters with bulk orders

### LCL — Less than Container Load

- You hire **part of a container** (e.g., 5 feet, 10 feet of space)
- Your small box/shipment shares space with other exporters' goods
- Ideal for:
  - Beginners testing a new market
  - Small trial/sample shipments
  - High-value, low-volume goods

### When to Use Which

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Starting out, small order | LCL |
| Testing a new buyer | LCL |
| Established relationship, bulk order | FCL |
| High-margin, low-weight goods | LCL (more economical) |
| Commodities, large quantities | FCL |

### Special Container Types

| Type | Use Case |
|---|---|
| **20ft Standard** | Dense/heavy goods |
| **40ft Standard** | General cargo |
| **40ft High Cube** | Light, bulky goods (disposables, textiles) |
| **Reefer/Refrigerated** | Fresh produce, temperature-sensitive goods |

## 7. Top 5 Products to Export from India

> *"If you want to make good money in exports, always choose finished products — not raw materials or commodities."*

### #1 — Textiles

- India is one of the world's largest textile producers
- Massive variety: cotton, silk, synthetic, blended
- Global demand is consistent and growing
- Easy to procure from major textile hubs: Surat, Ludhiana, Tirupur

### #2 — Jute Products

- **Eco-friendly** — carries a "Pro-Earth" certification globally
- Demand is rising due to the global sustainability movement
- Major production: Kolkata region
- Products: bags, ropes, mats, packaging, decorative items
- Fahad personally deals in jute exports

### #3 — Leather & Bio-Leather

- India is in the **top 5 globally** for leather exports
- Sourcing hubs: Kanpur, Agra, Kolkata, Chennai
- New category: **Vegan/Bio Leather**
  - Tomato leather (made from tomato plant fibres)
  - Cactus leather (made from cactus bark)
  - No moisture issues, no smell problems like real leather
  - Growing demand from animal-rights-conscious markets (Europe, USA)

### #4 — Furniture

- Good margins when sourced correctly
- India has strong craft traditions in wood and metal furniture
- Key: proper sourcing, quality showcasing, and packaging for sea freight
- Rajasthan and UP are major furniture production hubs

### #5 — Handicrafts & Exotic Items

- **Best category for beginners** (explained in detail in Section 32)
- No MRP — sell at whatever price the market accepts
- Lower quantities needed → lower capital requirement
- Easy packaging for sea freight
- High margins possible

### Products to Avoid as a Beginner

| Product | Reason to Avoid |
|---|---|
| Fresh fruits & vegetables | Perishable; mandibhav pricing; spoilage risk |
| Onions/tomatoes | Export bans, volatile prices |
| Copper/metal scrap | Huge capital needed; high fraud risk; complex regulations |
| Electronics import | Very high competition; duty complications |
| Agricultural commodities | Government ban risk |

## 8. Documents & Registrations Required (Complete List)

### Step 1 — Business Entity (choose one)

| Type | Requirements | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| **Sole Proprietorship** | Shop license (Gumasta) or GST registration | Individual starters |
| **LLP (Limited Liability Partnership)** | Minimum 2 directors | Partners starting together |
| **Private Limited Company** | Minimum 2 directors | Scaling up; investor-ready |
| **Public Limited** | Multiple directors + public shareholding | Large corporates (Tata, Reliance level) |

> *"Even a sole proprietor with a shop license can export. The government gives full freedom."*

**Key rule:** Savings bank account holders **cannot** export. You must have a **current account** in your business name.

---

### Step 2 — Current Bank Account

- Must be in the **company/firm name**
- Personal savings account is NOT allowed for export transactions
- Get this at any bank alongside your business registration

---

### Step 3 — GST Registration

**Why is GST mandatory even though exports are tax-free?**

The government needs proof that:
1. You bought goods (and paid GST)
2. You actually exported them (not sold locally to evade tax)

**How GST works in exports:**

Option A: **Pay GST while buying, claim full refund later**
- Buy goods with 18% GST
- Export them with valid shipping documents (Shipping Bill + Bill of Lading)
- Claim full 18% GST refund from government

Option B: **Buy without GST using LUT (Letter of Undertaking)**
- Get LUT from GST department
- Purchase goods without paying GST (zero-rated purchase)
- Export under the undertaking that you will export

---

### Step 4 — IEC (Import Export Code)

- Issued by **DGFT (Directorate General of Foreign Trade)**
- Fee: **₹500 only**
- Processing time: **1 day** (previously 15 days)
- Available online at dgft.gov.in — no agent needed
- **Without IEC, you cannot legally import or export anything**

> *"People used to sell IEC for ₹10,000–₹25,000. It's a ₹500 document you can get yourself in one day."*

---

### Complete Document Checklist

- [ ] Company/Business Registration (Proprietorship / LLP / Pvt Ltd)
- [ ] Current Bank Account
- [ ] GST Certificate
- [ ] IEC (Import Export Code) — ₹500, same-day online
- [ ] LUT (Letter of Undertaking) — if buying without GST

## 9. LUT — Letter of Undertaking Explained

**Full form:** Letter of Undertaking

**What it does:**
- Allows you to purchase goods **without paying GST**
- Acts as a promise to the government that you will export the goods
- Issued by the GST Department
- Your CA can help you obtain this

**When to use LUT:**
- When you want to avoid blocking capital in GST refunds
- When cash flow management is critical
- Large exporters use this routinely

**When to claim GST refund instead:**
- When you've already paid GST while purchasing
- Government refunds 5%, 12%, or 18% depending on the product category

**Documents for GST refund:**
- Shipping Bill (export document)
- Bill of Lading (shipping company document)
- These two prove the goods left India

## 11. The 5 Seller Framework — Why You Need Multiple Suppliers

For any product you plan to export, you should have **minimum 5 suppliers**.

### Why 5?

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Competitive rates** | Compare quotes; choose the best price for each order |
| **Quality options** | Different suppliers offer different quality grades |
| **Timely delivery** | If one supplier delays, others can cover |
| **Never be held hostage** | One supplier backing out won't kill your deal |
| **Negotiation power** | Competing quotes give you leverage |

### The Three Non-Negotiables You Must Deliver

1. **Competitive rates** — your buyer is comparing you with China and others
2. **Quality** — exactly what was promised, every time
3. **Timely delivery** — missing delivery windows is relationship-ending

> *"When you meet these three, you go from a one-time exporter to a serial exporter. The client's first order is based on trust in your face. The second is based on trust in your words and service. Words are money — your commitments must be honoured."*

## 13. China's Manufacturing Dominance — Why & How

### China's Export Position

- Consistently in the **top 2–3 global exporters**
- Top 3 exporters globally: USA (#1), China (#2), and a rotating 3rd

### Why China Dominates

**1. Province-wise specialisation (implemented ~1970s)**
- Each Chinese province is assigned a specific manufacturing sector
- One province: glass
- Another: toys
- Another: hardware
- Another: electronics
- Government trained workers, subsidised plants, built infrastructure

**2. Ultra-cheap labour costs**
- India: ₹600–700/day labour
- Vietnam: ~₹300/day
- China: Historically even lower → now rising but systems are so efficient it doesn't matter much

**3. Supply chain efficiency**
- From order to dispatch: often within hours for established products
- Drop-shipping possible (send 1 piece at a time)
- Indian suppliers: often won't sell less than bulk; poor responsiveness

**4. Professionalism**

| Parameter | Chinese Suppliers | Indian Suppliers |
|---|---|---|
| Quote turnaround | 15–20 minutes | Days to never |
| 1-piece orders | Yes | Refused |
| Commitment to specs | Very high | Often changed last minute |
| Working hours | Start at 8:00–8:30 AM | Shops open at 11 AM–1 PM |

**5. Volume × Low cost = Global dominance**

### The Irony

> *"China imports the most copper scrap in the world. Yet people try to buy copper scrap FROM China. Always check what a country actually exports vs. imports before choosing a supplier country."*

## 14. Luxury Goods & How Brands Use China

### The Reality of Luxury Manufacturing

- **Louis Vuitton, Gucci, and many luxury brands** manufacture in China
- LV bag that sells for ₹4 lakh is manufactured in China at ₹4,000–₹5,000
- Why? China's manufacturing is unbeatable in quality + cost

### Why Brands Still Win

- The brand (LV, Gucci) has built its identity, heritage, and marketing over decades
- China contributes manufacturing skill, not brand value
- A Chinese manufacturer cannot refuse — if they do, someone else will take the contract

### The 40% Value Addition Rule

To claim "Made in Country X," a product needs **minimum 40% value addition** in that country:
- Buying thread → making a shirt in India = "Made in India" ✓
- Buying a shirt from China and just relabelling = NOT "Made in India" ✗

### The "First Copy" / "Super Master Copy" Market

- 99% identical copies of luxury goods exist
- Available in China, Thailand, Dubai
- The original brands often know this exists and allow it — because:
  - Copies create brand awareness (who copies unknown brands?)
  - Grey market volume is massive
  - It maintains exclusivity of the genuine product (not everyone can afford real LV)

> *"Only Shah Rukh Khan gets copied. Nobody copies unknowns. The brand becomes famous through copies too."*

## 15. Unclaimed Cargo — What Happens to Abandoned Shipments

### Scenario

When imported goods arrive at a port but the buyer:
- Ran out of money
- Got scammed (paid and received nothing real)
- Simply abandoned the shipment

### What the Government Does

- Goods become **"unclaimed cargo"** after a defined period
- Government takes ownership: *"Those who have no one — the government becomes their family"*
- Published in newspapers, social media, television: "Auction notice"
- Goes to public **auction at major ports**

### Where Auctions Happen

- JNPT (Jawaharlal Nehru Port Terminal, Mumbai)
- ICD Tughlakabad (Delhi)
- Every major port has its own auction schedule

### Auction Pricing

| Actual Market Value | Auction Rate |
|---|---|
| ₹1,00,000 | ₹25,000–₹33,000 (1/3 to 1/4 of market value) |

### How to Participate

1. Register with the port/customs authority
2. Attend the auction on announced date
3. Place your bid
4. Highest bidder wins
5. Even the winning bid is 3x below market value

### Key Point

The Bill of Lading (BL) and Performa Invoice describe the contents of the container — so you know approximately what you're bidding on before the auction.

## 16. Customs Corruption — The Real Truth

### Does It Exist?

> *"Yes, money works everywhere. But it doesn't work the way Bollywood shows it."*

### How It Actually Works

It is **NOT** like movies where you hand a briefcase to an officer:

1. Officers have jobs and reputations to protect
2. They also face criminal liability (jail + career end)
3. Direct dealings are nearly impossible

**The actual mechanism:**
```
You (importer)
      ↓
Intermediary/Dalal (broker who knows the officer)
      ↓
2–3 more intermediaries (each taking a cut)
      ↓
Customs Officer (receives via trusted chain)
```

- The broker knows which officer can "help"
- Money passes through 2–3 layers
- Each layer takes their cut
- Officer only interacts with trusted intermediaries, not strangers

### Fahad's Advice

> *"This is not advice. But this is reality. Don't go down this path — the risk to you is much higher than the benefit. If the officer is caught, so are you."*

## 18. Import Duty — How It Works on Every Product

### Two Things That Apply on Every Imported Product

1. **Basic Customs Duty (BCD)** — varies by product
2. **IGST (Integrated GST)** — typically 5%, 12%, or 18%

### Duty Rates by Category

| Product Category | Duty Rate |
|---|---|
| Raw materials / unfinished goods (e.g., scrap) | 2% |
| Semi-finished goods | 10–20% |
| Finished manufactured goods | 30–50% |
| Electronics | 10–50% (varies widely) |
| Luxury cars | 100%+ |
| Robots / unusual items | 100%+ (variable) |

### The Robot Example (Dhairya's Personal Experience)

- Ordered a small robot from the USA for a video shoot
- Original cost: ~$400 (~₹33,000)
- After customs duty + IGST + handling charges: **₹40,000**
- Additional challenges:
  - Was initially flagged as a "security risk" (robots not allowed without clearance)
  - Had to reclassify it as a "music player" to get clearance
  - Took over 1 month to arrive
  - Extreme documentation headaches

> *"For a single video shot, the robot cost ₹40,000 and 1 month of headaches. Imagine what importing at scale requires."*

### Why China Still Wins Despite High Duties

Even after paying 30–50% import duty on Chinese electronics, the product is still competitive because:
- China's manufacturing cost is so low
- Example: A TV manufactured in China → exported → 30% duty applied → still cheaper than most alternatives
- Even after duty, Chinese goods land profitably in Indian markets

## 19. The Complete Export Cost Model (Step by Step)

Using **corrugated cardboard boxes (korrugated box)** as the example:

### Step 1 — Procurement Cost

₹10 per box (raw material, manufacturing, labour included)

### Step 2 — Add Profit Margin

Add 40% profit:
- ₹10 + ₹4 = **₹14 per box**

*(Note: There will be negotiation — always quote a bit higher to accommodate)*

### Step 3 — Packaging the Packaging

The boxes themselves need to be packed/bundled:
- ₹0.50 per unit for outer packaging = **₹14.50 total**

### Step 4 — Transportation to Port

From manufacturer to port (inland freight):
- ₹0.50 per unit = **₹15.00 total**

### Step 5 — Government Formalities at Port

Customs documentation, port handling, CHA fees:
- ₹0.50 per unit = **₹15.50 total** (approximately)

### Step 6 — FOB Price

**FOB = Free on Board** (includes loading into the container)

This ₹15.50 is your **FOB price** — the price up to putting goods on the vessel.

### What Happens After FOB

- Freight charges (ocean freight) = buyer or seller pays depending on Incoterm
- Insurance = buyer or seller (per agreement)
- Destination charges = buyer pays

### Bulk Makes This Work

The above is per-piece. In export, orders are bulk:
- Buyer may order 200,000 boxes
- Your ₹4 profit × 200,000 = ₹8,00,000 profit from one order
- Build relationships, get repeat orders → ₹8 lakh per shipment × multiple shipments per year

## 20. Packaging — Why It Destroys or Makes Your Business

### The Most Underestimated Factor in Export

Most beginners think: *"I have a good product and a buyer. What else is there?"*

The answer: **Packaging is everything.**

### What Can Go Wrong During Sea Transit

A 30-day voyage to Africa means your goods endure:
- Constant humidity and salt water mist (especially in monsoon months)
- Temperature fluctuations
- Physical movement/vibration from ocean waves
- Pressure from stacking inside the container

### The Cardboard Box Problem

If cardboard gets wet:
1. It softens and becomes rubbery
2. Strong odour develops (foul smell)
3. Structural integrity collapses
4. Buyer rejects the entire shipment

**Result:** No payment. No repeat order. End of relationship.

### Rules for Export-Grade Packaging

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Moisture barrier** | All packaging must be moisture-resistant or sealed |
| **Carton dimensions** | Must precisely fit the container to eliminate gaps |
| **Weight distribution** | Cartons must handle stacking weight of what goes on top |
| **Desiccants** | Use silica gel sachets inside cartons for hygiene-sensitive goods |
| **Labelling** | Clear, waterproof labels with destination info |
| **Inner packaging** | Bubble wrap, foam, or padding for fragile items |

### The Gap Problem in Containers

> *"If even a small gap is left on the side of the container, when the vessel moves in ocean waves, boxes shift into that space and get damaged."*

**Solution:** Design your carton dimensions so that when loaded, the cartons fill the container wall-to-wall with zero gap.

### Why Your Supplier Matters for Packaging

Your supplier needs to understand:
- How the product will be shipped (sea, air)
- Transit time and conditions
- Destination climate
- Stacking requirements

A supplier who doesn't care about packaging will cost you your buyer.

## 22. Merchant Exporter vs. Manufacturer Exporter

### The Two Types

| Type | Description | % of Indian Exporters |
|---|---|---|
| **Merchant Exporter** | Sources product from manufacturer; acts as trading middleman | ~60% |
| **Manufacturer Exporter** | Makes the product themselves; sells directly | ~40% |

### Merchant Exporter

**Advantages:**
- Lower capital to start
- No factory headaches (labour, electricity, quality control, ESI, government compliance)
- Flexibility to change products easily
- Can work from home initially

**Disadvantages:**
- Lower margins (manufacturer takes their share)
- Cannot control production quality directly
- Supply chain dependency
- Buyers may prefer manufacturers

### Manufacturer Exporter

**Advantages:**
- Higher revenue (control of entire value chain)
- Better credibility with international buyers (factory visit possible)
- Control over quality and delivery

**Disadvantages:**
- Enormous setup cost (factory, machines, labour, compliance)
- Regulatory burden: ESI, EPF, pollution control, fire NOC, labour inspections
- Less flexibility to change products
- The factory owner's day = managing operations, not sales

### The Manufacturer's Paradox

> *"I have manufacturer clients with ₹30 crore annual turnover who draw only ₹2 lakh salary per month. Why? Because their entire day is spent managing raw materials, labour, power supply, quality checks. Their focus on sales is minimal."*

## 23. How to Find Suppliers — Step by Step

### Step 1: Networking (Most Important)

> *"Your network is your net worth. Invest in networking. People sitting at home on the internet are losing real-world connections."*

- Attend trade fairs, exhibitions (EPCH, EEPC, Pragati Maidan)
- Join industry associations
- Visit manufacturing clusters (Surat for textiles, Kanpur for leather, Jodhpur for furniture)
- Meet people in your product's supply chain
- Physical network > Digital network for supplier trust

### Step 2: B2B Portals (Basic Level)

Post a buyer inquiry on portals like:
- IndiaMART
- TradeIndia
- Justdial

**How to use them correctly:**
- Post a query as a **buyer** (enquiring about products)
- Sellers will reach out to you
- Use this for initial price discovery only

**Warning:** Most suppliers on these portals are **merchant exporters masquerading as manufacturers**. They add their margin, making your price uncompetitive.

### Step 3: Initial Filtering Call

When a supplier contacts you:
- Schedule a call
- Exchange documents:
  - Your company registration + IEC
  - Their GST certificate, business registration, product catalogue
- Verify their background
- Ask about MOQ (minimum order quantity), pricing, lead time, samples

### Step 4: Visit Their Facility

For any significant order:
- Visit the supplier's factory or warehouse
- Physical visit confirms they exist and can produce what they claim
- Check production capacity, quality standards, worker conditions

### Step 5: Sample Order

Always place a small sample/trial order before committing to bulk:
- Verify quality matches what was shown
- Test packaging and labelling
- Check delivery time against commitment

### Step 6: Build the Relationship

> *"Sourcing is not a transaction — it's a partnership. Your supplier is your partner in crime. If they give you wrong rates or quality, you can't sell anything. No matter how many promises you make to buyers."*

Relationship-building takes weeks to months but pays for years.

## 25. The Client Filtration System

### The Core Principle

> *"A well used the filtration system is worth more than 1,000 cold emails."*

### Step 1: The Minimum Commitment Test

Fahad's method: Before engaging seriously with any potential buyer, require them to:

### Step 2: Identify Intent

Ask yourself and the buyer:
- Do they have genuine, current need for this product?
- Do they have budget/capital?
- Is this the right time for them?

**Wrong approach:** Emailing 500 random contacts hoping some will buy
**Right approach:** Identify 50 contacts with verified intent; email them specifically

### Step 3: Know Your Buyer's Role

Identify who you're emailing:
- Purchase Manager? (decision-maker for buying)

### Step 4: The Zoom/Video Call

After initial email contact:
- Follow up with a call to acknowledge the email
- Get them on a zoom/video meeting

### Step 5: Beyond the B2B Portal Trap

> *"When you post on a B2B portal, the same lead goes to 10 other exporters. You're in a race from Day 1. Your competition was created before you even spoke to the buyer."*

**Better:** Build inbound demand so buyers approach you specifically, not through a platform that dilutes you among competitors.

### The Cold vs. Warm Distinction

| Cold Approach | Warm Approach |
|---|---|
| Cold calling from port data | Content/network that generates inbound |
| Mass emailing | Targeted 50 emails to verified prospects |

## 26. Major Scams — Real Stories

### Scam 1: Fahad's ₹35 Lakh Copper Scrap Loss

**What happened:**
- Ordered 10 tonnes of copper scrap from China
- Paid advance (Chinese suppliers work on advance only)
- Documents showed 10,000 kg
- Actual arrival at Mundra Port: **900 kg total**
- Of that 900 kg: **150 kg was the wooden crate/box weight**
- Net actual copper received: ~750 kg out of 10,000 ordered

**How the fraud worked:**
- Documents (Bill of Lading, Invoice) were perfectly forged
- Weight listed on paper matched what should have been there
- By the time goods arrived (1 month transit), the Chinese company had vanished

**Why it happened:**
- Copper scrap is what China imports most — buying from them made no logical sense
- Too high a discount was offered (30–40% below market = fraud signal)
- No on-ground verification at loading point in China
- No experienced mentor to flag the red flags

**Lesson:**
> *"Everything can be forged if you don't know the inside secrets. A Bill of Lading can be faked. A Bill of Lading shown to you means nothing without independent verification."*

---

### Scam 2: ₹2 Crore Copper + Aluminium Scrap Loss (Pune Importers)

**The victims:** A team from Pune + investors' money

**What happened:**
- Ordered 2 containers:
  - 1 container copper scrap
  - 1 container aluminium wire scrap
- Paid ₹80 lakh–₹1 crore as advance
- Balance on Bill of Lading (BL)
- When goods arrived: **Weight significantly less than invoiced**
- They had already paid based on the BL
- **Total loss: ₹2 crore**

**How they found Fahad:**
- His phone number appeared in **port data** (old data from when he used to export in his own name)
- Scammers had used Fahad's old BL to show as proof of their legitimacy
- These victims called him wondering if he had dealt with that supplier

**Lesson:** Port data is public and can be misused by scammers to fake references.

---

### Scam 3: The Onion Credit Disaster (Madhya Pradesh Exporter)

**What happened:**
- Exporter sold onions to multiple countries
- Gave goods on **credit** (payment to come later)
- Bangladesh: ₹8 lakh credit
- UAE: ₹16 lakh credit
- Multiple other countries: various amounts
- **Total unpaid: ₹40 lakh**

**Why this happened:**
- Trusted buyers based on relationship, not documented financial safeguards
- No letter of credit, no advance, no escrow
- By the time he followed up, buyers gave excuses and delayed

**Lesson:** Never give goods on pure credit without:
- LC (Letter of Credit) from a bank
- 50%+ advance payment
- Escrow arrangement for balance

## 27. How to Protect Yourself from Fraud

### Protection Rule #1: Never Accept a BL as Proof

- Bill of Lading (BL) can be **forged very convincingly**
- A genuine-looking BL is not proof of genuine goods
- Always verify independently

### Protection Rule #2: For Imports — Be Present at Loading

> *"Every serious serial importer who takes goods from China goes there and is present at loading. Video call verification is not sufficient for bulk imports."*

You must physically verify:
- Actual weight at loading
- Quality matches sample
- Quantity is correct
- Container is sealed properly

### Protection Rule #3: Only Work with Verified Suppliers

- Get GST certificate, company registration, MSME certificate
- Check how long they've been in business
- Ask for references from other exporters
- Video call before committing
- Physical visit before large orders

### Protection Rule #4: Use LUT/Advance Payment + LC

- Never ship without payment security
- Preferred: **100% advance**
- Acceptable: **50% advance + 50% via LC from a genuine bank**
- Never: Pure credit without security

### Protection Rule #5: LC Verification

If receiving LC from a buyer:
- LC MUST come through your bank (bank-to-bank)
- LC received by courier or email = **FAKE**
- Take the LC to your bank and verify before shipping

### Protection Rule #6: Use Local Help in Foreign Countries

When doing business in a new country:
- Hire a **local contact/agent** who speaks the language
- Get them vetted first (references, track record)
- Pay them enough to be loyal to you
- BUT: Know they may have divided loyalties — manage carefully

> *"A localite can be on your side or their side. If you pay them $2,000/month and someone else offers $20,000 for one deal, where do you think they'll go? Have backup plans."*

### Protection Rule #7: Calculate Risk Appetite

> *"If your inventory is ₹10 lakh, take ₹1 lakh of risk — something you can absorb. Don't go all-in as a beginner."*

Experienced exporters **calculate risk**. Beginners go **all-in** and then lose everything.

## 29. Why 90% Fail — The 4 Root Causes

### Cause 1: Desperate Energy (Desperation)

When experienced buyers sense desperation in a seller, they immediately know they have full negotiating power. They will extract maximum discount or simply not engage.

> *"I can tear apart an exporter in 10 minutes. I know exactly where to push. Indian exporters have a weak mindset — one objection and they change their terms completely."*

**Fix:** Build confidence through preparation, product knowledge, and having alternative buyers.

### Cause 2: Begging Mindset Instead of Sales Mindset

Many beginners approach like:
- "Sir, please buy from me"
- "Sir, can you give me an order?"
- "Sir, do you want to buy something?"

**This never works.** No one has ever gotten anything by begging.

**Fix:** Make your pitch about the **buyer's benefit**, not your need for the order.

### Cause 3: Wrong Methods

Many "mentors" teach wrong methods:
- Buy port data lists and cold-call
- Spam embassy databases
- Mass email campaigns

These are:
- Often ethically questionable (port data = private data being sold illegally)
- Ineffective in practice
- Creating competition with identical sellers

> *"The day a law comes against selling port data, these vendors will go to jail. It's a loophole, not a right. But it doesn't work anyway."*

### Cause 4: No Application Discipline

Even with the right knowledge:
- Many apply for 10 days, then stop for 11 days
- Cold approaches are abandoned after first rejection
- Consistency is never maintained

> *"Standing in front of someone's house every day won't make them buy — eventually they'll chase you away. But random bursts of cold outreach are even worse — they accomplish nothing."*

## 32. Final Advice & Mindset for Beginners

### The Surgeon Analogy

> *"If you want to be a surgeon but don't know how to perform surgery — you are a murderer with a knife. The surgery happens safely because the doctor is trained by experienced mentors. The same applies to export."*

**Meaning:** Don't enter any field without proper training. Overconfidence without knowledge leads to massive losses.

### The Three Paths (Choose One)

| Path | Cost | Learning |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct market** | ₹15–30 lakh+ (learning tax) | Trial and error; painful |
| **Mentor-guided** | ₹50,000–₹2 lakh | Structured, faster, safer |
| **Employment first** | Free (paid to learn) | Slow but comprehensive |

> *"Either invest a few lakhs in yourself and a good mentor, or lose 15–30 lakh in the market. The market teaches through your losses."*

### Don't Blame Your Ancestors

> *"We hear: 'If my grandfather hadn't sold that land, it would be worth crores today.' But you have the chance to CREATE that asset now. Stop blaming the past. Buy a shop, start a business, build something. Nobody is stopping you."*

### The Final 5 Rules

1. **Take calculated risk** — not all-in risk, not zero risk
2. **Invest in yourself** — ₹2 lakh invested in skills → ₹20 lakh return next year
3. **Build a team** — you cannot do everything alone (sales, operations, accounts, logistics, procurement)
4. **Upgrade your network** — stop spending all your time with people at your level; spend time with people one level above
5. **Start now** — not after conditions are perfect (they never will be)

### The Struggler Warning

> *"Stop sitting with strugglers all day. You become comfortable thinking struggle is normal. You need people one level above you. From a struggler, you can only learn how to struggle better."*
