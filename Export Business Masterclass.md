## 3. The Biggest Scam in the Export Industry

> ⚠️ **Sagar's words:** "Quote this line and remember it. Record it. Across the entire world — not just India — **no institute, no consultant, no broker, no company has ever been born, nor will ever be born, that will find you a buyer, get you your first export, ship your first container, sell your goods, and deposit advance payment into your account.** This does not exist."

### The Expectation vs. Reality Gap

| What People Expect | What Actually Happens |
|--------------------|----------------------|
| "Learn from the institute → get a buyer" | Nobody shares their buyers with anyone |
| "Pay for a program → they'll arrange a deal" | Program fees get collected; buyer never comes |
| "First shipment guaranteed" | Legal cover for the institute; no actual buyer delivered |
| "Advance payment will come to my account" | Never happens through a third party |

### The Psychology Behind the Scam
- Human nature includes **greed** (lobh) — the desire to earn more, to grow
- Scammers and fake institutes **exploit this greed** as their business model
- The more desperate or new you are, the more vulnerable you are to promises like:
  - "Rice buyers are ready"
  - "Onion buyers are standing by"
  - "DM us to get a buyer immediately"

> "When they say 'DM us', they're actually starting to sell you their program — not the buyer."

### Why Nobody Shares Their Buyers
Ask yourself honestly: **If you worked hard for a year and finally found a genuine, paying, honest buyer — would you share that buyer with a stranger?**

The answer is obvious. So why would anyone else?

### The Legal Cover Trick
Fake institutes use language like:
- "First Shipment Guarantee"
- "First Export Guarantee"
- "We'll help you complete your first shipment"

This language is **legally clever** — they can technically get you to send one container to a low-value open market (like Dubai) and say "we did it." But your pockets will be empty.

## 4. The Dubai / Sri Lanka / Vietnam Trap

### Why These Markets Are Pushed So Hard
The most promoted "beginner export markets" in India are:
1. **UAE (Dubai)**
2. **Sri Lanka**
3. **Malaysia**
4. **Vietnam** (newer addition)

These markets are promoted because they are **easy to access**, not because they are profitable:

| Feature | Why It Attracts Beginners |
|---------|--------------------------|
| Flight distance | 3–4 hours from India |
| Visa | On-arrival / very easy |
| Cost of visit | Very affordable |
| Indian community | Large Indian diaspora already there |
| Indian companies in those markets | Many Indians have set up shops there |

### The Open Market Reality

These markets operate as **open commission markets**:
- Dubai's main market: **Al-Aweer Market**
- Sri Lanka's main markets: **Dambulla** and **Colombo**
- You can bring any produce → hand it to a commission agent → they sell at whatever price the market decides

**Critical truth:** The price in these markets is often **lower than India's domestic wholesale price** once all costs are added.

### How the "First Shipment" Scam Works

```
Step 1: You pay an institute ₹X for "first export guarantee" training
Step 2: They send your goods to their OWN setup in Dubai/Sri Lanka
Step 3: Their people earn commission on your shipment
Step 4: They take cuts in logistics too
Step 5: On import side, they manipulate costs
Step 6: Market price at destination is flexible (no fixed rate)
Step 7: You see a "shipment done" but your wallet is lighter, not heavier
```

### The Margin Illusion (The Classic Social Media Trick)
You see a reel: "Onion in India = ₹20. Onion in Dubai = ₹150. 7.5x profit!"

**What they don't show you:**
- Freight cost alone for one container: ~₹9 Lakhs+
- Packaging, FSSAI, quality testing costs
- Import duty at destination
- Commission agent charges at destination
- Wholesaler margin at destination
- Retailer margin at destination
- **What remains for you: 5–10% if you're lucky**

### The Painful Truth
> "If you take 100 people who did this Dubai/Sri Lanka export route and ask them honestly — hand on heart — 99% of them made a loss. But nobody talks about it. Because when your hard-earned money disappears, you don't have the mental strength to speak about it."

### Countries Are Fine — The People Are the Problem
> "I have no issue with Dubai, Sri Lanka, Malaysia, or Vietnam as countries. They're all good. But our own people have set up their companies there and are running scams on our own Indian exporters. That's not okay."

## 5. Personality Traits of a Successful Exporter

Sagar's daily lifestyle and the mindset he recommends:

### Non-Negotiable Traits

#### 1. 🧘 Patience (Sabr)
> "Patience was the thing I lacked the most early on. But time taught me this lesson."

Why patience is critical in export:
- Finding a genuine buyer takes months
- Shipment delays happen (congestion, global tensions)
- Payment cycles are long
- Market conditions fluctuate
- Mistakes happen — learning takes time

**Without patience, every setback feels fatal.**

#### 2. 🔁 Consistency (Nirantarta)
> "Consistency means not giving up. Today's generation quits at the first failure."

What consistency looks like in practice:
- Sending follow-up emails even when no one replies
- Returning to the same market after a shipment loss
- Showing up for shipments yourself even in harsh conditions (rain, heat, no food)
- Never abandoning your learning process mid-way

**The biggest differentiator between successful and failed exporters is whether they stayed consistent past the first 3 failures.**

#### 3. 💯 Honesty (Imandari)
> "When you work with honesty, nature also gives you something no money can buy — peace of mind."

In export, honesty means:
- Sending exactly what you promised (grade, weight, quality)
- Not over-communicating or over-promising to buyers
- Pricing fairly without hidden costs
- Not misclassifying products for duty benefits

**Sagar's personal rule:** "I never over-deliver and I never under-deliver. I deliver exactly what I commit."

#### 4. 📦 Customer Experience Obsession
- Sagar attends **every shipment personally** — sweating through loading operations, no food, no water, under the sun
- Reason: The experience he gives his buyer today cannot be replicated by a competitor tomorrow
- **His first-ever buyer from Europe is STILL buying from him in 2026 — 8+ years later**
- This is what "customer retention" looks like in real-life export

## 6. English — Is It Mandatory?

### The Short Answer
**No. Basic communication is enough.**

### The Longer Explanation

| Country | English Reality |
|---------|----------------|
| USA | US accent is so different, even Indians struggle to understand |
| Arab countries | Speak English in their own distinct accent |
| China | Most Chinese traders have very limited English |
| Vietnam | Sagar visited recently — "they know even less English than we do" |
| India | Regional accent, not Oxford English — and deals still happen |

> "The world's business English is: I say something, you understand. You say something, I understand. If this minimum communication works, business happens."

### What "Business English" Actually Means
- **NOT required:** Fluent grammar, perfect pronunciation, IELTS-level writing
- **Required:** The ability to:
  - Write a clear product offer email
  - Understand a buyer's requirements
  - Negotiate quantity, price, payment terms
  - Follow up professionally
  - Handle complaints in writing

### The Mindset Shift
> "The excuse 'my English is weak' is just that — an excuse to not do the work."

If a Vietnamese manufacturer with zero English can export millions of dollars in goods, a middle-class Indian with basic English has no valid excuse.

**Practical tip:** Use Google Translate, WhatsApp voice messages with translation, Google Docs for email drafts, and AI tools to polish your written communication.

- Family time (consciously protected)
- Reflection on the day

## 10. Global Tensions as Challenge & Opportunity

### Context
Sagar was asked about the Iran situation, Strait of Hormuz tensions, and how geopolitical instability affects exporters.

### His Honest Assessment
> "Right now, yes, it's the biggest challenge. My own shipments are stuck."

The practical problems:
- **Ship congestion:** Vessels taking alternate, longer routes
- **Port backlogs:** Vessels not getting berthing slots on time
- **Reefer (refrigerated) containers:** No plug-in power sockets available at ports
- **Transit times extended:** Perishable goods at higher risk
- **Shipment count reduced:** From 3–4 per month to ~2 per month

### But Here's the Opportunity

**The Stock Market Analogy:**
> "When do smart investors put money into the stock market? When the market is UP... or when it has CRASHED DOWN? When it's down — that's when you earn."

**Apply this to export:**
- While the market is slow, **start building your foundation**:
  - Register your company
  - Get your IEC, GST, RCMC
  - Research your product and country
  - Find suppliers
  - Understand logistics
  - Start your marketing/social media presence

- **By the time the market recovers** (could be 12 months), you'll be ready to ship immediately while others are still figuring out basics

> "You start now. Build now. And by the time Trump solves the Iran problem, you'll be ready to export. Don't worry about geopolitics — worry about your readiness."

### The Slow Market Is NOT a Dead Market
- Business slows, but never stops
- People still need medicines, food, clothing, raw materials
- **Trade is as old as civilization** — it has survived every war, crisis, and pandemic

## 12. How to Find Buyers — The Real Framework

> ⚠️ **Golden Rule:** "Never go looking for a buyer FIRST. If a buyer comes while you're still learning how to export, the buyer will run away before you're ready. Learn export first. Buyer comes second."

### The 3-Before-Buyer Rule
Before finding any buyer, you must first have:
1. ✅ **A registered company** with IEC, GST, RCMC, AD Code
2. ✅ **A specific product** (selected scientifically, not randomly)
3. ✅ **Complete logistics knowledge** (from purchase → warehouse → port → buyer's warehouse)

Only then does actively seeking buyers make sense.

### The 15–16 Buyer Finding Methods (Overview)

Sagar mentions there are 15–16 distinct methods. He covers several in the podcast:

| # | Method | Description |
|---|--------|-------------|
| 1 | **Indian Embassy / High Commission** | Provides buyer databases and leads for Indian exporters in target countries |
| 2 | **Chamber of Commerce — Business Delegations** | Join trade delegations to target countries; meet buyers in person at organized events |
| 3 | **Trade Exhibitions & Fairs** | Attend/exhibit at international food, textile, agri fairs (Anuga, SIAL, Gulfood, etc.) |
| 4 | **Direct Market Visits** | Travel to the target country, physically visit wholesale markets, meet buyers face-to-face |
| 5 | **Third Party Trade Agents** | Use local agents in the target country to represent you |
| 6 | **B2B Portals** | Alibaba, IndiaMART, TradeIndia, Global Trade Atlas — generate leads (not guaranteed buyers) |
| 7 | **Digital Marketing / Social Media** | YouTube, Instagram, LinkedIn — inbound buyer inquiries over time |
| 8 | **AI-assisted Lead Generation** | Specific software tools that extract buyer contact data from trade databases |
| 9 | **APEDA / FIEO** | Government export promotion bodies provide market intelligence and buyer contacts |
| 10 | **Referrals from existing buyers** | When you deliver quality, buyers refer you to other buyers in their network |
| + 5 more | (not fully listed in this episode) | Part of the full curriculum |

### The Realistic Timeline & Expectation
| Year | Realistic Outcome |
|------|------------------|
| Year 1 | 1–2 genuine buyers found after sustained effort |
| Year 2 | 1–2 more added; year-1 buyers become repeat customers |
| Year 3+ | Chain effect begins — buyers refer to other buyers |

> "1–2 buyers in a year sounds disappointing. But this is a wholesale business. 1 buyer doing 2 containers a month = 24 containers a year. That's a serious business."

### The Volume Game
| Business Type | Customer Count | Volume | Revenue |
|--------------|---------------|--------|---------|
| Retail | High | Low | Medium |
| Wholesale | Low | High | High |
| Export | Very Low | Very High | Very High |

**Export is the most extreme version of the wholesale model.**

### Horizon Exim's Own Growth Story

## 13. B2B Portal Subscriptions — Worth It?

### The Question
B2B portals charge ₹1–2 Lakh/year, promising to "give you buyers." Is this worth paying?

### Sagar's Honest Answer

**First, the fundamental truth:**
> "No platform, however much it claims, will give you a buyer. A buyer means someone who knows you, trusts you, and will send ₹15–20 Lakhs to your account. How can a stranger, referred by a platform, send that to you?"

**What B2B portals CAN do:**
- Provide **leads** — company names, email IDs, contact details of potential buyers
- These leads need to be:
  - Contacted
  - Followed up
  - Nurtured
  - Converted through relationship building

**What B2B portals CANNOT guarantee:**
- That the lead is genuine
- That they'll place an order
- That they won't be a scammer
- That the inquiry will convert to business

### The Lead Quality Reality
- Out of **50–60 inquiries** from a B2B portal subscription
- Only **1–2 are genuinely useful**
- The rest are: low-quality, time-wasting, or outright fake

### Should You Subscribe?
| Scenario | Verdict |
|----------|---------|
| You have zero other strategy | It's one of 15-16 methods — use it alongside others |
| You rely on it exclusively | No. This alone will not build your business |
| You expect a "buyer" not a "lead" | Wrong expectation. Adjust accordingly |
| Budget is tight | Prioritize direct market visits over portal subscriptions |

### The Bottom Line
> "A B2B platform can make your journey slightly easier. But it cannot guarantee success. The onus of converting leads to buyers is always on YOU."

## 14. Realistic Profit Margins in Export

### The Social Media Myth vs. Reality

**What you see on Instagram:**
- "India: ₹20/kg onion → Dubai: ₹150/kg onion → 7.5x profit!"
- "₹50 product in India → $5 on Amazon USA → 10x profit!"

**What they hide:**
- Freight cost alone: ~₹9 Lakh for one container
- Packaging, labeling, FSSAI costs
- CHA (customs agent) charges
- Port handling fees
- Marine insurance
- Import duty at destination (USA now has additional 20–25% tariff)
- Destination CHA / customs clearance fees
- Wholesaler margin at destination
- Retailer margin at destination

### The Honest Margin Formula

| Product Type | Realistic Net Margin |
|--------------|---------------------|
| Agricultural commodities (perishable) | 5–10% |
| Processed food products | 8–15% |
| Textiles / garments | 10–20% |
| Manufactured goods | 8–15% |
| Monopoly / unique products (only your factory makes it) | 25–40% possible |
| **Average across most products** | **5–20%** |

> "After 9 years of exporting, I have NEVER achieved 100% profit margin on any shipment. Not even close. This is a wholesale volume game, not a retail markup game."

### Government Incentives That Boost Net Returns
These are REAL benefits that increase your effective margins:

| Scheme | What It Is | Benefit |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **RoDTEP** | Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products | Refund of embedded taxes in product cost |
| **DBK (Duty Drawback)** | Customs duty refund on exported goods | Cash refund post-shipment |
| **GST Refund** | Full GST paid on export product is refunded | Direct cash back from government |

> "These benefits, when added to your base margin, can increase your effective return significantly. Factor them in."

### The Volume Math (Why It Still Works)
| Scenario | Numbers |
|----------|---------|
| Shipment value | ₹10 Lakhs |
| Net margin | 10% |
| Profit per shipment | ₹1 Lakh |
| 5 shipments/month | ₹5 Lakh/month profit |
| Investment required | ₹50 Lakh working capital |

This is not a "get rich quick" model — it's a **sustainable, scalable, compounding business.**

## 15. Sagar's Biggest Mistakes — Lessons from Experience

### Mistake #1: Logistics Ignorance (Most Costly)

**What happened:**
- Found a buyer ✅
- Found the product ✅
- Was ready to ship ✅
- **The container didn't arrive on time** ❌

**Consequence:**
- Perishable cargo (agricultural product) was waiting
- Cargo sat without refrigeration
- Goods **rotted and spoiled**
- **First shipment = massive financial loss**
- Reason: No knowledge of how container booking, port logistics, and timing work

**The lesson:**
> "I thought: buyer done, product done, now just put it in a container and send. That's NOT how this works."

### Logistics Knowledge You MUST Have Before First Shipment

| Term | What It Means |
|------|--------------|
| **ETA** | Estimated Time of Arrival — when the ship arrives at destination port |
| **ETD** | Estimated Time of Departure — when the ship leaves origin port |
| **Port Cut-off** | Last date/time to submit shipping documents for that vessel |
| **Gate Cut-off** | Last date/time to physically deliver your cargo to the port/CFS |
| **Gate Opening** | When the port starts accepting cargo for that vessel |
| **Detention** | Charges for keeping the container outside port beyond the free time |
| **Demurrage** | Charges for keeping the container inside the port terminal beyond free days |

> "Detention and demurrage charges are the 'hidden costs' that new exporters ALWAYS forget to calculate. They can eat your entire profit margin."

### Mistake #2: Product Selection Without Research
- Spent 7–8 months chasing dry fruits for export
- India **imports** dry fruits — it doesn't export them
- Never validated using DGFT/HS code data first
- **Months of wasted effort and energy**

### Mistake #3: Learning Sequence Was Wrong
- Should have been: Learn export process → Select product → Find buyers
- Was actually: Find buyers → Panic → Try to learn while on the run

## 16. Three Core Skills for Young Exporters

If a young exporter or student could only develop three skills, Sagar says:

### 1. 💯 Honesty (Imandari)
- Honesty with your buyer (product quality, grade, weight)
- Honesty with your supplier (payment terms, volume commitments)
- Honesty with yourself (don't over-promise, don't fake capabilities)
- **This is the foundation** — without it, every other skill is a liability

### 2. 🔁 Consistency (Dhaaravahikta)
- Show up every day, regardless of results
- One email not replied → send another. Second not replied → follow up again
- Don't switch products/countries every 3 months
- Build expertise in ONE product, ONE market before expanding
- **Long-term relationships in export are built by people who didn't give up**

### 3. 🧘 Patience (Sabr)
- Your first buyer might take 6–12 months to find
- Your first shipment might take another 3 months to execute
- Global tensions will delay your containers
- Buyers will ghost you
- **The ones who wait through the hard phase are the ones who eventually scale**

> "These three things — honesty, consistency, patience — apply to every business on earth. Not just export. But in export, where cycles are long and stakes are high, they matter more than anywhere else."

## 17. Three Biggest Life Lessons

Sagar's three lessons from 9 years of real export experience:

### Lesson 1: Never Go Looking for a Buyer First

> "जीवन में पहले कभी बायर खुझने मत जाओ।"
> ("In life, never go looking for a buyer first.")

**Why:** If a buyer lands while you're still unprepared:
- You don't know how to price your goods
- You don't know the logistics
- You don't know what documents they need
- You don't know payment terms
- **The buyer leaves before you figure it out**

**Correct sequence:**
1. Open your company
2. Select the right product (scientifically, not randomly)
3. Learn the full export process (logistics, customs, documents)
4. Only then go find a buyer

### Lesson 2: Select the Right Product — Not Just Any Product

> "Whatever is lying around is not an export product. First check whether India actually exports it."

**Product selection checklist:**
- [ ] Does India export this product? (check DGFT / HS code export data)
- [ ] Is there growing demand in target country?
- [ ] Can you source it reliably near your location?
- [ ] Does your budget support the logistics?
- [ ] Is competition in target market manageable?

### Lesson 3: Learn the Complete Process First

**"Complete process" means:**
- How to buy goods from supplier with right quality standards
- How to arrange CHA for customs clearance
- How to book containers (FCL vs. LCL)
- How to coordinate with freight forwarder
- How to prepare export documentation (packing list, invoice, BL, COO, etc.)
- How to handle buyer communication from inquiry to payment receipt
- How to use government schemes (RoDTEP, DBK, GST refund)

> "When a buyer asks you any question, you must have the answer. Including the price — you need to know how to calculate a landed cost. If you don't know the cost, you can't price it."

## 19. Key Takeaways & Action Items

### ✅ The Correct Starting Sequence
```
1. Register company (Proprietorship → IEC → GST → RCMC → AD Code)
2. Use DGFT data + HSN codes to select the RIGHT product
3. Identify target country (start with developing markets — Africa, SE Asia)
4. Learn the FULL logistics process (ETA, ETD, CHA, port cut-off, etc.)
5. Calculate ALL costs (freight, customs, packaging, duties, agent fees, detention)
6. THEN go find buyers using 15–16 methods
7. Build social media presence alongside — it's a long-term buyer magnet
```

### ❌ Do NOT Do These
1. Go looking for buyers before knowing how to export
2. Trust anyone who promises to "get you a buyer"
3. Fall for the Dubai/Sri Lanka "first shipment guarantee" scam
4. Choose a product randomly without checking if India exports it
5. Expect 100% or even 50% profit margins — 5–20% is the reality
6. Rely solely on B2B portals for buyers
7. Ignore logistics until after you've found a buyer
8. Let English language anxiety stop you from starting
9. Give up after 1st or 2nd failure — the game really starts at failure #3+
10. Start with developed markets (USA/EU) before gaining experience

## 📚 Glossary of Key Terms (Episode-Specific)

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **IEC** | Import Export Code — mandatory 10-digit DGFT-issued identity for trading |
| **GST** | Goods and Services Tax — Indian indirect tax registration |
| **RCMC** | Registration Cum Membership Certificate — issued by export promotion councils (APEDA, FIEO, etc.) |
| **AD Code** | Authorized Dealer Code — bank code registered with customs for receiving foreign payments |
| **CHA** | Custom House Agent — licensed intermediary for customs operations at ports |
| **B2B Portal** | Business-to-Business platform (Alibaba, IndiaMART, TradeIndia) listing buyers and sellers |
| **Lead** | Contact information of a potential buyer — NOT a confirmed buyer |
| **FCL** | Full Container Load — you book and fill the entire container |
| **LCL** | Less than Container Load — shared container with other shippers |
| **Reefer Container** | Refrigerated container for temperature-sensitive cargo |
| **ETA** | Estimated Time of Arrival |
| **ETD** | Estimated Time of Departure |
| **Detention** | Charges for keeping container outside port beyond free period |
| **Demurrage** | Charges for keeping container inside port terminal beyond free days |
| **RoDTEP** | Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products — govt. export incentive |
| **DBK** | Duty Drawback — government refund of customs duties on exported goods |
| **DGFT** | Directorate General of Foreign Trade — apex trade policy body |
| **COO** | Certificate of Origin — document certifying where a product was manufactured |
| **Open Market** | Unregulated commission-based market (e.g., Dubai Al-Aweer, Sri Lanka Dambulla) |
| **Commission Agent** | Middleman in an open market who sells goods on your behalf for a percentage cut |
| **Proprietorship** | Simplest business structure — single owner, uses personal PAN/Aadhaar |
| **APEDA** | Agricultural & Processed Food Products Export Development Authority |
| **FIEO** | Federation of Indian Export Organisations |
