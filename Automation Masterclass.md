## 1. The Core Warning: AI Can Replace — But It Won't Replace Taste

The central thesis of this conversation is not fear. It is awareness.

Companies do not work for employees. They work for shareholders. They will do whatever it takes to maximize shareholder returns — including replacing humans with AI wherever it is cheaper, faster, and more reliable.

This is not a debate about whether AI *can* do jobs. It can. The question is: what part of your work cannot be replicated?

The answer, consistently, is: **taste.**

> *"The part that is not replaceable — for a CEO, for you, for me — is taste. Everybody has their own taste."*

Taste is:
- Knowing what to do and what not to do
- Original thinking, not step-following
- Creating something new rather than executing a known process
- Having a distinct perspective that emerges from personal experience

**People who work like tools** — step one, step two, step three, step four, done — those are the jobs at highest risk.

**People who think** — who bring original judgment, who solve problems in new ways, who have opinions — those people will thrive. In fact, because the operational layer is now handled by AI, thinkers can now multiply their impact dramatically.

## 2. Tasks vs. Purpose: The Most Important Distinction

One of the most clarifying frameworks in this conversation:

> *"Task is sending an email. Purpose is why the hell are you sending that email."*

| Element | Definition | AI Replaceability |
|---------|-----------|------------------|
| **Task** | The mechanical action — writing the email, filing the report, building the spreadsheet | High to complete |
| **Purpose** | *Why* the action is being taken, what outcome it's driving toward, what decision it's informing | Not replaceable |

Most people in most offices are doing **tasks in the name of a job.** That is the fundamental problem. They are executing steps without owning the "why."

When you look around your office, look for people who:
- Follow a defined process step by step
- Do not think about *why* the process exists
- Would describe their job as "doing X" rather than "achieving Y"

Those are the people whose roles are at highest risk.

The people who are safe — and will thrive — are those who:
- Delegate the "what" to AI
- Own the "why"
- Make decisions about purpose and direction

## 3. Why Smart People Are Getting Dumber With AI

A critical observation from running a 400-person company with AI deeply integrated:

Team members who were sharp independent thinkers became weaker over time after using AI heavily.

**The pattern:**
- Before AI: Team members consumed hundreds of pieces of content, formed their own opinions, brought their best 10 ideas to the table, and made the final judgment call on what to present
- After AI: Team members dump everything into AI and let it choose what to present. They outsource the judgment layer.

**The result:** Generic, predictable, mediocre output. "AI slop."

The specific failure mode in the content context:
- Old process: watch 100 things → pick 10 best → bring to decision-maker
- New (broken) process: generate 100 scripts → dump into ChatGPT → "you choose the best 2" → present AI's choice

The judgment layer — the act of choosing — is where expertise is built. Outsourcing that layer atrophies the muscle.

**The fix:** Use AI for the operational work. Keep the judgment layer human. The moment you let AI also make the decision, you get average output at best.

## 4. Observed Exposure: The Real Data on Job Risk

Anthropic coined the term **Observed Exposure** in their Income Index Report.

**What it measures:** For a given job category, what percentage of the tasks involved could theoretically be automated by AI — and how much is actually being automated today?

The gap between:
- **Potential AI exposure** (what could be automated)
- **Observed AI exposure** (what is being automated right now)

...is the gap between where we are and where we're heading.

The key insight: **The potential is already enormous. The observed coverage is still low. When they converge, massive disruption will happen.**

This is not speculation. It is directional data about the trajectory.

## 5. White Collar vs. Blue Collar: Who Is Really at Risk?

Based on US research data:

| Job Type | Percentage of US Workforce | AI Exposure (Potential) |
|----------|---------------------------|------------------------|
| **White Collar** (computer-based work) | 70% | ~80% of jobs could be end-to-end automated |
| **Blue Collar** (carpenter, plumber, etc.) | 30% | Nearly zero |

**The counterintuitive finding:** The higher the degree, the higher the job risk.

> *"The more degrees you have, the higher your jobs are on risk."*

Why? Because white-collar work — software engineering, finance, management consulting, marketing — is precisely the work that involves processing information, writing, analyzing, and making decisions from data. All of which AI can now do.

Blue-collar work involves physical manipulation of the real world. A plumber, an electrician, a carpenter — these require physical dexterity, real-world problem-solving, and presence that AI cannot yet replicate.

**The irony:** The people who spent the most money on education, took the safest-seeming career paths, and optimized for "good jobs" are the most exposed.

## 8. What Companies Are Actually Doing

Two landmark examples of what is actually happening:

### Example 1: Block (Jack Dorsey's Company)

After leaving Twitter, Jack Dorsey built Block (formerly Square) into a major fintech company.

In a now-famous tweet with 64 million views, he announced:
- Business was growing faster than ever
- Gross profit was continuing to grow
- They were serving more customers profitably

**AND THEN:** He laid off 4,000 of 10,000 employees.

The reasoning (quoted directly):

> *"We are making this tough decision not because we are in trouble — our business is strong, gross profit continues to grow, we continue to serve more and more customers profitably. But something has changed. We are seeing that the intelligence tools we are creating and using, paired with smaller and flatter teams, are enabling a new way of working which fundamentally changes what it means to build and run a company."*

**Translation:** Business is great. But 4,000 of our employees were doing tasks, not thinking. Now AI does the tasks. We don't need them.

The 6,000 who stayed were those who were using AI to multiply their output — not the ones doing things step by step.

### Example 2: Oracle & Meta

- **Meta:** Laid off ~8,000–10% of workforce, stock rose 10%+. Took the salary savings and invested them directly into AI infrastructure (GPUs, data centers).
- **Oracle:** Laid off ~30,000 globally (including significant India layoffs), approximately 20% of global workforce. Best quarters and highest profitability ever.

**The cycle:**
1. Lay off humans → save billions in salary
2. Invest savings in AI infrastructure → buy more GPUs
3. More computing → less need for humans
4. Less need for humans → lay off more humans
5. Repeat

> *"It's a bloody vicious cycle — cycle over cycle over cycle."*

**The market reward:** Every time a company announces layoffs alongside strong business results, the stock goes up. The market is rewarding efficiency, not employment.

## 10. The AGI-ASI Trajectory

**Current state:** AI (Artificial Intelligence) — tools that can perform specific tasks with superhuman performance in narrow domains.

**Next stage:** AGI (Artificial General Intelligence) — an AI that is smarter than every single person in the world, combined, on every dimension, and can do anything and everything better than any human.

**After AGI:** ASI (Artificial Super Intelligence) — what happens once AI can develop and improve itself without human AI researchers.

**The Singularity:** The moment AI can self-improve, the entire AI wave — currently driven by maybe 1,000–1,500 AI researchers globally — transitions to being driven by AI itself. From that point:
- Unlimited "AI engineers" working 24/7
- Self-evolving, self-improving systems
- Exponential acceleration that no human institution can keep up with

**Why every lab is racing:**
The company that reaches AGI first will reach ASI first. At that point, they will have such an unfair advantage that competition becomes impossible. Everyone else is riding a bicycle while they drive a Ferrari.

**The two camps:**
- Sam Altman: believes rapid acceleration will happen
- Dario Amodei (Anthropic): agrees on rapid acceleration post-AGI, hence the safety focus

The race to AGI is not about commercial advantage in the ordinary sense — it's about who controls the singularity.

## 11. The Energy Problem & Universal Basic Income

**The two scenarios for what happens when jobs disappear:**

### Scenario 1: Universal Basic Income (UBI)

When mass unemployment happens from AI:
1. Governments will heavily tax AI companies (65–80%)
2. Use those taxes to give everyone a "pocket money" — Universal Basic Income
3. People have income without jobs

**Problem for India:** The AI companies are mostly in the US and China. India doesn't have its own AI company to tax. How does India's UBI work?

**India's smart play:** Force AI companies to set up data centers in India (through GST on digital transactions creating an effective tariff). When companies invest infrastructure here, India captures economic value through the data center economy.

Visakhapatnam is becoming India's AI capital — Google ($1 billion+), Meta, Oracle all building data centers there, in part due to Chandrababu Naidu's proactive tech policy (same approach that made Hyderabad India's IT capital).

### Scenario 2: Universal High Income (if energy becomes free)

The bigger moonshot theory:

The reason everything costs money is ultimately energy. All manufacturing, computing, logistics — the fundamental cost is energy (electricity).

**If energy cost approaches zero:**
- Robots do most physical work
- AI does most cognitive work
- The cost of almost everything drops to near-zero
- This is called **deflation** (the opposite of inflation)

**How energy could become near-free:**

1. **Nuclear fusion** (Sam Altman's major investment bet) — clean, almost unlimited energy
2. **Dyson Sphere** — theoretical: surround the sun with solar panels, capture its full energy output, transmit wirelessly to Earth/space data centers
3. **Data centers in space** — Jeff Bezos, Google, Elon Musk all discussing orbiting data centers powered by space-based solar

This is theoretical but directional — it explains where the biggest technology bets are being placed.

**The problem with "everything is free":** If nobody has jobs, who has money to buy things? This remains the unsolved question.

## 12. How to Find Jobs With AI: Step-by-Step

A complete live demonstration of AI-assisted job hunting.

### Step 1: Get Career Counseling From Claude With Indeed Connected

1. Go to Claude (claude.ai)
2. Connect the **Indeed** MCP connector
3. Upload your resume into the chat
4. Write a detailed prompt:

```
You are an expert career counselor. 
I have been learning AI deeply for the last year — I'm quite proficient.
I want to switch into an AI-first job that is resilient to AI changes.
I don't know coding but I know vibe coding, AI agents, and AI workflows well.
I am an AI generalist.
I can speak English.

Look at my resume and find me:
- AI-first roles
- Remote work preferred
- Minimum $100,000/year salary
- Jobs where software engineering is NOT required
- Companies that are decently funded (so they don't go bankrupt)

Search Indeed and give me 10 jobs I can apply for, ranked by fit.
```

**What Claude does:** Searches Indeed in parallel with multiple keywords simultaneously (something a human cannot do), matches against your resume, and returns 10 ranked job opportunities.

### Step 2: Personalize Resumes for Each Role (ATS Optimization)

**The ATS problem:** Most large companies use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) — AI that filters resumes before any human sees them. When 10,000 people apply, ATS eliminates 99% automatically.

**The most common mistake:** Sending the same resume to every job. This tanks your ATS match rate.

**The correct approach:**

```
Okay, I have 5 companies I want to apply to.
Pick the 5 roles you think I'm best suited for.
Go through the job description of each role.
You have my resume.
Act as an expert career advisor.
Rewrite my resume for each role to maximize ATS pass-through.
Use the right keywords. Use the right framing.
Do NOT make up anything not on my resume.
Generate:
- 5 optimized resumes (one per role)
- 5 cover letters (customized per role)
- 5 cold emails to the HR of each role

Output everything as PDFs.
```

**Result:** Five tailored application packages ready to send.

### Step 3: LinkedIn Job Search (Via Comet Browser)

In parallel, use **Comet browser** (AI-native browser with memory of all your browsing history):

```
Go to LinkedIn. 
Look up jobs that are remote.
Fit my resume.
I want AI-first roles, minimum $100K salary.
Research all of this and give me 10 jobs from LinkedIn.
```

Comet takes over the screen, logs into LinkedIn, runs multiple keyword searches simultaneously, and returns matching jobs — all autonomously.

### Step 4: Auto-Apply With Codex

Once you have 5 job links and 5 sets of application materials:

1. Open Codex
2. Give it access to a folder containing your resumes and cover letters
3. Activate **Computer Use** plugin (gives Codex access to all apps on your computer)
4. Tell it:

```
I have given you access to my resumes and cover letters.
The links to the 5 roles are below.
Go to each website, find the job, use the resume and cover letter, 
fill in all details you need.
Apply for all these jobs for me.
Don't stop until everything is done.
Don't ask me questions — figure out what you need from my documents.
```

**What happens:** Codex opens its own browser, navigates to Indeed.com, fills in your details from your resume automatically — including pin codes, contact information, everything. It applies while you do something else.

- Self-learning: gets better every time you interact with it
- Has pre-built skills for many common tasks
- Very reliable compared to older agent frameworks (like Open Claw, which frequently crashed)

**Step 1: Get hosting (cloud computer)**

1. Go to Hostinger
2. Buy a VPS (Virtual Private Server) — essentially renting a computer on the cloud
3. Recommended: KVM 2 plan (enough processing power)
4. Cost: ~₹800/month (use code "figuring out AI" for 20% off)

2. Click "Compose" → "One-click deploy" → Search for "Hermes"

Done. Hermes is running on a cloud computer that never turns off.

**Step 3: Connect an AI Model**

Hermes needs an AI brain to think with:

1. Use **Open Router** (openrouter.ai) — a unified interface to 370+ AI models
2. Create account → Get API key → Copy it

**Cost-saving hack:** For long, repetitive tasks that don't require top-tier intelligence, run them on **Ollama** — local AI models on your own computer (Mac Studio recommended). Zero cost, runs overnight.

## 18. How to Develop Taste in the Age of AI

If taste is what saves you, how do you develop it?

### The Experimentation Framework

> *"The simplest framework according to me that has worked for me is experimentation. There is no yes or no. Either it works or it doesn't. There is no good idea, bad idea. Either the idea works or it doesn't."*

As you experiment, you build the muscle of taste. You develop a feel for what works. You accumulate the data of experience.

### The Constraint Catalyst

> *"The first step to becoming an experimenter is constraint. Those who have no constraints don't develop taste."*

Constraint can be:
- Time pressure (deadline tomorrow)
- Money pressure (rent due next week)
- Job loss (what do I do now?)
- Resource limitation (can't afford a team)

Without pressure, the brain doesn't push into experimentation mode. Comfort breeds complacency. Constraints force creativity.

### The Exposure Path

The other path to taste: **get exposed to more things.**

> *"One way I believe is by getting yourself exposed to more things. Automatically you build an inclination. You build your own taste. You find your calling."*

- Read widely outside your domain
- Try things that seem unrelated
- Notice what you're drawn to
- Follow the curiosity

Steve Jobs taking a calligraphy class — not obviously related to computers — shaped the design philosophy of Apple products. You cannot always trace how exposure becomes taste, but it does.

### The Purpose and Hunger Component

Taste also requires purpose and hunger. People who are doing tasks without caring about why are not building taste — they're just executing. Those with ambition — who can see a better version of their life and are hungry for it — are the ones who experiment, who build taste, who thrive.

## 19. The New Opportunity: AI Is the Reset Button

The most optimistic framing of everything discussed:

> *"AI doesn't matter what you have done in the past. It doesn't matter how well it has worked out for you. It doesn't matter which college you went to. It doesn't matter if you won or lost. Everybody is on the same starting line. It's a reset. It's zero."*

**The 2020 analogy:**

In 2020, during COVID, content creation was an open field:
- Reels had just launched
- TikTok was banned (opening YouTube/Instagram)
- Huge audience, few high-quality creators
- The arbitrage window was enormous

Those who saw it and acted on it — when most people were hesitant — built massive audiences. Both hosts of this podcast are products of that wave.

**2026 is that moment again — but 100x:**

- In 2020, you needed some skill to win (content creation skill)
- In 2026, AI has removed almost all skill barriers
- Everyone starts from zero
- The person who learns to use it better, faster, more creatively wins

> *"Reel was an opportunity for a small set of people who had some skill. AI is open to everyone."*

**The information arbitrage:**

Right now, the people watching this content are in the top 0.1% of AI knowledge globally. Most people still think AI = ChatGPT for chat. The gap between what you know is possible and what most people think is possible — that gap is the arbitrage.

The question is: do you see it as fear or opportunity?

> *"Instead of seeing it as fear — see it as an opportunity. How do I leverage this information arbitrage to be ahead of most people?"*

### Freshers vs. Experienced Workers

A controversial but data-backed observation:

> *"A smart fresher, six months to one year into AI-first learning, will be better than two or three people with five years of experience."*

Why?
- Freshers have no old SOPs to unlearn
- They are "fluid like water" — quick to adopt new tools
- They don't say "I know how this works" — they experiment
- The new generation is being born into AI

The hiring reality: The middle layer is disappearing.

**Two types of hires that work now:**
1. **Zero experience:** Smart freshers who are AI-native learners
2. **Senior level:** 6–10 years of experience in managing people and strategy

**What doesn't work:** The 2–5 year experience layer — people experienced enough to have old habits but not senior enough to manage teams. This middle tier is being compressed out of existence.
