## 1. Identity & Self-Definition

### Core Principle: Never Lock Yourself Into a Label

Deepinder explicitly refuses to define himself by his achievements — not as "Zomato's founder," not as "a billionaire," not by a rags-to-riches story.

> *"If you define yourself by what you've achieved, you lock yourself into just that. Then you can't become anything more."*

**Why this matters for business builders:**
- Identity rigidity prevents pivoting, learning, and growth
- Leaders who over-identify with past success resist necessary change
- The most adaptable founders hold identity loosely

**What he does identify with:**
- A childlike, curious person who just wants to *do things*
- Someone driven by exploration, not fame or money
- A builder motivated by interest, not outcome

### The "Born to Lead" Poster
He had a poster in his room as a child: *"I was born to lead, never to be ruled."* But he acknowledges it may have been a subconscious revolt against being bullied — not a conscious ambition for power.

**Lesson:** Trace your motivations honestly. Ambition often has emotional roots. Understanding those roots leads to better decision-making.

## 2. Childhood, Resilience & Origin Story

### The Triple Disadvantage
Growing up, Deepinder faced three simultaneous social handicaps:
- **Short** (gained ~1.5 feet of height at age 12)
- **Dark-skinned** — subject to colorism
- **Stammer** — affecting speech confidence

His friends' parents actively discouraged friendships with him. Even teachers participated in social exclusion.

### The Deepest Wound
Not the appearance-based bullying, but **not being listened to.**

> *"When I'd speak, people would start talking about something else mid-sentence. They'd tune out. That hurt the most."*

**Business implication:** Being ignored is often more damaging than being attacked. This wound became his primary motivator — he wanted to build something significant enough that people would *have* to listen.

### The Cheat-to-Win Turning Point
In school, a teacher helped him cheat during an exam, and he came third in class. The result: immediate social validation — a girlfriend, new friends, respect from family.

**Insight extracted:**
- Achievement = social capital in most environments
- This was his first proof that outcomes change perception
- He never became a cheater — his father instilled strong moral values against corruption — but he understood the *leverage* of achievement

### The IIT Path
- Initially placed in the **lowest section** of a competitive coaching batch in Chandigarh
- Felt like an "uninvited guest" — out of place among toppers
- Was about to skip the JEE exam entirely
- His father said: *"You've paid the fees — at least go for practice."*
- He opened a physics book casually one day and something clicked — he just started solving problems for the joy of it
- Physics: near-perfect score. Chemistry and Maths: barely passing. Still got into IIT Delhi.

> *"That's luck. Pure, strong luck."*

**Key lesson:** Showing up (even reluctantly) changes trajectories. Intrinsic curiosity — solving problems without pressure — often unlocks real ability.

## 3. Building a Startup from Zero

### How Zomato Started
Started as a menu-scanning business. No grand vision. Just: *"I don't want to work for anyone, and ₹2 lakh can be made."*

His father gave him almost all his savings — about ₹2 lakhs — to support the early experiment.

> *"Neither of us knew what startups or valuations were. He was just backing his son because — if I don't, who will?"*

### The Visioning Myth
He explicitly rejects the idea that great startups need a 10-year vision:

> *"We never had a vision of what to build. We were just doing things because we wanted to do things."*

**What they did have:**
- A clear focus on the **next week** and **next month**
- Trust in people to do their best work
- A willingness to adapt constantly

### No Financial Models, No Quarterly Goals
Even post-IPO as a publicly traded company, Zomato operates without:
- Quarterly goals
- Annual targets
- Financial forecasts for investors

**What they tell investors instead:**
> *"This is how it has gone until now. This is what we have. We don't know the future."*

The philosophy: if you give someone a goal, they calibrate their effort to that goal — which may be lower than their actual best effort.

## 4. Leadership Philosophy

### Founder Mindset vs. Employee Mindset
The clearest sign of an employee mindset vs. an owner mindset:

> *"There's a big flaw in the system. It will take two hours to fix. It's Friday 6 PM. You say: we'll do it Monday."*

He would not go home. He would finish the job.

**But the nuance:** It's not about clock hours. It's about **attitude**.

> *"Startups don't get built on a 9-to-5 attitude. They can be built on 9-to-5 hours — but not 9-to-5 attitude. Those are different things."*

### Obsession Without Madness
He wants people who are obsessive about building — but clarifies:

> *"I don't want anyone to be mad. Obsession can happen with peace. The best people manage both."*

The ideal: someone who can call their family and say *"I can't make it tonight, I'll make up for it tomorrow"* — and mean it — while still fixing the Friday night bug.

### Vulnerable Leadership
He balances two leadership modes:
1. **Athlete mindset:** Show up regardless of personal state. The team cannot see you low.
2. **Vulnerable leadership:** With 3–4 senior leaders, he openly shares stress, exhaustion, and uncertainty.

> *"When money was running out, I'd put 50–100 senior people in a room and say: listen, we're running out of money. I'm stressed. But that doesn't mean we can't make it. Breathe. Let's go."*

He names the feeling, then pivots to action. He doesn't hide the reality, but he doesn't wallow in it either.

### No Town Halls — For 7+ Years
Because of his stammer, he hasn't addressed the whole company in a live town hall in over 7 years. Instead he runs **Slack town halls** — text-based, threaded, async. He discovered this works *better* for him and arguably for the organization.

**Lesson:** Adapt your leadership style to your authentic strengths. Don't perform leadership modalities that don't work for you.

## 5. Hiring, Firing & People Management

### What He Looks For in Interviews

**Green flags:**
- **Vulnerability** — ability to say "I don't know"
- **Humility** — not over-selling themselves
- **Curiosity** — asking more questions than giving answers

> *"A learner asks more questions than they give answers to."*

**Red flags:**
- Over-selling: *"I'm amazing, I did everything, my team was just execution"*
- This signals politics, ego, and likely poor team attribution

### The Rock Bottom Method
For high-potential people stuck at 10% of their capability, he sometimes deliberately puts them in an emotionally difficult place — "rock bottom" — to force introspection.

- **4 out of 5 times:** The person introspects, opens up, and comes back stronger
- **1 out of 5 times:** It doesn't work, and he carries that guilt

> *"Why would I give feedback to someone I don't care about? The feedback — even the harsh kind — is an act of care."*

He acknowledges this may be rationalisation, but the track record supports it for 80% of cases.

### The Complacency Test (Friday 6 PM Rule)
The test isn't whether someone works weekends — it's whether they show **urgency**:
- Is there a critical bug? Do you find someone to fix it, or defer it?
- Are you running meeting-to-meeting poking holes but never solving?
- Are your all-day "busy" hours actually producing zero company progress?

> *"You've been busy all day. The company made zero progress. But you were *busy*, bro."*

### No HR for People Issues
Zomato's HR team is **recruitment only**. All difficult conversations — performance, exits, conflicts — are handled directly by Deepinder or senior leaders.

### Firing Philosophy
- 6–9 months of observation and feedback before action
- Multiple feedback conversations, both parties try to change
- If divergence persists → exit
- Never done lightly: *"It never gets easier. Every firing feels like the first one."*
- People he fires are people he has **worked alongside**, shared ups and downs with

> *"There is almost zero reason for me to ask anyone I've met to go. So when it happens, there is always personal attachment."*

### The Market-Rate Salary Trap
If someone comes with a competing offer to negotiate:
> *"That conversation is not entertained. If it's gotten to that point, we've already failed them."*

The failure was not recognizing their growth early enough. Giving a 2x salary at that point doesn't rebuild trust — it makes things worse.

## 6. Company Culture & Structure

### Zero Hierarchy
- No reporting managers (in the traditional sense)
- No formal appraisal cycles with pre-set salary timelines
- No defined career paths

> *"There is absolute nothing. This is what you're doing right now. Nothing else."*

**The downside (he admits openly):**
This is genuinely unsettling for employees — the inner chaos of *"Am I in the right place? Will I grow?"* is real. The answer the system gives is:
> *"Look at the people who've been here for years. They're happy. Trust the process."*

### High-Trust Environment
The culture is built entirely on trust:
- People are trusted with their lives, not just their jobs
- No tracking of leaves — unlimited PTO
- Deliveries happen because people genuinely own outcomes

> *"Zomato is a system built on trust. People trust me, I trust them."*

### Titles Are Flexible
He is "Group CEO" but behaves as a Chief Product Officer. The actual CFO role is run by Akshant who handles all investor relations — Deepinder hasn't met investors in 7 years.

> *"We don't fit people into titles. We tailor roles to people."*

### The Eternal Mission
"Eternal" (इटरनल) is not a claim of permanence — it's a **mission statement**:
- Build a leadership pipeline so strong the organization outlasts any individual
- Succession planning is the only true way to build something that lasts
- Systems degrade; culture degrades; only a pipeline of great leaders sustains

## 7. Investor Relations & Fundraising

### The Non-Pitch Pitch
He never gave financial projections to investors. The pitch was:
1. Here is the market size
2. Here is our execution track record
3. We have a fair shot at winning this

> *"We could get these steps wrong. But here's why you should bet on us."*

### Equity Always, Never Debt
A deeply personal rule: he will never raise debt financing. The reason:

> *"If you're taking debt from a position of weakness — when equity can't be raised — that debt will be worse for the business than equity at a lower price."*

The psychology: debt means a future self must pay it back. If that future self can't, collection agents come knocking. This irrational fear (he admits it's irrational) kept Zomato equity-funded through every crisis.

His family's upbringing reinforced this: his parents never took loans. His father fought with him for buying a car on a car loan.

## 8. Competition Strategy

### Zero Competitor Obsession
He does not track Swiggy. He does not order from Zepto to test them. He does not analyze Blinkit's competitors.

> *"We don't think about any of this. We do our own work."*

**Why this works:**
- Competitor obsession is a scarcity mindset
- It pulls resources, attention, and culture toward reaction rather than creation
- The best companies win by being excellent, not by being better than a specific rival

### On Swiggy
Swiggy's customer love in their peak period was real. He admits Zomato lost ground because he resisted the logistics-first model for too long.

> *"When your own friends say they're ordering from Swiggy, not Zomato — you know you have a problem."*

He gives credit where due. No bitterness.

### Swiggy's Shark Tank Move
Swiggy sponsored Shark Tank and had Deepinder removed as a judge. His response:

> *"Cool. Get a lot of cash out of them."*

He wouldn't have done the same. If he were a competitor sponsor, he'd have let the rival judge stay — or simply not sponsored.

> *"My conscience wouldn't allow me to do that. I'd rather not sponsor."*

### On Zepto
- Founder Aaditya Palicha is smart, well-spoken, honest
- Zepto's marketing is creative and nimble
- Many Zepto team members came from Zomato — he takes mild pride in that
- Doesn't spend time worrying about them

## 9. Near-Death Experiences & Crisis Management

### The COVID Crisis: 45 Days of Cash
When COVID hit, Zomato's food delivery revenue was nearly wiped out overnight. Costs remained fixed. They went from 4–5 months of runway to **45 days.**

**What he did:**
1. Called every investor on the cap table: *"I need $1–2 million. Whatever you can give."*
2. Result: **Zero** was raised. Everyone was dealing with their own crisis.
3. Monthly burn: ~$4.5M (₹~45M)

**The solution:** Voluntary salary cuts.

- Asked ~4,000 employees to voluntarily defer salary
- No flat cut imposed — employees chose what they could afford
- Some gave 100%, some gave 50%, some gave nothing (they had families to support)
- Over **80%** of the organization participated
- In return: ESOPs (stock options) — 2–3x the cash deferred

**The outcome:** Those who deferred ₹100 of cash received ESOPs that returned 30x at IPO.

> *"We didn't tell them upfront that ESOPs would be given. That came two days after the cuts — as a thank-you, not as an exchange."*

### Always One More Day
Despite multiple near-death moments, he never felt the company would truly die.

> *"I always felt we had one more day. One more move."*

This is not blind optimism — it's a **decision-making posture** that keeps options open.

## 10. Acquisitions — The Blinkit Story

### The Decision
When Grofers (later Blinkit) was struggling, Deepinder acquired it. The world called it a bailout of his best friend.

His actual reason:
> *"I had never personally experienced a service as addictive as Blinkit. That addictiveness was real."*

He had already tested quick commerce with a few stores near his home. The customer behavior data was undeniable.

**The friendship factor — why it helped, not hindered:**
- He wouldn't bet Zomato's future on an unknown founder
- With Albinder Dhindsa (Aaditya's co-founder), he had 15+ years of deep trust
- You go to war with people you'd trust with your life

> *"This is not a negotiation about equity or ESOPs. This is a spiritual agreement to go to war together."*

### The Hard Call: Asking His Best Friend to Leave
Right after acquisition, he asked Albinder to leave.

> *"You're not going to be able to cut it in this new structure."*

Albinder accepted. They worked together to transition. Albinder later helped reshape the organization. The relationship survived and deepened.

**Lesson:** Friendship and professional objectivity must coexist. Protecting a friend by keeping them in the wrong role is not friendship — it's harm.

### Grofers → Blinkit Rebrand
Grofers was associated with a specific (struggling) business identity. Blinkit was a fresh start — connoting instant delivery.

He likes both "Blinkit" and "Zepto" as names. Both have zing.

## 11. Gig Economy & Delivery Partners

### Scale
- 7.5 lakh (750,000) monthly active gig workers across Zomato and Blinkit
- Monthly churn: ~1.5–2 lakh leave voluntarily (seasonal, temporary)
- Monthly additions: ~1.5–2 lakh join
- Terminations for cause: ~5,000/month (out of 750,000)

### Earnings
Working 6 days/week, 8–10 hours/day: **₹25,000+ per month**

Students and seasonal workers join for a few hours per day, make supplemental income, and leave when not needed. Fully flexible.

### No Time Pressure on Riders
A critical myth he debunks:
> *"No rider at Zomato or Blinkit ever receives a timeline — 'deliver in 10 minutes.' That timer is only visible to the customer."*

**How 10-minute delivery actually works:**
- Delhi NCR has 400+ Blinkit dark stores — one per major neighborhood
- Average packing time: 1 minute 40 seconds
- Distance to customer: often under 1 km
- No pressure needed — physics does the work

Riders don't know what ETA was promised. They just pick up, drop off, return.

### On the Road Accident Allegation (Parliament Claim)
An MP claimed 10-minute delivery causes deaths from reckless riding.

His response:
> *"Would those riders not jump red lights without our app? Indians jump red lights universally. That's not us — that's a culture problem we didn't create."*

His standard: if only Zomato/Blinkit riders were jumping lights, he'd accept the blame. They're not.

### Insurance & Death Policy
- Every partner is insured (minimum ₹10 lakh)
- A team reaches out to families on death
- Personally, he knows 3–4 families of riders who died — he is personally funding their children's education
- *"We know the hidden cost of this business. There is grief, not guilt."*

### The Wealth Distribution Argument
His counterargument to exploitation claims:

> *"The people leaving your employ as drivers and domestic helpers — to become our delivery partners — are leaving because this is better. They now have financial independence, dignity, and their children can go to school consistently. That was not guaranteed before."*

He frames Zomato/Blinkit as a **generational wealth tool** — not for the current workers, but for their children.

## 12. Product & Customer Experience

### Why India Has the World's Best Food Delivery Apps
India's model: **same restaurants on both Swiggy and Zomato**. True competition.

US model (DoorDash, Uber Eats): restaurants are **exclusive** to platforms. No real competition → complacency in customer service.

He actively discourages restaurant exclusivity:
> *"Why would you lose business from the other platform? List everywhere."*

### Customer Fraud — A Real Problem
Common fraud types:
- Hair in food claims (sometimes using AI-generated images)
- "Smashed cake" claims (increasingly AI-generated)
- Cash-on-delivery runners (take change and disappear)
- Missing item claims from regulars

The Karma Score system determines who to believe when there's a dispute between customer and rider.

**50–70% of the time:** When genuinely uncertain, Zomato eats the cost — refunds the customer AND doesn't penalize the rider.

### The Karma Score System
- Tracks both customer and rider behavior over time
- High-karma customers: disputes resolved in their favor quickly
- Low-karma customers: extra scrutiny
- **Not AI-driven** — it's a rule-based scoring system

## 13. Global Expansion — Lessons Learned

### The Original Mistake
In 2012, Zomato expanded internationally because they didn't believe India's market would be large enough. They went to 9 countries.

> *"India positively surprised us. We failed to truly see how large India would become."*

**What went wrong globally:**
- Assumed the model was copy-paste
- Different markets have different restaurant discovery behaviors
- Most markets ended up **mediocre** — neither thriving nor dying

> *"Mediocre is the worst place to be. Either win or go home. Stuck in the middle and you don't even know what to do."*

### The Sunk Cost Trap
He watched his teams fall into sunk-cost thinking: *"We've spent so much — let's do a little more."*

The culture had "Paint the Map Red" posters. Walking away felt like cultural betrayal.

**The pivot:** When the India food delivery war started, he needed capital and people focused domestically. He shut everything down.

> *"If I lose India, there is no global. Home market is sacrosanct."*

### Why Not Re-Expand Now?
India itself is still an **early-stage business**:
- Indians eat out ~4 times/month
- Singapore: ~50 times/month
- US: ~25 times/month

The journey from home-cooked culture to delivery culture is just beginning. The opportunity in India alone is massive — he doesn't want to be distracted.

## 14. Ethics, Integrity & Institutional Values

### The Corruption Discovery
Early Zomato: a salesperson took ₹15,000 cash from a restaurant to lower their commission from 15% to 10%.

When confronted, the salesperson said: *"What's wrong with it? My father does this too."*

> *"Corruption travels through families. People justify it to themselves because they've seen it normalized."*

**Response:** Ethics classes for the entire sales team. Not lectures on morality — practical understanding of *why* this harms the system.

### The "Right to Disconnect" Bill
A proposed Indian law that would let employees ignore work messages after hours.

His position: **Support the law's spirit, but challenge the application to senior roles.**

> *"For a customer care agent who works fixed shifts — absolutely. For a CEO? They chose this. The gun should never leave their head."*

His metaphor: *"As soon as you take the gun off your head — you're dead. Ironically."*

Meaning: the moment a leader stops feeling urgency, the organization starts dying.

### On Deepinder's Own Firing Controversy (April 2025)
Reports of 500–600 employees fired in April 2025 via abrupt Slack deactivation:

> *"That news was wrong. It was about 20 people — performance-based, after months of feedback — and someone dramatized it on social media."*

He doesn't sue. He lets it pass.

## 15. New Ventures — Lat Aerospace, Temple & Continue

### Lat Aerospace
**Vision:** Small planes (6–20 seats) that take off and land in 20 meters. Distributed air stops across cities instead of centralized airports.

**Why:**
- Delhi to Chandigarh: 6 hours by road or 6 hours via airport rigmarole
- If you could fly city-to-city neighborhood in 45 minutes, people would **leave cities**
- Decongestion → wealth distribution → economic activity in smaller towns

**Investment:** ₹~25M committed personally (50% of ₹50M total round)

**Mindset:** If it fails — it fails. It was worth trying.

> *"If we don't try this, who else will?"*

### Temple (Wearable Device)
A non-invasive wearable worn on the temple (side of head) that measures **cerebral blood flow (CBF)** continuously.

**Origin:** He was building a 6-hour daily health routine and realized: *"This is 25% of my life spent on health — with no evidence it's giving me 25% more life."*

He started looking for a **leverage point** — one small input with outsized health output.

The idea: **gravity may be an overlooked aging factor.**

### The Gravity-Aging Hypothesis
His core hypothesis (published publicly, inviting global research):

- Heads are above hearts
- Gravity makes the heart work slightly harder to push blood upward to the brain
- Slightly less blood reaches the brain than optimal
- Over decades, this micro-deficit accumulates
- Brain ages first → body follows (since brain is the master regulator)

**Critical nuance:** He is NOT saying:
- Gravity is the *only* cause of aging
- Gravity is *bad* and should be avoided

He IS saying:
- Gravity may be an *underexplored* aging accelerant
- Intervening in CBF (cerebral blood flow) may be a leverage point for health optimization

**Validation process:**
- 4 contra-teams failed to disprove it
- 100+ global researchers reached out after publication
- Harvard Medical School, French universities, Canadian neuroscientists engaged
- Not yet clinically proven — it's a hypothesis seeking collaborative research

**The device (Temple):**
- Sticker placed on the temple region
- Reads CBF using near-infrared sensors
- 5–7 day battery life
- Waterproof (swim, sauna, triathlon ready)
- Correlates with overall health input/output

**The consistent finding:**
> *"Everything known to be healthy increases brain blood flow. Everything unhealthy decreases it."*

This makes CBF a potential **master biomarker** — one number that reflects your entire health state.

### Continue (.com)
His longevity and brain health research initiative — exploring how to extend healthy years of life, not just lifespan.

## 16. Money, Wealth & Mindset

### His Relationship With Money
- Never chased money — it was never the goal
- Considers it a side effect of building things he cares about
- Spends relatively little considering his net worth

> *"The only thing money does is save you some time. Pretty much that."*

**One exception:** He buys cars during emotional low days. He doesn't drive most of them.

> *"They're childhood toys. Emotional crutches. Thankfully, good cars hold their value."*

### The Abundance Mindset
> *"Money is like orgasms — the more you chase it, the less likely you are to get it."*

This is literally on the wall in his office.

### Charity vs. System Building
His framework:

- Charity is necessary — it cushions present pain
- But charity money runs out
- Systems (companies) generate perpetual employment and wealth cycles
- Generational change comes from systems, not distributions

> *"My father didn't think about what I'd do. He just made enough to send me to school. I did what I did over generations. That's how countries change."*

### No Debt — Personal Rule
Will never take personal or business debt if it can be avoided.

Exception: equity at a lower valuation is always preferable to debt from a position of weakness.

## 17. Personal Life, Relationships & Mental Health

### The Stammer
Still present. Still causes nerves before public appearances. He has chosen to stop trying to fix it.

> *"If I try to fix it, I'm telling myself I'm still bothered by it. So I've stopped telling myself that."*

He's at peace with it because:
> *"Even if I stammer, people now listen to me. I've earned that."*

The stammer also made him a **much better writer** — compensation became a strength.

He sometimes gets physically sick before scheduled public appearances — his body's revolt against the anxiety. Solution: he simply stops committing to such events.

### His Father
- Government teacher, deeply anti-corruption
- Gave almost all his savings to support Zomato's launch
- Still argues with Deepinder about "wasteful spending"
- Both are equally stubborn — can't be in a room with a disagreement without it escalating
- Yet: the relationship is close and loving underneath

### Friendship with Albinder Dhindsa
15+ years of deep friendship. Shared finances, shared failures, shared victories. They've been close, drifted apart, reunited — multiple cycles.

The marker of the friendship: neither has ever kept score on money.

> *"From college days, we've always operated from a shared pool. It's never been 'your money, my money.'"*

### The Impostor Syndrome He Acknowledges
> *"Success always has a huge element of luck. When you consciously think 'I got lucky in so many places' and then receive all the credit — it feels dissonant."*

He calls it genuine impostor syndrome, not false modesty.

### What Angers Him Most
When someone has 100% potential but is playing at 10% — **and is relaxed about it**.

> *"The world owes you nothing. You earn things by creating value for others."*

### Sleep & Wellbeing
- Previously: 5–6 hours per night, terrible sleep
- Now: working toward 7.5 hours using a wind-down routine
- Protocol: phone away by 8:30–9 PM, no stimulation, light music, lie down
- Takes melatonin to help

## 18. Key Business Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. The Gun Metaphor
> *"The gun should never leave your head. The moment it does — you're dead. Ironically."*

Urgency is not stress — it's life force for a startup. Complacency is the silent killer.

### 2. Best Effort > Goals
Setting goals causes people to calibrate effort to those goals. Without goals, best effort is the ceiling — and it's usually higher.

### 3. Skin in the Game vs. Job
The only question that matters for senior leaders:
*"Do they think of this as a job, or as their life?"*

### 4. Rock Bottom as a Tool
Genuine care sometimes requires putting someone through emotional difficulty to break through armor and enable real growth.

### 5. The Mediocrity Trap
In any expansion (geographic, product, team), mediocre performance is more dangerous than failure:
- Failure teaches and frees resources
- Mediocrity traps resources, creates false hope, and prevents decisive action

### 6. Trust as Infrastructure
Zomato's entire operating model is built on trust. High-trust environments outperform high-control environments over time — but require extremely careful cultural maintenance.

### 7. Succession Planning = True Longevity
Systems degrade. Values fade. The only thing that lasts is a **pipeline of leaders** who carry the mission forward.

### 8. Curiosity as Identity
The most durable motivation is not money, not fame, not validation — it's **genuine curiosity**. It is self-renewing, immune to market cycles, and leads to breakthrough ideas.

### 9. Intellectual Honesty as a Core Value
> *"My biggest fear is ending up lying to myself in any way. If I lose intellectual honesty, everything I do can go very wrong."*

Constant self-checking: *Am I confirming my biases? Am I chasing the truth or my preferred version of it?*

### 10. The Scarcity vs. Abundance Lens
Trying to block a competitor (as Swiggy did by removing him from Shark Tank) is a scarcity move. Letting competitors exist and focusing on your own excellence is abundance.
