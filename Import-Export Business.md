## 2. What is Import-Export Business?

Import-Export is the **cross-border trade of goods** between countries. India has an immense opportunity right now because:

- **China vs. USA trade war** → global supply chains are shifting
- India gets the **lowest tariffs** from the US among competing nations
- Africa is a vast **untapped, developing market**
- **190 countries** to sell to — no single exporter can saturate even one country

> *"Import-Export is not the game of buyers. It is the game of sellers."*
> — Fahad Siddiqui

## 3. Personal Journey & Lessons from Failure

### The ₹35 Lakh Scam (Biggest Lesson)

**What happened:**
- Fahad ordered **10 tonnes of copper wire scrap** from a Chinese supplier
- Paid **advance** (Chinese suppliers work only on advance payment)
- Only **800–900 kg arrived** (instead of 10,000 kg) — packed in 10 boxes of ~100 kg each
- Of that, **~200 kg was wood/packing material weight**
- By the time the shipment arrived (1 month shipping), the Chinese company had **shut down and vanished**

**Why it happened — Root Causes:**

| Cause | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Greed & rush** | Wanted to be successful at 25; societal pressure to "settle" |
| **Wrong commodity** | China is itself the world's largest *importer* of copper scrap — buying from them made no sense |
| **Too high discount** | Supplier offered 30–40% below market — a classic fraud signal |
| **No proper guidance** | Only watched YouTube videos; no practical, real-world mentorship |
| **No risk management** | Didn't know "how much risk to take and how to manage it" |

**The aftermath:**
- 1.5–2 months of deep depression (self-described "unofficial depression")
- Stopped socialising, blood sugar spiked
- Family and friends' money was involved — some was returned, some forgiven, some lost
- **But he did NOT quit**

> *"When I was tired, I wanted to take rest — but not to quit."*

**Recovery path:**
- Studied intensively
- Networked with EPCH (Export Promotion Council of Handicrafts, Jodhpur)
- Networked with CEPC (Carpet Export Promotion Council)
- Observed that experienced exporters **never share their methods** — fear of competition
- Eventually: first successful export in 2021 — Leather Shoes to Australia

## 4. Can a Beginner Start This Business?

**Short answer: Yes, but with important caveats.**

- Starting with **zero money** is technically possible, but **odds of success drop significantly**
- Competition is high — fellow exporters are well-resourced and experienced
- This is not the 1990s when one person could dominate a product; today **100 exporters sell the same item**

> *"Going empty-handed significantly reduces your odds. You need at least ₹5–8 lakh to launch comfortably."*

## 6. Documents & Registration Required

### Mandatory (Entry-Level) Documents

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Company Registration** | Business entity (Pvt Ltd, LLP, Proprietorship, etc.) |
| **Bank Account** | Current account in the company's name |
| **GST Registration** | Goods & Services Tax number (18% GST, refundable on exports) |
| **IEC — Import Export Code** | Issued by DGFT; now processed **same day** online; mandatory for any import/export activity |

> **Without these 4, you cannot export or import a single item.**

### Additional Documents (Commodity-Specific)

These vary by sector:

| Sector | Additional Documents |
|---|---|
| **Pharmaceuticals** | Clinical trials, FDA clearance |
| **Food & Edibles** | FSSAI license, FDA approval |
| **Nutraceuticals** | Separate regulatory approval |
| **Carpets / Handicrafts** | EPCH/CEPC membership can help |
| **General manufactured goods** | Usually the 4 basics are sufficient |

## 7. Choosing the Right Commodity

### The Ideal Category for Beginners: **Desire Goods (Luxury / Lifestyle)**

**Definition:** Products where the buyer does NOT bargain on price — they buy out of desire, not necessity.

**Why this works:**
- Maximum margin
- Less price negotiation
- Finished goods → no further processing needed by the buyer

**Examples of Desire-Category Products:**

- Leather handbags, wallets, shoes
- Handicrafts
- Hand-knotted / hand-tufted carpets & rugs
- Home décor items
- Jewellery and luxury accessories

### High-Margin Example: Handmade Carpets

- Made in **Bhadohi-Mirzapur** (UP) — India's carpet hub
- Takes 1.5–2 years to make a high-quality piece
- Cost of making: ₹1 lakh
- Selling price: ₹3 lakh+
- **Margin: 200–300%**
- Buyers will **wait and pay** because the product is irreplaceable

### What to AVOID (Especially for Beginners)

| Product | Reason to Avoid |
|---|---|
| **Copper/metal scrap** | High fraud risk; China itself imports this |
| **Fresh fruits & vegetables to UAE** | Market is oversaturated; supply > demand; losses common |
| **Any commodity with export bans** | E.g., onions, rice — India periodically bans these to control domestic prices |
| **Semi-finished goods** | Margins are slim; multiple processing steps reduce your leverage |

### Need vs. Desire Products — Key Difference

| Category | Example | Margin | Bargaining |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Need** | Rice, onions | Very low | Extremely high; 1% can break a deal |
| **Desire** | Leather bags, carpets | Very high | Minimal |

## 8. Step-by-Step Process to Start

### Step 1 — Choose Your Product (Commodity Research)
- Pick a **desire-category, finished good**
- Verify demand internationally (use trade data, portals, market research)
- Ensure India has a manufacturing hub for it

### Step 2 — Find a Reliable Supplier
- The **most critical step** — 90% of deals fail because of supplier problems
- Test them with various conditions:
  - Smallest possible order
  - Largest possible order
  - Fastest timeline
  - Slowest timeline
  - Lowest price
  - Highest quality requirement
- A **good supplier** stays consistent across all these scenarios

### Step 3 — Build Your Documentation
- Get IEC, GST, Company Registration, Bank Account
- Additional commodity-specific licenses if needed

### Step 4 — Develop Communication & Sales Skills
- Presentation, negotiation, English + local language proficiency
- How to conduct video calls professionally
- Avoid WhatsApp-only deals for significant transactions

### Step 5 — Identify Target Market / Country
- Research which country has demand for your product
- Start with 1 city in 1 country
- **Recommended for beginners:** East Africa (Tanzania, Kenya)

### Step 6 — Contact Buyers
- Build your **profile/brand** so buyers come to you
- Use LinkedIn, trade fairs, export councils
- Avoid cold-calling random contact lists — it rarely converts

### Step 7 — Logistics & Supply Chain
- Partner with a CHA (Customs House Agent)
- Understand packaging requirements, labelling, HS codes
- Get competitive freight rates

### Step 8 — Execute & Iterate
- First shipment — document everything
- Build on the relationship
- Expand to more products or countries

## 10. Finding a Reliable Supplier

### The 90% Problem
- **90% of Indian suppliers back out at the last moment**
- They promise one thing, deliver another (different colour, quality, quantity)
- Indian suppliers are far less cooperative than Chinese suppliers

### Chinese vs. Indian Suppliers — Comparison

| Parameter | Chinese Suppliers | Indian Suppliers |
|---|---|---|
| Response time | 15–20 minutes for quotation | 6+ days |
| Professionalism | Very high | Generally low |
| Small order acceptance | Yes (even 1 piece for drop-shipping) | Rarely |
| Commitment to quote | Honoured for stated validity | Often changed last minute |
| Communication | Disciplined, via VPN on WhatsApp | Unpredictable |
| Proforma Invoice | Within 2 hours | May take weeks |

### How to Test a Supplier (Before Committing)

- Ask for quotes at multiple price points and timelines
- Request samples
- Check if they respond consistently across all queries
- Video call them; visit in person if the deal is large
- Check GST registration, business history, reviews from other buyers

## 11. Finding Buyers (Importers)

### The Right Mental Model

> *"Export is not the game of buyers — it is the game of sellers. China is the world's biggest exporter not because it has the best buyers, but because it has the best sellers."*

**Wrong approach (common beginner mistake):**
- Download port data
- Cold-call/email random buyers
- Spam LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook

**Right approach:**
- Build a **strong personal/company profile**
- Develop such strong communication that **buyers come to you**
- Position yourself as an expert in your niche

### Where Do Buyers Actually Come From?

- **Trade fairs** (domestic & international)
- **Export Promotion Councils** (EPCH, CEPC, APEDA, etc.)
- **B2B platforms** (use with caution — Alibaba is now considered risky)
- **Referrals** from existing buyer relationships
- **Inbound marketing** — LinkedIn content, website, product catalogues

### On B2B Portals like Alibaba

> **"They are dead now."** — Fahad Siddiqui

Reasons:
- Riddled with scammers (both Indian and foreign)
- Fraudulent buyers placing fake orders
- No quality control on who signs up

**Alternative:** Build direct relationships through councils and trade shows.

## 12. Country-Wise Opportunities

### China

**Role:** World's largest exporter — "Factory of the World"

**Why China dominates:**
- Province-wise specialisation implemented ~1970s by visionary leadership
- Each province masters one sector (glass, toys, hardware, handicrafts, etc.)
- Government trained youth, set up manufacturing plants, gave subsidies
- **Cheap labour cost** → cost-competitive globally
- Sewing needle to aircraft — everything is manufactured

**For Indian exporters — what to buy from China:**
- Electronics, hardware, machinery components
- Raw materials for manufacturing
- High-precision goods

**What NOT to buy from China:**
- Copper scrap (China itself imports it — never buy from a country that imports what you want to buy)
- Any commodity where China has no genuine exportable surplus

**China + Luxury Brands (Interesting Reality):**
- Louis Vuitton, Gucci, and many luxury brands manufacture in China
- To claim "Made in Country X", minimum **40% value addition** must happen there
- A ₹4 lakh LV bag might cost ₹4,000–₹5,000 to make in China
- The brand sells the name, not the manufacturing

**Chinese Professionalism:**
- Start at 8:00–8:30 AM
- 2-hour turnaround on quotes for ₹1 crore orders
- Work through VPN to use WhatsApp despite it being blocked in China
- Trained from childhood to do business

### UAE / Dubai

**Strategic Position:**
- Geographically central — India/Asia to the right, Africa/Europe/Americas to the left
- Primary function: **Re-trade hub** (buy from one country, sell to another)

**Why UAE works:**
- Only 5% VAT (vs. India's 30% income tax + 18% GST = 48%)
- No concept of black/white money — only "green money" (what you earn is yours)
- Makes Dubai attractive for storing and re-routing capital

**The Dark Side of UAE for Fresh Produce:**
- **Severely oversaturated** — containers of vegetables rot because supply >> demand
- 1,000+ Indian exporters have already flooded this market
- ₹10 lakh of produce sells for ₹8 lakh after freight, storage, and competition losses
- **Coaches falsely promote UAE fresh fruit/vegetable exports** — avoid this

**What actually works in UAE:**
- Use it as a **re-export hub**, not a final consumption market
- Re-trade: Import from India → add value or repackage → sell to Africa/Europe

**Corruption Level:**
- Significant corruption exists in UAE trade — be aware
- More visible because many South Asians are involved and word spreads in Hindi

### Africa

**The Single Biggest Opportunity for Indian Exporters Right Now**

**Why Africa?**
- Developing continent — where India was 40 years ago
- Limited manufacturing, high demand for basic to mid-range goods
- B2B + B2C + **B2G (Business to Government)** all work here
- Government tenders are available at **good margins** (not exploitative)
- Easy entry market for newcomers

**Safe Regions:**

| Region | Countries | Safety |
|---|---|---|
| **East Africa (Best)** | Tanzania, Kenya, Zambia | Very safe and stable |
| **West Africa** | Nigeria, Sierra Leone | Generally safe; check current news |
| **North Africa** | Morocco, Egypt | Safe for trade |
| **Avoid** | DRC (Democratic Republic of Congo) | Historically unstable |

**What to Sell in Africa:**

| Product | Margin | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Textiles / Garments | 30–40% | Africa doesn't produce competitively |
| Animal feed | 30–40% | Huge market; minimal local production |
| Tiles (ceramics) | 20–60% | One of Fahad's students (age 23) sold 250 containers |
| Leather goods | Good margins | Desire category works well |
| Machinery (JCBs, Tractors) | High | Second-hand Indian equipment is in demand |
| Decorative items | High | Desire category |

**Indian Presence in Africa:**
- Gujarati business communities well-established in Kenya (some of the richest billionaires in Kenya are Indians)
- Tanzania has significant Indian community; Fahad himself has a company there
- Sierra Leone (West Africa): Gujaratis trading diamonds and gold

**Business Model That Works:**
- Import second-hand JCBs/tractors from India → rent them out in Africa → hire locals as drivers → sit back and collect revenue

**Key Insight:**
> *"In 10 years, competition in Africa will increase and margins will compress. The time to enter is NOW."*

### USA

**World's largest economy — highest buying power**

**Why sell to the USA:**
- Dollar earnings → ₹87+ per dollar at current rates
- Americans are **credit-card consumers** — they spend freely (often beyond means)
- High purchasing power → will pay for quality

**What to sell:**
- Nearly everything: USA imports from India across 95%+ of categories
- Best categories: **Top-quality finished goods** (USA demands quality, not cheapness)
- Indian handicrafts, textiles, pharma, IT services, gems & jewellery all do well

**Challenge:**
- Must maintain **top-notch quality** — USA buyers are unforgiving of substandard goods
- Quality control in manufacturing must be tight

**USA's Trade Reality:**
- Largest importer AND large exporter globally
- Very high **labour cost** in USA → manufacturing is expensive there → good for Indian exporters
- USA economy runs significantly on **debt** (both government and citizens)

## 15. HS Code — What It Is & Why It Matters

**HS = Harmonised System**
Also called **HSN (Harmonised System of Nomenclature)**

- Every product in the world has a unique numeric code
- Used universally in shipping documents (Bill of Lading, customs declarations)
- Ranges from **2 digits to 12 digits** (more digits = more specific)
- **Standard used globally: 8-digit HS Code**

**Example:**
- "Mango" as a fruit = one HS code
- Mango pickle = different HS code
- Mango seeds = different HS code
- Mango tree seedling = different HS code

**How to find your HS Code:**
- DGFT website
- Talk to your CHA (Customs House Agent)
- Confirm with your buyer (they know what their country's customs will accept)

**Does a wrong HS code cause problems?**
- Historically yes, but now much easier to verify
- Confirm with buyer before shipping
- Your CHA/freight forwarder can also help verify

## 16. DGFT — Key Regulatory Body

**DGFT = Directorate General of Foreign Trade**

- India's **apex body** governing all import-export activity
- Under the Ministry of Commerce & Industry
- Issues the **IEC (Import Export Code)** — mandatory for all exporters/importers

**Key functions:**
- Issues IEC codes (now issued same-day online)
- Publishes circulars on banned/restricted/allowed items
- Sets export incentive schemes
- Manages Foreign Trade Policy

**Practical tip:**
- Download the **DGFT mobile app** (free)
- Stay updated on:
  - What items are banned/restricted
  - Export duty changes
  - New schemes and incentives
  - Policy announcements

## 17. Government Schemes for Exporters

### Most Impactful: **GST Refund on Exports**

- When you export goods, the **entire GST you paid is refunded**
- This is a massive benefit: 5%, 12%, or 18% (depending on product) comes back to you
- Makes Indian goods more price-competitive globally

### Other Schemes (Smaller but useful)
- Duty Drawback Scheme
- MEIS / RoDTEP (Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products)
- Export credit guarantee (ECGC) — insurance against buyer default
- Interest equalisation scheme for MSME exporters
- Various sector-specific incentives via Export Promotion Councils

> *"The GST refund alone (5–18%) is the biggest government support for exporters. Smaller schemes give 1–2%, which matters less."*

## 18. Communication Skills — The #1 Weapon

**This is the single most important skill in import-export.**

> *"If your communication has strength, you can sell a comb to a bald man."*
> *"Selling is very easy for those who know how to do it."*

### What "Communication" Actually Means

It is NOT just:
- Speaking English fluently
- Using fancy vocabulary

It IS:
- **Confidence** in how you carry yourself
- **Body language** and personality
- Ability to **understand the buyer's real need**
- Knowing when to speak and when to listen
- Ability to **build trust** quickly
- Speaking the buyer's local language where needed

### Communication in Local Markets

- When sourcing from local Indian suppliers — speaking in local language builds trust
- A foreigner speaking formal English in a local market = easy target for overcharging
- Know Hindi, and if possible, the regional language of your supplier's area

### Why Communication Beats Everything Else

- A buyer with ₹20 lakh to spend will NOT give it to someone who seems uncertain
- Experienced buyers (some with 90 years of family business) will judge you in **5 minutes**
- They know if you're genuine or wasting their time
- Your **profile, confidence, and presentation** must make the buyer feel safe giving you money

## 21. Top 3 Pieces of Advice for Beginners

### Advice 1: Exit the Fantasy World

- Nobody is coming to save you
- This life is your **own responsibility**
- Don't be lured by "invest ₹10, earn ₹100" YouTube content
- Ground reality is different from reel reality
- **Learn first, then act** — formal guidance beats trial-and-error (which is expensive)

### Advice 2: Take Controlled, Calibrated Risk

- Only risk what you can afford to lose
- If someone is selling you dreams, **walk away immediately**
- Build a real business in the real world — not in dreamland
- Understand your risk appetite and stay within it
- Use guidance/mentorship to understand risk management

### Advice 3: Start Learning Now (Especially if Young)

- If you're 16–20 years old: **this is your golden window**
- Your degree alone will not protect you
- By the time you graduate, a degree = **"just a piece of paper"** that gets you a survival wage
- **Invest in skills** — in any field you love
- **Discipline > Motivation**: Motivation fades in 5 days; discipline builds a business that lasts 90 years

> *"Sone ko bhi aag mein tapna padta hai." (Even gold has to be refined in fire.)*
