## 2. Childhood & Early Life

### Family Structure

- Parents divorced when Dana was **8 years old**
- Left with his mother; father largely absent
- One sister — they remain extremely close ("both our parents are dead, it's just us")
- Father struggled with **alcohol**

### The Father

- Selfish, lived for himself
- Took Dana and his sister to see *Beverly Hills Cop* — arrived **drunk**, seats were gone, threw money at them for popcorn
- That moment taught Dana what **carelessness** looks like
- Dana's lesson from his father: **"This is the direction we don't want to go."**

### The Mother

- **Single mom, nurse** — worked extra shifts AND went to school simultaneously
- Effectively raised two children alone
- Moved the family cross-country from **Boston to Las Vegas** with two kids, a dog, and a car
- Drove through tornados in the Midwest doing it
- Later relationship broke down over **money demands** (see Section 11)

### The Chores Lesson

Dana and his sister had strict household chores — mowing lawn, cleaning pool, trimming bushes.

> *"The day I make a dollar, I'm never doing this ever again."*

This instilled in him the drive to earn his own way out.

### Quit Drinking at 21

Dana quit alcohol the moment it became legal for him to drink.

> *"I believed that I wouldn't be able to accomplish all the things I wanted to accomplish if I was drinking. I saw family members where drinking had been detrimental to their lives."*

He watched talented family members waste their potential through alcohol — and chose a different path.

## 3. The Anti-Victim Philosophy

### His Core Belief

> *"You see people play the victim — 'I didn't have any role models growing up.' Yes, you did. You just didn't look at it the right way."*

Everyone in your life is a role model — in **good and bad ways**. The people who showed him what NOT to do were just as valuable as positive examples.

### On the Past

> *"The rearview mirror means nothing. Whatever happened yesterday means nothing today."*

- Doesn't reflect on the past
- Doesn't look back at business wins either
- Always focused on: **"What's next? What's next? What's next?"**

### On Crying & Being Hurt

> *"What does crying do? How does crying help in any way, shape, or form?"*

- Doesn't know the last time he cried
- Claims it's "pretty hard to hurt his feelings"
- Credits this to being raised by a "tough crew" — when people who were supposed to love you say harsh things, you develop **thick skin**

> *"They built me for this life."*

He would not change his childhood, his parents, or his setup — he says it was all incredible preparation.

## 4. Entrepreneurship — The Real Picture

### Who Should NOT Be an Entrepreneur

> *"A lot of people, especially young people these days, want to be entrepreneurs because they'd love to plan their own schedule."*

> *"You're going the wrong way. You need to find a 9-to-5 job where you have weekends and holidays off."*

If your reason to start a business is **more free time** — you've chosen the wrong thing.

### What Entrepreneurship Actually Looks Like

- **No days off** — no birthdays, no Christmas, no Thanksgiving
- Bad things happen **every single day** that need solving
- Office 8:30 AM to 7/8/9/10 PM — still, after 25+ years
- Travel: **4 days home in the last 2 months**
- "When your foot hits the floor every morning, you're going to war"
- This younger generation thinking you can **work from home** and build a successful business — "It's impossible"

### The Daily Chaos Test

One morning, going to a board meeting at 9 AM:
- Both electric razors died
- Car tire pressure sensor went off mid-drive — "Yeah, we're not stopping"
- Got to work → laundry list of problems

> *"That's just life standing there to kick you in the face. You have to get through all of this."*

**Key insight:** The ability to find solutions to problems **every single day** is what makes a good entrepreneur.

### The Son Lesson — U-Haul & Hard Work

His youngest son Aiden flew home from college with a business idea, wanting to quit school.

Dana said: **"You're not quitting school. Go get a job first."**

Aiden worked at U-Haul.

Why? Because Dana wants his kids to understand what **real hard work** looks like:

> *"Real hard work is construction. Hard work is concrete. 115° out in summer. 30° in winter. Physical labor every day. You eat, go to bed, get up, do it again."*

> *"In your life, to figure out what you want to do, you have to figure out what you don't want to do."*

### Nobody Owes You Anything

Story from Dana's youth — a friend whose father put $1 million away for him. Father hit hard times and took it back.

Friend said: *"My father stole a million dollars from me."*

Young Dana (age 12-13) thought:

> *"Are you out of your mind? The fact that your dad even thought of you that way, to put that money away, is incredible. But that was never YOUR money. You didn't earn that money."*

That guy grew up exactly as expected — with an **entitlement mentality**.

> *"Nobody owes you anything. Nobody."*

## 5. Building UFC — From $2M to $50 Billion

### The Purchase (2001)

- Bought with Frank and Lorenzo Fertitta for **$2 million**
- Nobody wanted it — "too barbaric"
- Couldn't get TV deals, couldn't get sponsors, couldn't hire production people (they all wanted NYC or LA, not Vegas)
- Craig Borsari left a rising career at **Fox** to join because he believed in the vision

### The Benchmarking Math

> *"The Lakers sold for $12.5 billion. The UFC is worth $50 billion."*

4x the value — built from a $2 million purchase.

### The Paramount Deal

- **$7.7–8 Billion, 7-year deal**
- Only global boxing TV deal — on Paramount, a **broadcast network**, not streaming
- Every other boxing promoter chased streaming deals → **killed their viewership**

> *"What we have done over the last 25 years is built a brand that not only the consumer trusts, but sponsors, television networks — the list goes on and on."*

## 6. The Formula — What Made UFC Unstoppable

Dana refuses to fully reveal the "formula" (he deliberately keeps 3 key daily focus areas secret), but from the interview, several pillars emerge:

### Pillar 1: Matchmaking

> *"Matchmaking is number one."*

The quality of fights you put together defines everything. You can't judge a card until it's over — those "bad cards" often produce the best fights.

### Pillar 2: Live Event Production

People who attend **live events** spend far more than TV viewers (flights, hotels, food, tickets).

> *"Every time we put on a live event, I want people walking out going, 'God damn, that was a good time. I'm glad we did this and I would love to do this again.'"*

### Pillar 3: Television Production Quality

- Dana sits ringside with a **red phone** that goes directly to the truck/producers
- Zach (producer) has freedom to try things
- Dana's feedback: instant, direct, no ego on either side
- "What was that?" → "Never do that again." Or: "That was incredible."

### Pillar 4: Long-term Brand Trust

While every boxing promoter was doing **"going out of business sales"** (grab money and run), Dana focused on:
- Sponsors who WANT to be there
- Networks that TRUST the brand
- Fans who come back event after event

> *"I am a logo slap guy — NOT. I want you to WIN when you're with me."*

### Pillar 5: Being the Customer

> *"I am a fight fan to the core. 25 years later, I am still a hardcore fight fan."*

> *"I am the customer."*

This is his superpower — he knows when something is great because he feels it as a fan.

### Pillar 6: Never Saying No

> *"The only thing you can't say at UFC headquarters is no."*

## 7. People & Team Building

### The Evolution

- **Start:** Wore all hats, 2 employees (including himself)
- **Now:** Rock-star teams in every discipline

### Superpower

> *"If there's a superpower I have, it has been picking the right people to surround myself with."*

### What He Looks For

- Talented at their craft
- **Hard workers** — talent alone isn't enough
- **Drive** — work ethic + ambition
- **Aligned with the vision** — everyone on the same page

### Key Team Members He Mentions

| Person | Role | Why Great |
|---|---|---|
| **Craig Borsari** | Head of Production | "The best in the business" — left rising Fox career to join UFC |
| **Len Breenidge** | Head of PR | "I don't make a move without talking to her" |
| **Hunter Campbell** | Business | Killer |
| **Zach** | Show Producer | Runs all shows, has creative freedom |

### The "Killers" Principle

> *"You can't be within a mile of me if you're not a killer."*

But Dana is clear — **not every employee needs to be a killer**. The organization needs all types. Just the inner circle must be killers.

### COVID — The Team Test

During COVID, Dana gathered ALL employees in the dining room and said:

> *"If any of you feel unsafe or have loved ones that could be unsafe, you can go home."*

**Nobody walked out.**

He did weekly FaceTime calls with all employees. Offered groceries, toilet paper, help if a loved one got sick.

Within weeks: *"We're going to work. Everybody — men, women — said we're with you. Let's roll."*

> *"I will never pick money over people ever."*

### On the "Team = Family" Debate

CEO quote he hates: *"Anyone trying to sell you we're a family is full of [BS]. This is transactional."*

Dana's response:

> *"My team IS the way I feel. And I do care. Who wants to run through a wall for a guy who says it's purely transactional?"*

His qualifier: The people who have shown **loyalty** are treated like family. It's earned, not automatic.

> *"If you don't have loyalty — what do you have?"*

## 8. Focus, Signal vs. Noise

### The Problem at the Top

The more successful you become, the more things **scream for your attention**:
- Movie deals
- Biopic requests
- Journalist commentary
- Random fighters making statements
- Ego-driven side opportunities

> *"You have to figure out what you pay attention to and what you don't."*

### What He Blocks Out

- Media that covers the sport → "Everything they say is wrong. Blocked from my feed."
- "Experts" with phones and podcasts → "Always wrong, waste of time"
- His ego → "When you let your ego get into all of this... you mess it up"

### What He Pays Attention To

- **Hardcore fans** — what they think about the show, the match, the card
- **His team** in the room brainstorming
- **Real operators** — people doing the actual work

### The Kevin O'Leary Reference

Dana references **"signal and noise"** as a framework — you have to constantly filter for the signal (what actually matters) and block out the noise (everything else screaming for attention).

## 9. Killer Instinct & Manifestation

### Can Killer Instinct Be Learned?

> *"H — that's a good question. I don't know if I've ever experienced somebody who didn't have it and got it."*

He's not sure it can be taught. But he believes if you want to be in business for yourself:

> *"You have to have a certain mindset and you have to be a killer."*

### Manifestation — Dana's Version

> *"Manifesting is real, and I'm a big believer."*

But — and this is critical — his version includes **the work**:

> *"When you decide who you are, what you want to do, what you're going to accomplish, and you get up every single day and work toward it — it is impossible for you to fail."*

The vision board without the gas pedal = useless.

> *"You program your GPS system and then never step on the gas — you go nowhere."*

### What Success Means

> *"My thing about success in life: Are you happy? Do you get up every day and love what you do?"*

> *"I know a lot of billionaires that are miserable human beings."*

Being happy is **a choice**.

## 10. The No Regrets Framework

### The Vision Quest Movie Lesson

A wrestling movie that changed Dana's life. Key scene: A chef character delivers a speech about watching Pelé play football on TV — alone in his apartment, he starts crying because *a human being, a species he's actually part of, can do something that incredible*.

The lesson: **"It's not the six minutes. It's what happens in those six minutes that matters."**

> *"We don't have time. There's not a lot of time. It DOES seem like we're going to live forever. But we're not."*

### The Hotel Bellman Moment

At 19, Dana walked out of a hotel job that had:
- Great cash pay
- Weekly paychecks
- 401k
- Full dental and medical

He watched a 45-year-old colleague — same money, same benefits, same schedule — and thought:

> *"I don't want to be 45 and standing in this lobby wondering what would have happened."*

He left to "get into the fight business" — not knowing exactly what that meant at what level.

> *"If I have to go back and go back to work, I tried. I gave it a shot. The worst case scenario is you never try."*

### The Big House Principle

When building his house in Vegas, he went way over budget.

> *"I wake up every day in my house and I love it. And I've been there eight years and I've never said, 'Damn, I should have.' Never."*

Applying the same logic to everything in life:

> *"Do not be afraid to take risk. What's the worst case scenario? You end up back at work. And it's not a bad worst case scenario."*

### The Final Quote

> *"Don't be the guy sitting home alone in your apartment thinking, 'I could have done more. I could have been bigger. I could have been better.' That is the worst thing that can happen to anybody."*

## 11. Loyalty, Family & Culture

### The Mother Situation

On a two-week trip to London with Lorenzo Fertitta, Dana was off email.

When he returned: **A chain of threatening emails from his mother** demanding a large sum of money to fund a bar purchase — with escalating ultimatums.

> *"That was the beginning of the end with me and her."*

His mother later published an **unauthorized biography** — with wedding photos and childhood pictures — without consent. He had never read the book at the time of this interview (lawyers briefed him). Many people mentioned in the book did nothing to her.

> *"Sometimes family is the worst. Pale in comparison to what you can get from some family members."*

Both parents passed away while Dana and his sister were **not speaking to either of them**.

His father: issues around distance and selfishness.  
His mother: money demands and the book.

> *"If you fail as a parent, it's the ultimate failure."*

### Forgiveness

> *"I wouldn't change anything. My parents made me a better parent."*

He credits both parents for making him who he is — good and bad — and doesn't tell negative stories about them publicly.

### His Sister

- Super close
- As adults they "talk about this stuff a lot"
- Both parents gone → "it's just us"

### His Own Parenting

Becoming a parent made him understand what **unconditional parental love** should feel like — and highlighted how different his parents were.

He aims for balance: giving his kids challenges (chores, jobs) while also supporting them in the right ways.

## 12. Health Transformation — Gary Brecka

### The Problem at 50

- Severe **sleep apnea** — waking up almost every night, throwing up from it
- Low energy, various health issues
- Doctors kept "switching pills" — nothing fixed

### The Gary Brecka Introduction

- Introduced by Carrie Kasem
- Dana initially reluctant
- Met at his son's 21st birthday in Miami
- Gary predicted Dana had **10 years to live** based on blood work

> *"Me and my wife truly believe he was wrong. We thought I had less because of the way I would sleep."*

### The 12-Week Protocol

- "Give me 12 weeks and I'll change your life. It's going to be hard."
- Dana: "It won't be hard. Once I set my mind to it, I'll do exactly what you tell me."
- Result at 6 weeks: **"I never felt better."**
- Sleep apnea: **Gone.**
- Clarity, energy, legs — all fixed

> *"I wouldn't be doing all the other stuff I'm doing. I wouldn't be able to run at the level I run at right now without him."*

### Lessons for Everyone

- Find what's actually wrong (blood work, data)
- Commit fully — half-measures don't work
- The body can be dramatically transformed with the right information and discipline
- Fix the foundation (sleep, hormones, nutrition) before piling on more work

## 13. On Competitors & Boxing

### Why Boxing Failed

Traditional boxing promoters ran a **"going out of business sale"** model:
- Every fight = grab as much money as possible and run
- Chased TV deals off broadcast to streaming → killed mainstream viewership
- Sponsors left
- Networks left

> *"I have the only global television deal right now on Paramount. All other promoters are on streaming services."*

### Why Dana Will Win in Boxing

- Boxing is his **first love** — he grew up studying it
- He can see exactly what's broken and knows how to fix it
- Already **two years ahead** of where he thought he'd be

> *"The guys doing it right now are just so bad at it."*

### On Eddie Hearn

- Eddie's father **Barry Hearn** is "the real guy" — built the business
- Eddie has impressive work rate but Dana sees him as not the principal architect
- Dana respects **Frank Warren** — old school, no talk, just action

> *"Frank Warren's just like, 'Let's fight. Let's start.' That's a guy."*

### The "No Competitors" Mindset

> *"All these other guys putting on fights — they don't matter. My competitors are the NFL, Major League Baseball, the NHL, and the NBA."*

Once you reach the top, you reframe who you're competing with entirely. Dana no longer sees other fight promoters as competition — he's fighting for attention against the biggest sports leagues on earth.

## 15. The Timing & Luck Factor

### Dana on Luck

> *"I wouldn't call it luck. I'd call it timing. Everything in life is about timing."*

### The Wedding Story — The Sliding Doors Moment

**Background:** Dana quit drinking at 21 and had a rule in his 20s — he did not go to anybody's wedding. Too much time, travel, energy.

**Exception:** His childhood friend Adam Cargan — who used to wake up **45 minutes to an hour early** every day just to drive Dana to high school because Dana had no car.

> *"How am I not going to go to Adam Cargan's wedding with all the things he did for me?"*

At the wedding, he ran into **Lorenzo Fertitta** — hadn't seen him in 10+ years.

Lorenzo: *"I just got on the Nevada Athletic Commission. I heard you have a boxing gym. I want to come train with you."*

Lorenzo came in on Monday. They've been together ever since. Eventually: **bought the UFC for $2 million together.**

> *"If you hadn't been loyal to your friend from school and gone to his wedding… who knows?"*

**Sliding doors.** One act of loyalty changed everything.

### On Karma

> *"I don't believe in much. I'm not religious. I don't believe in God, the devil, ghosts, goblins. But I believe in karma because I've seen it play out — good and bad."*

## 💡 3 Most Counterintuitive Insights

### 1. Don't Start a Business to Have More Freedom
If you want work-life balance and personal time — get a 9-to-5. Entrepreneurship means zero days off, being at war daily, and the more successful you get, the more people come after you.

### 2. The "Team = Family" Isn't BS — It's Just Earned
The reason people hate "we're a family" from CEOs is because those CEOs fire people during hard times. Dana's version: those who showed up and stayed loyal ARE family. Everyone else is a colleague.

### 3. The Rearview Mirror is Useless — Even for Wins
