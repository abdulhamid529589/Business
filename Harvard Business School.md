## 1. Career Capital Framework

### The Equation

```
Career Capital = Intellectual Capital + Social Capital + Brand Capital
```

| Component | What It Means | How to Build It |
|---|---|---|
| **Intellectual Capital** | What you know and how you apply it | Top-quality education; project-based learning; case studies |
| **Social Capital** | Who you know; the strength and breadth of your network | Build strong ties AND weak ties; attend events; diversify your network beyond your industry |
| **Brand Capital** | How people talk about you when you're not in the room | Perform visibly; build personal brand; get known beyond your immediate circle |

---

### Strong Ties vs. Weak Ties

| Type | Definition | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong Ties** | People you can call at 3 AM — deep trust relationships | Emotional support, funding, personal references |
| **Weak Ties** | Acquaintances across different industries and functions | **Information** and **influence** — the source of opportunities and insights you can't get from close friends |

> **Key Insight:** Most career breakthroughs come from **weak ties**, not strong ones.  
> Your next job, investor, or partner likely comes from someone you barely know.

**Questions to audit your network:**
- Are all your weak ties in the **same industry**?
- Are all your weak ties in the **same function**?
- Are all your weak ties in the **same country**?

If yes to any — you are limiting your own growth.

---

### Ashwin's Real Example — Harvard Network in Action

- Two Harvard classmates (strong ties) gave him his **first investment cheque**
- 4 out of 5 board members were **HBS alumni**
- First VC investor was an **HBS alum** (KP Badrash, Westbridge)

> *"It was not the business plan that had merit alone — those two knew me, liked me, trusted me. That's social capital."*

## 2. The Power of Compounding in Career

### Scenario A — Small Starting Difference

| Person | Starting Salary | Annual Increment | Salary After 20 Years |
|---|---|---|---|
| Person A | ₹15 Lakhs | 12% | X |
| Person B | ₹20 Lakhs | 12% | X + **₹3.5 Crores** |

> A ₹5 lakh difference at the start compounds to **₹3.5 Crore** in 20 years.

---

### Scenario B — Career Jumps from Education & Networking

| Person | Starting Salary | Growth Strategy | Salary After 20 Years |
|---|---|---|---|
| Person A | ₹20 Lakhs | 12% increment only | Y |
| Person B | ₹20 Lakhs | 12% + **30% jump every 5 years** (3 times) | Y + **₹9 Crores** |

> **Lesson:** Invest in education, social capital, and brand capital — they create these periodic jumps.

## 3. Negotiation Frameworks

*(Based on HBS Professor Jim Sebenius's course — linked to the Harvard Negotiation Project)*

---

### 3.1 Anchoring

> **Rule:** If the negotiation is **price-only** → **go first**.

- Making the first offer sets the psychological anchor
- The other party's counteroffer gravitates toward your number
- Being bold with the anchor gives you room to negotiate down while still winning

---

### 3.2 Open-Ended vs. Closed Questions in Negotiation

| Question Type | Example | When to Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Closed** | "Should I do X or Y?" | When you want a binary decision |
| **Open-Ended** | "Why do you want this?" / "What matters most to you?" | When you're negotiating — to **discover interests** |

> If there are **many issues** beyond price → **listen first**, ask open questions.

---

### 3.3 Position vs. Interest

| Concept | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Position** | What someone says they want | "I won't sell my house at any price." |
| **Interest** | Why they want it — the deeper motivation | "My dog is buried in the garden. I need to protect that memory." |

**Power Move:** Discover the interest → create a creative deal  
*"You keep the property at ₹1 Crore, but I have lifetime access to visit my dog's grave."*

---

### 3.4 ZOPA — Zone of Possible Agreement

```
Founder wants: ₹1 Billion valuation
VC offers:     ₹500 Million

Founder's real minimum (hidden):  ₹800 Million
VC's real maximum (hidden):       ₹600 Million

ZOPA = ₹600M to ₹800M
```

> ZOPA is the **overlap range** where a deal can happen.  
> You find it through **conversation, open questions, and patience.**

---

### 3.5 BATNA — Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement

> **Your BATNA = your walk-away power.**

| Situation | Negotiation Strength |
|---|---|
| You have a strong BATNA (another offer) | Negotiate confidently — you can walk away |
| You have a weak BATNA (2 months of cash left) | Be flexible — don't overplay your hand |

**Key:** Know **your** BATNA and try to understand **theirs**.  
If both parties have strong BATNAs → chance of a deal is lower.  
If one party has a weak BATNA → they are more likely to agree.

---

### 3.6 Real-Life Negotiation Story — Naspers Deal

- **Situation:** Investor (large South African fund) wanted **10% stake** at ₹750M valuation
- **Problem:** Ashwin didn't want to dilute 10% immediately
- **Discovery:** The investor had a **tax reason** to own exactly 10%
- **Creative Solution:** Split the deal into two tranches
  - Tranche 1: Invest now at ₹750M (7% stake)
  - Tranche 2: Invest 1 year later at the new valuation (3% more)
  - The second tranche crossed **₹1 Billion** → Eruditus became a **Unicorn**
- **Result:** Investor got their 10%. Eruditus got more money + unicorn status + less immediate dilution.

> *"They got what they wanted. We got what we wanted. That's a win-win negotiation."*

## 4. Decision-Making & Cognitive Biases

*(Based on HBS — influenced by Professor Neil Barden at IESE)*

---

### 4.1 The Calibration Exercise — Express Ignorance Through a Range

**Exercise:** "How many McDonald's stores are there in the world?"

| Response | Quality |
|---|---|
| "10,000" | ❌ Overconfident — and wrong (~50,000) |
| "0 to 10 million" | ✅ Honest range — correct |

> **Lesson:** The first step to better decisions is knowing what you **don't** know.  
> When you're uncertain → give a wide range. Don't fake precision.

---

### 4.2 Steps to Better Decision-Making

1. **Admit ignorance** — say "I don't know" and give a range
2. **Seek more information** — don't decide on incomplete data
3. **Assemble diverse perspectives** — don't surround yourself with people who all think alike
4. **Consider cognitive load** — better decisions are made **in the morning** (cognitive load increases throughout the day)
5. **Make your best educated choice** — accept that outcomes are uncertain

---

### 4.3 Cognitive Biases — The Mug Experiment

**Setup:** Two perfect mugs for ₹100 vs. Two perfect mugs + one broken mug for ₹100

> Most people choose the **two perfect mugs**, ignoring that both deals give you the same usable value.

**Why?** The **broken mug** triggers a mental "flaw" signal — even though it's irrelevant.

> **Lesson:** Humans are **not rational consumers**. We are driven by perception, not logic.

---

### 4.4 Tylenol (Branded vs. Generic Paracetamol) — Irrational Loyalty

| Scenario | Consumer Behavior |
|---|---|
| Buying paracetamol **for themselves** | Chooses **generic** (cheap, logical) |
| Buying paracetamol **for their child** | Chooses **Tylenol** (brand, expensive) |

> Same drug. Same effect. But **emotional stakes** override rationality.

**Implication for Business:** Build brand trust = command premium pricing  
**Implication for Life:** Recognize when your decisions are driven by emotion, not logic

## 5. The Three Box Framework

*(Vijay Govindarajan, Dartmouth — "The Three Box Solution")*

---

### The Framework

```
BOX 1 (TODAY)         BOX 2 (YESTERDAY/FORGET)      BOX 3 (TOMORROW)
─────────────────     ──────────────────────────     ─────────────────
Current business      Challenge your assumptions     Future you're creating
What's working now    What got you here won't        The innovation you must
Cash cow              get you there                  invest in now
```

---

### Box Definitions

| Box | Focus | Action Required |
|---|---|---|
| **Box 1 — Today** | Current strengths and revenue | **Manage and optimize** |
| **Box 2 — Yesterday** | Old assumptions, habits, mental models | **Challenge and forget** |
| **Box 3 — Tomorrow** | Future vision, disruption, new markets | **Create and invest (min. 20% of time/attention)** |

---

### The High Jump Analogy

| Era | Technique | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Before 1960s | Jump facing forward, land on feet | Limited height |
| After Fosbury Flop (1968) | Jump **backward**, arc over bar, land on back | 1.5 feet higher |

> Dick Fosbury **challenged the assumption** that you must face forward.  
> He trained different muscles (back, neck) — what got others here did NOT get him there.

**That is Box 3 thinking.**

---

### Applying to Real Life

| Context | Box 1 (Today) | Box 2 (Challenge) | Box 3 (Tomorrow) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mid-level Manager** | Domain expertise | "My small network is enough" | Become CEO — build board-level relationships now |
| **Eruditus (Company)** | Online courses for working professionals (35–45) | "We are only an online company" | Foreign university campuses in India for 17–25 year olds |
| **Newspaper** | Print circulation | "Digital can't replace print" | Digital subscription, video, podcasts |
| **You as a Professional** | Current skill set | "What I know is sufficient" | What will the market pay for in 10 years? |

---

### How to Use This Right Now (Practical Exercise)

Take **3 pieces of paper**:

1. **Paper 1 → Box 3:** Write your dream/goal for 10 years from now. Be specific.
2. **Paper 2 → Box 1:** Write what you must do TODAY to reach that future.
3. **Paper 3 → Box 2:** Write the assumptions, habits, and limiting beliefs you must **forget or challenge**.

Review this every week. Audit your progress monthly.

## 6. POCD Framework — Evaluating a Business

*(Bill Sahlman, Harvard Business School — Entrepreneurial Finance)*

```
P — People
O — Opportunity  
C — Context
D — Deal
```

---

### P — People

> *"Exceptional people on a mediocre idea beats mediocre people on an exceptional idea."*

BUT — people alone are not enough. The boat analogy:

> You and a novice rowing **downstream** vs. Olympic rowing team rowing **upstream**  
> → You **win** — not because you're better people, but because you have a better **opportunity**.

---

### O — Opportunity

Two dimensions:
1. **Addressable Market Size** — How big is the total market?
2. **Right to Win** — How much of it can **you** realistically capture?

> *"Go for the aspirin market, not the vitamins market."*  
> Aspirin = urgent need (people will pay now)  
> Vitamins = nice to have (people delay buying)

**Also consider:** Can this opportunity expand into adjacent areas?  
(Groceries → medicines → household goods = expanding S-curve)

---

### C — Context

Context = the external environment that either enables or kills an idea.

| Type of Context | Example |
|---|---|
| **Regulatory** | Indian government allowing foreign universities (2022) → new opportunity created |
| **Technological** | Smaller chips → smartphones → new industries |
| **Aspirational** | Tata Nano failed — people didn't want "cheap," they wanted aspirational |
| **Demographic** | India's youngest population + only 27% college enrollment = massive gap |

> *"Align your business interests with national interests — your chances of success multiply."*

**Counter-example:** Same idea, different context  
- India allows foreign universities → HUGE opportunity (1.4 billion people, 27% enrollment)  
- Singapore allows foreign universities → Smaller opportunity (5 million people, great local universities)

---

### D — Deal

Deal ≠ just valuation. A term sheet has many dimensions:

| Term | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Valuation** | What % you give away for what amount |
| **Liquidation Preference (Liq Pref)** | Investor gets paid first in a sale |
| **Anti-dilution** | Investor's stake is protected if you raise at lower valuation later |
| **Reserved Matters** | List of decisions you cannot make without investor approval |

> **Indian VCs:** Long list of reserved matters  
> **US VCs (e.g., Excel):** Short list — more founder-friendly

> *"On the deal part — most VCs never make money when things go down. Might as well have a good relationship with the founder."*

## 7. Self-Style-Situation (SSS) Framework

*(HBS — Moral Leadership / Leadership Course)*

---

### The Three Elements

| Element | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Self** | Your core values, intrinsic belief system, who you fundamentally are | "I value respect and kindness" |
| **Style** | How you act — your behavioral approach, which should reflect your Self | "I give constructive feedback gently" |
| **Situation** | External circumstances you face | "I must let go of 30% of staff after an acquisition" |

---

### When All Three Align = Golden Period (But You Learn Nothing)

> When Self → Style → Situation all line up perfectly, you are comfortable, confident, and productive — **but you grow the least.**

---

### When They Don't Align = Maximum Learning

| Example | Conflict |
|---|---|
| You value kindness (Self) but must fire someone (Situation) | Style must adapt — learn how to have **difficult conversations** with compassion |
| You're hands-on (Style) but must delegate as CEO (Situation) | Must evolve style to **trust and empower** others |

> **Key Principle:** You never change your **Self** (your values). You evolve your **Style** to navigate new Situations.

---

### Career Implication

> *"Put yourself in situations where your Self, Style, and Situation do NOT align — that is when you will learn the most."*

## 8. Moral Leadership & Dirty Hand Situations

*(HBS — Professor Joe Badaracco)*

### What Are "Dirty Hand" Situations?

> Moral dilemmas where **every choice has a cost** — there is no "clean" option.

**Classic Example:** Truman's Atom Bomb Decision  
- Drop it → 80,000 innocent civilians die immediately  
- Don't drop it → 1,000 American soldiers die **every day**

---

### Two Ethical Frameworks for Such Decisions

| Framework | Origin | Logic |
|---|---|---|
| **Duty-Based (Deontological)** | Kant | "My duty is to protect American lives — I must drop it" |
| **Societal Good (Utilitarian)** | Bentham/Mill | "Maximize good for the most people — don't bomb civilians" |

> **Which you choose depends on your Self — your core value system.**

> *"To thine own self be true, and it must follow, as the night the day, thou canst not be false to any man."* — Shakespeare (Hamlet)

---

### Practical Takeaway

- There is **no universal right answer** in moral dilemmas
- What matters is that your decision **aligns with your values**
- Learn to sit with ambiguity — business is full of dirty hand situations

## 9. Financial Reporting & Learning from Failure

*(HBS — FRC: Financial Reporting and Control)*

### The HBS Approach: Learn from Autopsies

> Instead of studying successful companies → **study financial frauds and collapses**

**Cases studied:**
- **Enron** — Off-balance-sheet manipulation, SPE fraud
- **WorldCom** — ₹$11B accounting fraud through expense capitalization
- **Theranos** — Blood testing fraud, overstated capabilities

---

### Why Study Failure?

> *"Failure is actually the best teacher."*

- Like the human body — you don't fix your health until something hurts
- **Stanford Professor:** "Given a first-time entrepreneur vs. a second-time entrepreneur whose first venture failed — I invest in the second one, 10 out of 10 times."

**Failure teaches:**
1. What NOT to do (hard to learn from books)
2. Resilience and resourcefulness
3. Humility and accurate self-assessment

## 10. The Harvard Case Study Method

### How It Works

| Day | Volume |
|---|---|
| Mon / Wed / Fri | **3 case studies** (~120 pages of reading) |
| Tue / Thu | **2 case studies** (~80–100 pages) |
| Per year | ~500 cases |
| Two years total | ~1,000 cases |

**No pre-given questions.** No "right" answer. You walk in prepared — or you get cold-called.

---

### The Cold Call

> Professor picks a random student and says:  
> *"You are the CEO of this company. Their sales are dropping. Supply chain is broken. What do you do?"*

**Purpose:**
- Forces **deep reading** of every case
- Simulates real business: **you always have 100 things going on** and must choose which 5 to prioritize
- Trains prioritization, confidence under pressure, and structured thinking under uncertainty

---

### The Gaming Strategy (Ashwin's Story)

> Trick: Speak **first** in a class. Once you've contributed, the professor targets those who haven't spoken.

Ashwin tracked participation in an **Excel sheet** by subject.  
One day, Professor Andy McAfee pulled out a **random number generator** and called him despite three prior contributions.

**Lesson:** There's no permanent shortcut. Be prepared.

---

### How Grades Work

- **~50% of your grade = class participation**
- Insights matter more than just reading out facts from the case
- Professor Kustow Rangan's feedback to Ashwin: *"You point to the exhibit. A Harvard MBA should synthesize across exhibits and derive new insights."*

## 11. Career Audit — 5 Key Life Questions

*(Ashwin's Framework for Mid-Career Professionals)*

---

### Question 1: Define Happiness and Success — For Yourself

> Before auditing anything else, answer this honestly:  
> **What does success mean to YOU?**

| Option | Implication |
|---|---|
| Financial wealth | Pursue high-ROI career moves, startups, investments |
| Fame and legacy | Build public profile, write, speak, create |
| Family and balance | Optimize for stability, not maximum income |
| Impact and purpose | Choose mission-driven organizations even at lower pay |

> *"If you're very clear about what happiness is, your choices become obvious."*

---

### Question 2: Is Your Career Destination Clear?

> *"First figure out which direction you want to run. Then run fast. Otherwise you may be going further from your goal."*

**Exercise:** Name one concrete goal for 10 years from now.  
Then ask: "Who hires people for that role? Do those people know I exist?"

---

### Question 3: Are You Investing in the Right Things?

If you're a **salaried professional:**
- Invest in **upskilling**
- Invest in **social capital** (weak ties especially)
- Invest in **personal brand** (does the CEO know you exist?)

> *"You may be the best performer. But if the CEO doesn't know your name, you'll grow 12% year on year — same as everyone else."*

---

### Question 4: Is Entrepreneurship the Right Path for You?

| Wrong Reason to Start Up | Right Reason to Start Up |
|---|---|
| "I'll become a billionaire" | "I deeply want to solve this problem" |
| "I'll have better work-life balance" | "I care about this mission" |
| "I saw someone else succeed" | "I have insight others don't" |

**Data Check:**
- India has ~7 lakh registered startups (DPIIT)
- Only ~120–150 are unicorns
- Odds: **1 in ~4,000–5,000**
- Of 250+ unicorn founders → only **3 are college dropouts**

---

### Question 5: Are You Forgetting and Challenging Old Assumptions?

Ask yourself:
- What habit or belief is holding me back?
- Am I staying in my comfort zone (Box 1)?
- What would I need to learn or unlearn to become who I want to be?

## 12. Indian Education Landscape & Global Opportunity

### Key Statistics

| Metric | India | US | UK | China |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Students finishing high school/year | **11 million** | — | 60M equivalent | — |
| Gross Enrollment Ratio (college) | **27%** | 80% | 60% | 40% |
| Universities in Global Top 100 | **0** | Many | Many | Few |
| Universities in Global Top 1000 | ~10–12 | Many | Many | Several |

> Out of 11 million finishing school → only 2.7 million go to college  
> Of those, the top 1 million are the "crème de la crème"  
> Only ~200,000 seats in top 1,000 global institutions in India  
> → **Gap of 800,000 high-quality students** with nowhere to go → Brain Drain

- World-class **social capital** and **brand capital**
- Students who studied oceanography end up at Google — because of **network + brand**, not subject matter

### India Campus vs. Abroad — Comparison

| Factor | Study Abroad | Foreign University in India |
|---|---|---|

## 13. Why Indians Lead Global Organizations

### The Chaos Advantage

> India is a **constrained economy** — resources are scarce, infrastructure is inconsistent, rules are often unclear.

Growing up in this environment builds:

| Trait | How It Develops |
|---|---|
| **Problem-solving instinct** | Traffic jams → figure out the fastest alternate route |
| **Never-take-no-for-an-answer mindset** | Sold-out cinema → wait for a spare ticket |
| **Resourcefulness** | A door half-shut → job is to open it |

> *"When we see a constraint, we say: my job is to solve that constraint. That is leadership. That is business."*

---

### Language and Interpersonal Skills

- India is a **heterogeneous society** — multiple languages, religions, cultures under one roof
- Indians are trained from birth to **adapt, communicate across differences, and build bridges**
- This is extremely valuable **working abroad** with diverse global teams

> *"Homogeneity is a killer of entrepreneurship and innovation."*

---

### Data on Indian Academic Leadership

- Indians and Chinese make up the #1 and #2 groups of international academics in the US
- Business school deans across Kellogg, Harvard (Nitin Nohria) and others have been Indian
- Head of US National Science Foundation — Indian
- Why? Indians are strong in **research, teaching, AND administration** — rare combination
