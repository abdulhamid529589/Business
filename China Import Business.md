## 1. The Foundation — Mindset & Core Philosophy

### Import-Export Is Eternal
> "Import business will exist until death. As long as the world exists, import-export will exist — because what you have in shortage, you bring from outside; what you have in surplus, you send outside. You cannot stop this."

This is not a trend — it is the **backbone of the global economy.** Every country, every era, every civilization has traded.

### The "How Can I Do This?" Thinking Pattern
The single most important mindset shift:

| Old Thinking | New Thinking |
|-------------|-------------|
| "This is difficult / impossible" | "How can I do this?" |
| "It can't be done" | "What way exists to do it?" |
| "I don't have enough" | "What can I do with what I have?" |

**Michael Jordan Story:**
His father gave him a T-shirt: "Sell this for $10." He sold it. Then: "Sell for $20." He drew a Mickey Mouse cartoon on it, stood outside a kindergarten at dismissal time, yelled "Mickey Mouse $20!" — sold it. Then: "Sell for $100."

Most people say impossible. Jordan said "Give me time."

He waited for a visiting Indian cricket legend (Sachin Tendulkar equivalent) visiting America. Got through security during a road block. Got an autograph. Sold it for $500 the next day.

**The lesson:** When you train your thinking to ask "How CAN I do this?" — you will always find a way. The size of your dream determines the size of your achievement.

### The Excuse Problem
> "We give ourselves excuses for our inabilities. We call something 'difficult' and use that as a comfort — 'I tried, but it's just hard.' That is an excuse, not a reason."

The difference between difficulty and impossibility:
- **Difficult** = needs time, effort, learning, adjustments
- **Impossible** = does not exist in import-export if you keep trying

### Warren Buffett's Rule
> "If you cannot do a business for 10 years, don't give it even 10 minutes. Those 10 minutes will be wasted."

**Implication:** Enter import business only if you are committed for the long haul. Otherwise don't start — your half-hearted attempt wastes capital and time.

### The Blood Circulation Analogy
Money in business = Blood in the body.

- More blood circulation = healthier body
- More money rolling (turning over) = healthier business
- **The product you can SELL is the best product** — because it keeps your capital rolling

A product sitting unsold is like blocked circulation — the body (business) suffers.

## 2. Who Should NOT Enter Import Business

### ❌ Those Who Expect Others to Sell for Them
> "Who says 'tell me the product, you sell it for me.' Selling is like hunger — if YOUR stomach is hungry, I cannot eat for you. Only you can eat for yourself."

No mentor, trainer, or consultant can sell YOUR product for you. They can teach the skill. The execution is yours.

### ❌ Those Looking for "Magic Products"
No product in the world sells automatically. **Every product requires selling.** Even the best product needs:
- A market
- A seller
- A buyer
- A sales process

> "This table didn't come here automatically. Someone brought it. You didn't come here automatically. Someone invited you. Nothing happens on its own."

### ❌ Those Who Think Import is Easy
Moving from local retail/wholesale to international import is moving "outside the box." **Everything outside your comfort zone is always more difficult at first** — but never impossible.

Going from cold to hot makes people sick temporarily. Entering a new field causes disorientation temporarily. Push through.

### ❌ Those Seeking Short-Term Gains
Warren Buffett's rule applies: if you can't do it for 10 years, don't start. The market rewards persistence. One good seasonal opportunity (like the onion price spike story) can compensate for years of modest returns — but only if you've stayed in the game long enough.

## 3. Capital — How Much Do You Really Need?

### For a "Complete" Importer (Scale Reference)
| Requirement | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| Capital needed | ~2 Crore BDT |
| Container structure | 4 containers running simultaneously |
| Container 1 | Being sold in the market |
| Container 2 | In hand (warehouse) |
| Container 3 | At sea, in transit |
| Container 4 | Being manufactured/prepared at factory |

This is the **goal state** — not the starting requirement.

### For Absolute Beginners — Start With What You Have

| Capital Available | What You Can Do |
|-----------------|----------------|
| ৳10,000 | 1 small product in small quantity |
| ৳25,000 | ~1,000 pieces of a low-cost item (e.g., earphones at ৳25/piece) |
| ৳50,000 | Small product, multiple SKUs |
| ৳1 Lakh | Small container share or door-to-door |
| ৳2 Lakh | LCL shipment |
| ৳5 Lakh+ | Approaching FCL territory |

**The ৳25,000 Example:**
- Buy 1,000 earphones at ৳25/piece from China
- Sell at ৳100–৳150 in Bangladesh
- Revenue: ৳1,00,000–৳1,50,000
- Profit: ৳75,000–৳1,25,000
- **Reinvest. Split into 2 batches. Then 3. Then 4. Capital grows.**

### The Growth Formula
```
Step 1: ৳25,000 → buy product → sell → ৳30,000
Step 2: Split ৳30,000 into 2 batches of ৳15,000 each
Step 3: Sell batch 1: ৳15K → ৳20K | Sell batch 2: ৳15K → ৳20K
Step 4: Now you have ৳40,000 with better supply chain
Step 5: Split into 3 batches → supply chain never breaks
Step 6: Gradually add product #2, then #3...
Step 7: Eventually reach full container capacity
```

**The key rule:** **Never let your supply chain break.** Split capital to ensure you always have product available. Supply chain breaks → customers find other suppliers → you lose them permanently.

## 4. Product Selection — The Right Framework

### The Golden Rule
> "The product YOU can sell is the best product — because selling keeps your capital rolling."

If you have an existing business → import what you already sell.
If you're starting fresh → use the framework below.

### The 4 Questions Before Selecting Any Product

| # | Question | Why It Matters |
|---|----------|----------------|
| 1 | **Can I understand this product?** | If you don't understand it, you can't source or sell it well |
| 2 | **Will this benefit me financially?** | Margin must justify the complexity |
| 3 | **Can I put in the work for this product?** | Lazy effort = poor results |
| 4 | **Do I love this product/business?** | When you love it, you work at 3 AM and still feel motivated |

### The Love Test (Real Example)
Two identical people standing in the rain at 3 AM waiting for a goods delivery with no vehicle available:

**Person A (No love for business):**
- Gets frustrated, throws his bag, swears at the business, says "I'll never do business again"
- Quits mentally that night

**Person B (Loves the business):**
- Notices how many people are on the streets at 3 AM (interesting!)
- Dhaka roads are actually safe at night (learning!)
- Does the Fajr prayer with gratitude
- Goes to sleep with a smile
- Wakes up ready to go again

**Same situation. Completely different outcome.** The differentiator is love for what you do.

### Best Product Characteristics for Beginners

| Characteristic | Ideal | Why |
|---------------|-------|-----|
| Perishable? | NO | Time pressure → panic selling → price crashes |
| Expiry date? | Long or none | No pressure to liquidate fast |
| Does value increase over time? | YES ideally | Iron, copper, certain metals gain value when stored |
| Market depth | Wide | Easier to find buyers |
| Competition level | Moderate | Not oversaturated, not too niche |

**Example of ideal beginner product:** Iron/steel goods. "Store it — it doesn't rot, doesn't melt, price rarely falls, usually rises."

### Categories That Work Well for Bangladesh Beginners

| Category | Why It Works | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| **Gadgets / Electronics** | Young generation demand is growing rapidly | Trend-based — enter early, exit before saturation |
| **Cookware / Kitchen items** | Non-perishable, always needed, design changes drive upgrades | Modern cookware (non-stick, etc.) constantly evolving |
| **Children's toys** | Emotional purchase (parents buy for love, not price) | Parents pay premium; product "breaks" = no warranty complaint |
| **Showpieces / Décor** | Non-perishable, value-added product | Good margin if you target the right sub-market |

### The Gadget Product Lifecycle Warning
> "When a new gadget enters Bangladesh (e.g., Bluetooth speaker), it sells for ৳2,000. China buying price: ৳100. Huge profit. But as more importers enter, price crashes to ৳200-৳500. The 18-month window is everything."

**Strategy:** Be **first** with new gadgets, not last. Exit before saturation.

## 5. Product Research — Online & Offline Methods

### Offline Research (The Market Walk)

**How to do it properly:**
1. Go to the relevant wholesale market (see Section 6)
2. Visit 3 days/week: e.g., Sun, Mon, Wed mornings
3. Stand patiently in shops — observe who buys what
4. Note which products are in every shop vs. only some shops
5. **The insight:** Shop owners pay rent per inch of space. **They will NOT stock a slow-moving product.** If every shop has it → it sells.
6. Ask retail prices. Ask wholesale prices. (Use the strategy below.)

**The Price Research Trick:**
- Watch someone buy a product → note the price → then approach dealer
- Ask for 1,000 pieces at lower price (say ৳60 when retail is ৳82)
- Dealer says no? That's fine — you learned the wholesale structure
- Dealer says YES? Buy carefully — this is also market research with a cost
- **Key:** Ask low enough that they won't give it, but not so low they throw you out. Match with humor.

> "I accidentally priced myself into a purchase once — asked ৳200 less and the dealer said yes. Lesson: Make it unambiguously low so they decline, but politely."

**Market signals:**
- Product in every shop near the front = high sales velocity
- Product at back or only in some shops = slow mover
- "Market speaks to you if you know how to listen"

### Online Research

**Platforms to check:**
| Platform | Use Case |
|----------|----------|
| **Alibaba** | World's largest B2B platform — source products globally |
| **Daraz (Bangladesh)** | Check what sells locally online |
| **Facebook Ads** | See which products are being actively advertised |
| **Digital marketing tools** | Track which FB ads get most engagement/sales |
| **ChatGPT / Gemini / DeepSeek** | Ask step-by-step research process questions |

**AI Research Prompt:**
> "Go to ChatGPT/Gemini and ask: 'I want to know which products are selling most via Facebook Ads — how do I find this?' It will give you step-by-step instructions."

Young generation especially can leverage AI + FB Ad libraries to identify trending products before they saturate.

## 7. Five Ways to Import (Without a Full Container)

Not everyone can fill a 40-foot container. Here are 5 legitimate methods, smallest to largest:

| Method | Scale | Best For |
|--------|-------|----------|
| **1. Luggage / Personal carry** | Tiny (kg scale) | Travelers bringing goods personally; no LC needed |
| **2. Postal service** | Small (parcel scale) | Low-value, light items; standard international mail |
| **3. Courier (DHL, FedEx, TNT, EMS)** | Small-medium | Fast delivery; higher cost; good for samples/small orders |
| **4. Door-to-Door service** | Medium | Company in China + company in Bangladesh handle everything; LCL included |
| **5. Full LC / Container (FCL)** | Large | Established importers; lowest cost per unit |

**For beginners:** Start with methods 1–4. Move to FCL only when capital and market understanding justify it.

### FCL vs. LCL

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **FCL (Full Container Load)** | You book and fill the entire container; all goods are yours |
| **LCL (Less than Container Load)** | Your goods share container space with other shippers |
| **CBM (Cubic Meter)** | Volume measurement for LCL cargo |

**LCL example:** You have 2 CBM of goods → you pay for 2 CBM → rest of container filled by other shippers. This is how small importers access sea freight affordably.

## 8. Common Mistakes New Importers Make

### Mistake #1 — Buying Based on Price Difference Alone

**The classic trap:**
- See a microphone in Bangladesh: ৳5,400
- See same microphone on Alibaba: ৳2,700
- Think: "Easy ৳2,700 profit!"

**What they forget:**
- China inland transport (factory → port)
- Export customs at origin port
- Sea freight cost
- Insurance (marine cargo)
- Bangladesh customs duty (varies 37%–105% by product)
- Port handling charges
- C&F (Clearing & Forwarding) agent fee
- Local transport (port → warehouse)
- Miscellaneous expenses

**Result:** ৳2,700 product + ৳250 tax = ৳2,950 landed cost. Bangladesh market price: ৳2,400. **You're already in loss before even opening the box.**

> "YouTube shows buying price and selling price. They never show the middle. That's where fortunes are lost."

### Mistake #2 — Ignoring the Time Factor

- Buy product in China → product arrives 40 days later
- Meanwhile, market may have changed
- With same capital in a different product, you could have turned over **twice** in 40 days
- **Always calculate: money × turns/year, not just margin per sale**

### Mistake #3 — Buying Too Much After First Success

**The classic pattern:**
1. Buy 15-20 units → sell at good profit ✅
2. Get excited → buy 500+ units of same product
3. Market is now saturated (everyone wants that product)
4. You can't sell → capital stuck
5. Forced to sell below cost to recover cash

> "I imported industrial electrical components (IGBT, magnetic contactors, limit switches, inverters). First small batch: huge profit. Then bought a large quantity. The same buyer who bought before now knew I had excess — stopped buying. Forced to sell at cost or less. Had to borrow to meet obligations."

**The Rule:** Test with small quantities. Only scale when you have **confirmed, recurring demand** — not just first-sale excitement.

### Mistake #4 — Not Understanding Incoterms

**Real damage scenario:**
Supplier quotes "EXW" (Ex-Works). New importer thinks this means delivered to their door.

**EXW actually means:**
- Price covers goods at factory gate only
- You pay for: factory → origin port truck, export customs, sea freight, destination port charges, Bangladesh customs, local delivery

**Miss this and you could lose ৳5 Lakh+ on a single shipment** just from unexpected logistics costs.

**Key Incoterms to know:**

| Incoterm | What Seller Covers | What Buyer Pays |
|----------|------------------|----------------|
| **EXW** | Nothing (factory gate only) | Everything |
| **FOB** | Factory → origin port loading | Sea freight onward + all destination costs |
| **CIF** | Factory + sea freight + insurance | Destination customs + local delivery |
| **DDP** | Everything to your door | Nothing (most expensive seller price) |

> "EXW written in a contract, if you don't know what it means, can cost you millions."

### Mistake #5 — Giving Away Too Much Business Information

**The sourcing exposure risk:**
- You give supplier's packing list to a buyer
- Packing list shows: source factory, cost price, volume
- Buyer contacts factory directly, cuts you out
- OR: buyer sets up his nephew in the same business using YOUR sourced supplier

**Rule:** Never share packing lists, factory contacts, or cost details with buyers. Your sourcing intelligence is your competitive advantage.

## 9. Tax Evasion Methods in Bangladesh (Know to Protect Yourself)

> ⚠️ **Important:** This section is documented so honest businesspeople understand why they face unfair competition, and what illegal practices exist. **Do not replicate any of these.**

### Method 1 — HS Code Manipulation
**What it is:** Changing the HS code of a product to one with lower duty.

**How:**
- Motor for home use: **67% tax**
- Same motor declared as industrial: **37% tax**
- Same motor declared as agricultural: **1% tax**

**How they do it:** Pre-arrange with a corrupt customs officer (bribe upfront). Submit documents with the wrong, lower-tax HS code. Officer "doesn't notice."

**Current status:** Government is tightening this. Electronic filing makes it harder. But still happens, especially with "influential" connections.

### Method 2 — Weight Under-Declaration
**What it is:** Declaring a container weighs less than it actually does.

**Example:**
- Container: 26 tons
- Declared: 23 tons
- Tax paid on 23 tons instead of 26
- The weighing station gets a "bundle" (bribe) to write the lower number

### Method 3 — Product Concealment / Substitution
**What it is:** Importing a high-duty product hidden inside or labeled as a low-duty product.

**Example:**
- Stainless steel mug (duty: 65%) → declared as ceramic mug (duty: 10%)
- Customs officer sees "ceramic" on documents → waves it through (sometimes with bribe)

**Real case:** Casino products (gambling items) were seized in Bangladesh — they didn't come through customs declared as casino items. They were hidden inside other product declarations.

### Method 4 — Invoice Undervaluation
**What it is:** Declaring purchase price lower than actual to reduce the taxable base.

**Example:**
- Actual purchase: ৳100/unit
- Declared: ৳20/unit
- Tax base: ৳20 instead of ৳100 → massive tax saving

**Why it partially works:** China produces everything from ultra-cheap to premium quality. A ৳20 item and a ৳100 item can look identical. Customs officers are confused about "true" value. Government has set **Minimum Load Values (MLV)** on 2,000+ products to combat this.

### Method 5 — Bonded Warehouse Misuse
**What it is:** Importers register to bring goods under "bonded" status (no tax) by declaring it's for re-export after value addition.

**The fraud:**
- Legitimately export 1 container (to maintain appearance)
- Smuggle 9 containers into local market tax-free
- Undercut all honest competitors who paid full duties

### Government Response (Positive Trends)
- Digitalization is reducing fraud (all records stored electronically)
- MLV (Minimum Load Values) set on 2,000+ products
- License cancellations for violators increasing
- Imprisonment of major fraudsters
- Customs commissioners actively improving culture

> "The more digital Bangladesh becomes, the less cheating will happen. We want full digitalization of this sector."

## 10. Market Strategy — Main Market vs. Side Market

### The Core Problem with Main Markets (Chokbazar)
When you bring a new product to Chokbazar or any major wholesale hub, you face established players who:
- Know you're new
- Know how much stock you have
- Can price below your cost temporarily (they have reserves) to drive you out
- Then raise prices once you're gone

**This is a documented predatory pricing practice**, not theory.

### The Reel Method (Counter-Strategy)
When a big player tries to undercut you:
1. Accept their lower price as market price
2. **Announce your stock is sold** (even if it isn't — or your warehouse elsewhere is your "stock")
3. Visit multiple shops, say "sorry, sold out"
4. Build relationships everywhere during this "sold out" tour
5. Wait 3-4 days
6. A buyer approaches you: "I heard you have this product?"
7. "Yes, but it's gone now. Need another shipment. 5 days."
8. They say: "I'll pay more — I need it in 5 days"
9. Arrange from your actual stock or expedite
10. **Price recovers. You sell at profit.**

> "This is wisdom (hikmah) — not lying. You said 'my stock is sold.' Your warehouse stock IS somewhere. Your visible stock is gone. There's a difference."

### The Side Market Advantage

Instead of fighting Chokbazar's big players, distribute to:
- Savar
- Noakhali
- Brahmanbaria
- Narayanganj
- Kishoreganj
- Rajshahi
- Khulna

**Why this works:**
- Big players can't be everywhere simultaneously
- You can dominate a regional market of 20 buyers more easily than compete with 10 giants in Chokbazar
- Once you grow big enough, big players come to YOU for tea — they want to control you, not eliminate you

> "When the moon rises, everyone sees it. When fire lights on a mountain, everyone sees it. When you grow big enough — the big players will come for tea. Then you can choose how to deal with them."

### The Bongsal Extortion Case (Motor Parts)
A man imported motor parts from Japan. Brought them back, announced them in Bongsal (motor parts hub).

A politically connected businessman who controlled that market called him in:
"Give me the goods. I do this business."
Importer refused.

Days later: gang of men attacked his shop, dragged him to a "club," demanded ৳56 Lakh "market entry fee" for a ৳56 Lakh shipment.

Result: He "sold" ৳56L worth of goods for ৳40L. Got a post-dated check for the rest. Received ৳16L over 2 years in installments. Was too scared to go to his shop for months.

**Lesson:** Main market entry can expose you to criminal-adjacent business practices. Side markets protect you until you're too big to be touched.

### The Sourcing Theft Story (Connector Parts)
A new importer brought 50,000 pieces of copper connectors (market rate: ৳48/piece). Tried to sell in main market. Dealers realized he had excess stock and low-balled him.

Enamul advised him: "Tell everyone your stock is sold. Visit all shops. Build relationships."

He did. Price stabilized. A buyer came: "Do you have connectors?" → "Just sold out, need 7 days."

Buyer said: "I need 10,000 pieces urgently. Pay premium." New order placed by air freight. Connectors arrived. Market was in short supply. **Sold at massive premium.**

### The Curtain Example — The Controlled Market Entry
A new importer (call him Nafis) brings curtain fabric. Landed cost: ৳150/meter.

An established player (call him me) knows Nafis's cost is ৳150. I deliberately sell at ৳140/meter — taking ৳10/meter loss — to make Nafis unable to sell.

Nafis can't move stock. Months pass. I approach him: "Nafis bhai, I have a buyer in Chittagong who needs curtains. ৳145/meter, cash today." Nafis agrees (better than rotting stock). I buy ALL of Nafis's stock.

Next day, I raise price to ৳160. Then ৳180. Then ৳200. Then ৳250. Market is now short supply (only my stock exists). I recover all my loss and make massive profit.

**Nafis's mistake:** Not distributing to side markets. Not protecting sourcing information. Not holding his stock longer.

## 12. Scams When Importing from China

### Protecting Yourself on Chinese B2B Platforms

**Verification checklist before ordering:**

| Check | Safe Signal |
|-------|------------|
| Company age | 5+ years preferred |
| Response rate | High (90%+) |
| Verified badge | Present |
| Company type | **Manufacturer** (not just trader) |
| Payment destination | **Company bank account** (NOT personal) |

> "If money goes to a company account in China, it cannot be withdrawn like personal money. It requires auditing and justification. Scam risk is near zero. Personal account = run away."

### Type 1 — Fake/Poor Quality Products
- You order Grade A, receive Grade C
- Prevention: **PSI (Pre-Shipment Inspection)** — have a 3rd party (SGS, Bureau Veritas) inspect goods AT the factory before shipping

### Type 2 — Delivery Delays
- Promised: 15 days. Actual: 60+ days
- Perishable goods spoil. Market opportunity passes
- Prevention: Contractual delivery terms + liquidated damages clause

### Type 3 — Covert Price Increases Post-Order
- Goods arrive at port; agent calls: "Tax went up, need extra ৳X"
- You're now trapped — goods stuck at port (storage fees accruing daily)
- Your agent is taking a cut from you AND the customs process
- Prevention: Only work with trusted C&F agents. Get full cost disclosure upfront.

### Type 4 — Sourcing Information Theft
- Your C&F agent or buyer gets your packing list
- They see your supplier, product, and cost
- They contact your supplier directly
- OR set up a competing business using YOUR sourced product
- Prevention: Never share packing lists showing supplier details

### Type 5 — Agent-Assisted Market Flooding
- Your importer-agent brings your goods to port
- Delays delivery by 1 day
- In that 1 day, releases his OWN stock of the same product
- He prices low, floods market
- Your goods arrive next day to a crashed market
- You sell at loss. He sold at profit.
- Prevention: Use agents with no competing business interests

### Type 6 — Small Trader Scam (Ghost Office)
- A small trading company (not manufacturer)
- Office value: ৳2 Lakh
- You gave them ৳50 Lakh advance
- They disappear with ৳48 Lakh net profit
- Prevention: Always verify factory existence. Visit physically or use PSI agents.

### PSI — Pre-Shipment Inspection
**What is it:** Before your goods are loaded on the ship, an independent inspector (SGS, Bureau Veritas, etc.) physically checks:
- Product quality matches specification
- Quantity matches order
- Packing is correct
- No defects in sample units

**When to use it:** For large orders, first-time suppliers, or when you can't visit personally.

**Cost:** Varies by location and quantity — typically a few hundred USD. Worth every dollar on significant orders.

## 13. Pricing Strategy — The Formula

### The Two Concepts: Costing vs. Pricing

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Costing** | Total cost to get the product to your hands in Bangladesh |
| **Pricing** | What you sell it for in the market |

**You cannot price correctly without costing correctly first.**

### Complete Costing Formula

```
COSTING = Product purchase price
        + China inland transport (factory → origin port)
        + Export fees at origin port
        + Sea/air freight
        + Marine insurance
        + Bangladesh customs duties & taxes
        + C&F agent fees
        + Port handling charges
        + Bangladesh local transport (port → warehouse)
        + Miscellaneous expenses (2-5% buffer)
        ─────────────────────────────────────────────
        = LANDED COST (true cost per unit in Bangladesh)
```

### Pricing Formula

```
PRICE = LANDED COST × 1.25 (minimum 25% gross margin)

Then check:
- Is this price below market? → You win customers
- Is this price at market? → Acceptable, compete on service/relationship
- Is this price above market? → Investigate why; adjust sourcing or reduce cost
```

**The standard global practice:** 25% gross margin minimum. Below this, your fixed costs, bad debt, waste, and slow-moving stock will eat your profit.

**Government intervention note:** For essential goods (rice, oil, onion, etc.), the government can cap prices and conduct raids on over-pricing. Know which products are "essential" — they have price controls.

### Advanced Pricing (When You Have Market Dominance)
- If you're the only supplier of a product → price can be much higher
- If short supply situation arises → price can spike dramatically (see the onion story below)
- **Free market = you can charge whatever buyers will pay**, as long as no price controls apply

### The Onion Story (Patience + Timing = Wealth)

An entrepreneur imported onions from Egypt for 3 years:
- Year 1: Broke even
- Year 2: Modest profit
- Year 3: Took a loss on third batch

Egyptian supplier called: "Do you want onions this year? I'll give you better price — how many containers?"

He said 200 containers (confident). Egyptian supplier asked for LC. He had NO MONEY — banks refused loans, friends refused.

Egyptian supplier FLEW to Dhaka, met him, said: "Send even ৳100-200 as TT (Telegraphic Transfer). I'll ship. Sell and send me cash."

He got goods on consignment (no upfront payment). Sold. Sent cash back. Cycle repeated.

**That year:** Onion prices in Bangladesh hit ৳90/kg (normal price: ৳20-25/kg). He was the only large supplier with stock. **Hundreds of crores in profit from a single season.**

**How?**
1. He stayed in the business through 3 losing/breakeven years
2. Built a genuine relationship with the Egyptian supplier
3. The supplier trusted him enough to ship on credit
4. Market timing aligned → he had product when no one else did

> "Stick around long enough. One big wave will come. When it comes, ride it as far as possible."

### The 7 Licenses for a Complete Importer/Exporter

| # | License | Issuing Authority | For |
|---|---------|------------------|-----|
| 1 | **Trade License** | Local municipality/city corporation | Legal business identity |

### Can You Start Without All 7?
**Yes — for small beginnings:**
- Buy products via door-to-door service, postal, courier, or luggage = **no license needed**
- Join 4-5 local traders to pool an order under their licenses = **no license needed for you**
- Risk: You depend on others; chance of being cheated increases

**The advice:** Start small (even without full licenses) to learn the market. Build licenses as capital and confidence grow.

### Cost Reality Check

Always verify government fee schedules directly on official websites before going through agents.

## 15. Customs Clearance — How It Works

### The Simple Reality
**If you declare exactly what you import, customs clearance is straightforward.** Any educated person with the right documents can do it.

1. Submit documents to customs (or through C&F agent)
2. Customs officer assesses duty based on HS code and declared value
3. You deposit tax amount at designated bank
4. Goods released and delivered

**It is only "complicated" for those who:**
- Are trying to cheat on HS code
- Want to undervalue goods
- Have something to hide

### The Role of a C&F Agent
**C&F = Clearing and Forwarding Agent**

The C&F agent is to customs what a lawyer is to a court — they navigate the complex paperwork on your behalf.

**A good C&F agent:**
- Takes your documents
- Handles all customs correspondence
- Tells you: "Deposit ৳X in tax" (single call)
- Delivers goods to your warehouse

**A bad C&F agent:**
- Creates drama: "Your goods are stuck", "Customs wants more", "Examination ordered", "Intelligence unit involved"
- Uses fear to extract extra money from you
- May be stealing by making up "problems" that need "resolving"

**Choosing a C&F agent:**
- Get referrals from established importers in your product category
- Each sector (textile, electronics, industrial goods) has specialized C&F agents
- Test with small shipments first

### HS Code — What You Must Know

**HS Code = Harmonized System Code** (international product classification)

- Every product in the world has a unique HS code
- Tax rate in Bangladesh depends entirely on the HS code
- Different HS codes for similar products can have wildly different duties
- **Google: "[Product name] HS code Bangladesh" → instant result**

**Duty structure example (Motor):**
| Motor Use | Tax Rate |
|-----------|---------|
| Household / residential | 67% |
| Industrial | 37% |
| Agricultural | 1% |

Same physical motor — different HS code = different tax. This is both a legitimate business consideration and a manipulation opportunity (hence the fraud section earlier).

## 17. Selling Your Imported Products in Bangladesh

### Online Method

**Minimum required:**
1. A Facebook Page (product posts, reels, live demos)
2. A YouTube Channel (product videos, how-to content)

**Optional but useful:**
- LinkedIn profile (B2B sales)

**Selling process:**
1. Post product content regularly (photos, videos, demos)
2. Run sponsored Facebook/Instagram ads (boost posts)
3. Customers see ad → message page → place order
4. **No physical shop needed** → deliver via courier
5. Courier collects cash from customer, brings to you (COD system)

> "You can sleep at home, no shop required, product orders come in, courier delivers and brings back the money."

**Warning on digital marketing:** Many people pay ৳1,000–৳3,000 for page setup and then get cheated on Facebook ad spending (fake dollar accounts, no actual boosting). Find reputable digital marketing freelancers with verified references.

### Offline Method

**The side market distribution strategy:**
1. Go to wholesale markets of SMALLER cities/areas (not Chokbazar/main hubs)
2. Visit shops in: Savar, Noakhali, Brahmanbaria, Narayanganj, Kishoreganj, Khulna
3. Introduce yourself, show samples, leave price list
4. Build relationships — small buyers become loyal buyers
5. Over time, you become the **dominant supplier** in those areas
6. Large Dhaka traders cannot serve them as well as you (distance, relationships)

**The compound effect:**
- 20 regional buyers × 2 orders/month each = 40 shipments/month
- Each shipment small by Dhaka standards, but collectively significant
- No predatory pricing competition from big players

## 19. The Step-by-Step Summary for New Importers

### The Complete Roadmap

```
PHASE 1: LEARN (Before spending any money)
├── Study import process basics (YouTube, podcasts, this guide)
├── Research 2-3 products you're genuinely interested in
├── Visit relevant Bangladesh wholesale markets 3x/week
├── Talk to traders, ask questions, observe patterns
└── Use AI tools to research online demand signals

PHASE 2: MICRO-TEST (₳10,000–₳50,000)
├── Pick ONE non-perishable product with good demand signals
├── Order smallest quantity possible via:
│   ├── Door-to-door service, OR
│   ├── Postal/courier, OR
│   └── Piggyback with another trader's license
├── Sell through Facebook, WhatsApp, local markets
├── Note: what sold fast? what didn't? why?
└── Document everything — customer types, price sensitivity, timing

PHASE 3: FORMALIZE (Once selling consistently)
├── Get Trade License
├── Get TIN Certificate
├── Open dedicated business bank account
├── Join Chamber of Commerce
├── Apply for IRC (Import Registration Certificate)
├── Apply for ERC (Export Registration Certificate)  
├── Get BIN (VAT registration)
└── Find a reputable C&F agent in your product sector

PHASE 4: SCALE (With growing capital)
├── Move from courier/door-to-door to LCL shipments
├── Split capital into 3-4 batches (supply chain never breaks)
├── Add 1 new product when first product is stable
├── Distribute to side markets (regional cities, not just Dhaka main hubs)
├── Build supplier relationship in China (visit when ready)
└── Gradually build toward FCL (full container) capacity

PHASE 5: OPTIMIZE (Established importer)
├── Negotiate better terms with proven suppliers
├── Use LC (Letter of Credit) for larger, higher-trust orders
├── Explore multiple source countries
├── Build brand / exclusive supply arrangements
├── Consider re-export opportunities (like Walton model)
└── Reinvest aggressively — compound the capital
```
