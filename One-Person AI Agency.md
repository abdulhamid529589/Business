## 4. Setting Up Claude Code + AI Operating System

### What Is Claude Code

Claude Code is an AI agent that runs in your terminal, inside a code editor (VS Code). It does 90-95% of the technical work for you. You don't need to write code.

### Setup Steps

**Step 1: Install Visual Studio Code**
- Search "Visual Studio Code" → download for Mac or Windows
- This is your code editor (you won't actually write code in it — Claude does)

**Step 2: Create your workspace folder**
- Create a folder on your desktop (e.g., "shiny" — your agency name)
- This is where your entire AI operating system lives

**Step 3: Open the folder in VS Code**
- File → Open Folder → select your folder
- Right-click in the sidebar → New Terminal

**Step 4: Install Claude Code**
- Go to Claude Code docs (search "claude code install quick start")
- Copy the Mac or Windows install command
- Paste into terminal → Claude Code installs automatically

**Step 5: Connect your Claude account**
- Type `claude` in terminal
- Choose "Claude account with subscription" (Pro or Max plan)
- Authorize via browser
- Done — Claude Code is running

**Recommended plan:** Start with Claude Pro ($20/month). Max plan ($200/month) is for when you need more usage.

## 6. Backing Up to GitHub (Cloud)

**Why:** If your computer dies, you lose your entire workspace with all your skills and work history. GitHub saves a cloud copy.

### Setup Steps

1. Go to github.com → create account
2. Click "New" → name it (e.g., "shiny-workspace")
3. Set to **Private** (your workspace is a high-value asset)
4. Click "Create repository"
5. Copy the repo URL
6. In Claude Code, paste the URL and say:

```
Please push this current folder to this repo: [paste URL]
```

7. Claude pushes everything to the cloud

**After this, your skills and workspace are safe even if your computer breaks.**

## 11. Bidding on Jobs — How to Write Winning Proposals

### Job Selection Rules

- **Posts with 50+ proposals:** Skip — too competitive
- **Posts with 20-50 proposals:** Good range
- **Posts with 5-10 proposals posted recently (within hours):** Jump on immediately

**Speed matters.** When someone posts, be the first to apply.

### Using the Proposal Skill

1. In Claude Code: type `/proposal`
2. Copy the entire job post (Ctrl+A)
3. Paste into Claude: "Write a proposal for this job"
4. Claude will flag red flags automatically (e.g., "$10 fixed price, client from Nigeria — probably just looking to hire, skip this one")

### Anatomy of a Winning Proposal

**What NOT to do:**
- Generic template that's clearly AI-generated
- Just listing features ("I have 6 years of experience in...")
- No personalization to the specific job

**What TO do:**

1. **The hook** (what they see before clicking "more"): Make it instantly relevant to THEIR job
2. **Educate them on what's possible:** Show something they don't know that you can do
3. **Make a Loom video:** 60-90 second screen recording showing relevant capability. Point the camera at your face (makes you human), show a real tool related to their request
4. **Add a screenshot:** Booked calendar, completed project, etc.
5. **Write "This is not written by AI"** (and then make sure it isn't)
6. **End with a question:** Ask about their current setup — shows genuine interest

### The Loom Video (Game-Changer)

Every single proposal should have a Loom video. Record yourself explaining:
- Something technical about their specific use case that they might not know
- Show your screen while explaining
- Include your face in the corner (big, not small)
- Keep it 60-90 seconds

> *"Almost no other freelancers are doing this. When every other proposal is a template, yours with a Loom stands out completely."*

### From the Buyer's Perspective

Albert showed what his job posts look like as a buyer:
- 11 proposals received
- Most were from Pakistan, Nigeria, India — generic templates
- Almost all sounded AI-generated
- The ones that stood out were personal, specific, and included Looms

**Translation:** Your competition is not that good. Western candidates with personal, specific proposals win.

## 15. Sales Call Rules — Before, During, After

### BEFORE THE CALL

**Rule 1: Never mention price before the call.**

If they ask: "How much does it cost?" → Don't answer.

Why: They'll judge you on the price without knowing your value. A $2,000 price looks expensive without context. After seeing the value, $2,000 looks cheap.

**Rule 2: Send confirmations and reminders on every channel.**

Timeline:
- Immediately after they book: Confirmation on email + phone
- Every 3 days leading up: "Looking forward to our call"
- 1 day before: "Excited to see you tomorrow"
- 1 hour before: "See you in an hour"
- 5 minutes before: "Just finishing another call, see you in 5"

**Pro tip:** If you build a demo before the call, mention it:

> *"I've prepared a demo for you based on your own company so you can see exactly how this will look for your business."*

Now they feel guilty if they don't show up.

**Rule 3: Never skip the call.** Don't sell over DMs or email. The call is where you close.

## 16. On the Call — The Doctor Approach

### The Core Principle

**You talk 20% of the time. They talk 80% of the time.**

When you talk, ask questions. When they talk, they answer your questions.

### Why This Works

When YOU say your service is valuable → they might believe you
When THEY say they need it → they convince themselves

The doctor analogy:
- A doctor doesn't prescribe medicine at the door
- First: asks where it hurts, how long, what makes it worse
- Then: diagnoses the problem
- Then: prescribes the solution

**You do the same thing:**

1. Ask questions that surface a pain
2. Get THEM to articulate the pain
3. Position your service as the solution to THEIR articulated pain

### Example: Google Reviews

> You: "I can see your competitor has 150 Google reviews and you have 5, one of which is from your mom. Who do you think a new customer is going to choose when they compare you both?"
>
> Them: "...probably my competitor."

Now THEY said it, not you. You haven't sold anything yet. You've just held up a mirror.

### The Call Structure

1. **Doctor questions** → surface the pain
2. **Confirm the pain** → "So it sounds like the main issue is X?"
3. **Show the solution** → demo or explanation
4. **Handle objections** → questions that show concern, not rejection
5. **Close** → mention price (only now)
6. **Shut up** → let them decide

### What NOT to Do

The "slideshow pitch" mistake:
- Open presentation about your product
- List features
- Ask "do you want to buy?"

Problems with this approach:
- You don't identify their specific pain
- They judge you on price, not value
- They zone out during the slideshow
- They say no and you don't know why

## 17. Closing Deals — Key Rules

### Rule 1: Always Book the Next Call On This Call

> "Great, happy you're on board. Let me put the next call in our calendar right now — I'll have [X] ready for you by then."

If you don't do this: they ghost you. Simple as that.

### Rule 2: Always Take Payment On the Call

Get payment information committed before they leave. Use Stripe:

1. Go to Stripe → Payment Links → Create
2. Product name: "Checking card" (or similar innocuous name)
3. Recurring: $1/year
4. Create link
5. Send them this link on the call
6. They fill in their card info
7. You now have their payment method on file
8. You can set up their actual subscription without involving them again

**Even for free trials:** Get the $1 payment link. "It's just to save your card. I'll refund the $1."

### Bonus: The Proof of Concept Offer

When someone is hesitant about price:

> "Here's our satisfaction guarantee. You pay $1,000 upfront. I build the entire [website/system] so you can see everything — every page, every feature. When I show it to you, if you're not happy with it, you get a full refund. No questions asked."

**Why this works:**
- Removes all risk for the client
- Makes you look extremely confident
- You still get commitment upfront
- Albert has given this offer many times and has NEVER had to refund anyone

## 18. After the Call — Retention & Growth

### Do a Hell of a Job

Your reputation is everything when you're starting. Go all out for your first clients. This generates:
- Testimonials (essential for trust)
- Referrals (free new clients)
- Case studies (marketing material)
- Upwork reviews (builds your profile)

### Bi-Weekly Calls (Most Agencies Skip This)

Schedule a call with every client every two weeks:
- Review performance
- Discuss improvements
- Upsell new services
- Ask for referrals

**Why this matters for churn:**
- Agency average churn: 20-30% (clients stay ~3 months)
- With bi-weekly calls: churn drops to ~5% (clients stay much longer)
- At $2,000/month, every extra month a client stays = $2,000

### Upselling

After helping a client build a website:
- "Would you also like an AI agent to answer phone calls from the visitors?"
- "Would you like Google Ads to drive more traffic to your new site?"
- "Would you like an automated follow-up sequence for leads?"

**Never stop at the first service.** Upselling is free revenue — you already paid to acquire the client.

### Asking for Referrals (The Right Way)

❌ Wrong: "Do you know anyone else who could use this?"

✅ Right: "Do you know any plumbers, electricians, or landscapers in your area who might need this?"

The specific question activates their memory. Generic questions don't.

**Incentivize referrals:** "If you refer someone and they become a client, I'll pay you 20% of everything they pay me."

### Step 2: Create an Implementation Plan

After the build plan is locked, create the implementation plan:

- Each task broken into substeps (checkboxes)
- Clear sequence: file structure → database → auth → frontend → backend → testing

**Again, don't start building until this is complete.**

### Step 3: Build (Step-by-Step)

- You can catch issues between steps

## 27. Pricing Strategy

### The Golden Rule: 5x Return on Investment

Always aim to give your clients a **5x return** on what they pay you.

**Formula:**
- Calculate what you save/make them
- Divide by 5
- That's your price

**Example — Voice agents replacing support reps:**
- 10 reps → 5 reps needed = 5 reps eliminated
- Each rep costs $2,000/month = $10,000 saved
- Your charge: $10,000 ÷ 5 = **$2,000/month**
- Client still saves $8,000/month → zero reason to churn

**Example — Revenue-generating system:**
- AI cold email system generates $100,000/month in new revenue
- Gross profit at 40% margins = $40,000
- Your charge: $40,000 ÷ 5 = **$8,000/month**

### The Three Pricing Structures

**1. Result-Based (Best when trackable)**

Take 20% of gross profit you generate for them.

- No upfront risk for client (huge sales advantage)
- If you're good: makes you far more money than retainer
- Albert example: one client worth $240,000 LTV vs ~$12,000 on retainer

**Best for:** Lead generation, sales automation, anything with directly attributable revenue

**Worst for:** Support agents, brand awareness, hard-to-track outcomes

---

**2. Upfront + Recurring (Best for most setups)**

- Upfront setup fee: $1,000–$5,000
- Monthly recurring: $200–$1,000

**Always add the Satisfaction Guarantee:**

> "I'm going to build the entire thing. If you see it and don't like it, you get a full refund. No questions asked."

**Why this works:**
- Removes all risk for the client
- You still get upfront commitment
- If you do good work, you never have to refund
- Makes you appear extremely confident

---

**3. Tiered Pricing (For usage-based services)**

Like a SaaS product:
- Plan 1: Up to 100 calls/month — $X
- Plan 2: Up to 1,000 calls/month — $Y
- Plan 3: Up to 5,000 calls/month — $Z
- Plan 4: 5,000+ — custom pricing

**Why:** Protects you from a massive client blowing up your costs when you quoted them a flat rate.

### What NOT to Do

- Don't mention price before showing value
- Don't sell "AI" — sell the outcome ("save $10,000/month" not "using the newest models")
- Don't include the software cost separately — bundle everything into one clean price (confused people don't buy)

- "I'm the only one on sales calls" → hire a salesperson

> *"Don't automate everything. Sometimes the best solution is just to hire someone. Building an automation that takes 3 weeks when you could hire someone in 3 days is a mistake."*

You don't need to quit your job to start this business.

- **5 AM - 9 AM:** Build your business (4 hours — your freshest thinking)
