## 1. Overview: Bangladesh's Export Reality

### Current State
- Bangladesh currently exports **$48 billion per year**
- This is considered **half of what it should be** — the potential target is **$100 billion**
- 20 years ago, the import-export gap was small (import: ~$20B, export: ~$14–15B); now import has nearly doubled while export hasn't kept pace
- **80%+ of exports** come from the garments sector (RMG)
- Other major sectors: Home textiles, jute, leather, fish/seafood, agro-products

### The Mindset Problem
- Bangladeshis are **highly skilled at local business** but rarely think globally
- Many entrepreneurs give local business 10–15 years but expect export results in 3–5 months
- Bangladesh captures only **~2% of world market** — 98% remains untouched

## 2. Why Export is Harder Than Import

Export is significantly more complex than import for the following reasons:

| Factor | Import | Export |
|---|---|---|
| Direction of risk | Goods come to you | You send goods out, often before full payment |
| Compliance | Local rules only | Must meet destination country's rules |
| Market knowledge | Local demand is visible | Overseas demand requires research |
| Buyer verification | Suppliers are often known | Buyers can be fraudulent |
| Certification | Minimal | Country-specific certifications needed |

A small mistake in export can cause **catastrophic loss** — e.g., sending a container worth ৳20 lakh to a port where the product isn't registered means the entire shipment gets stuck and must be returned at your cost.

## 3. Export Readiness: The 5 Core Requirements

Before starting export, you must assess yourself on five dimensions:

### 3.1 Exportable Product
- The product **must have overseas market demand** — you cannot force a product
- Research demand before choosing the product (see Section 4)
- Do not enter a market just because friends or relatives are there

### 3.2 Competitive Pricing
- Your product must be **price-competitive internationally**
- Bangladesh competes with China and India on most products — except garments
- If your raw material import duties are high, your final product can't compete
  - Bangladesh average import duty: **27%**
  - India average import duty: **~20%**
  - This cost disadvantage flows into the export price

### 3.3 Product Customization Capability
- Chinese manufacturers can produce **any spec, any price, any quantity**
- Bangladesh struggles with customization requests
- **Real example:** A Lexus biscuit (250g) was shown to an Indian buyer who needed 30g packs; a Gulf buyer needed 90g. Bangladesh couldn't fulfill either — China could.
- Solution: Build customization capacity or find niche products where you already have an edge

### 3.4 Export Skills & Knowledge
- Most entrepreneurs, universities, and colleges **do not teach export skills**
- Lack of information leads to: trial and error → mistakes → financial loss → exit
- Skills needed: Documentation, compliance, market research, buyer communication, negotiation

### 3.5 Minimum Time Commitment
- Minimum **3 to 5 years** to grow a real export business
- Year 1: Enter 5 markets, test the process
- Year 2: Check for repeat orders; scale to 10 markets
- Year 3+: Focus on volume in proven markets
- Expecting results in 3 months is a recipe for failure

## 4. Market Research: Choosing the Right Market

**Key principle:** Market research must be done **6 months before starting**, not after.

### Step-by-Step Market Selection Process

**Step 1 — Find the top 20 demand markets**
- Use HS code of your product in international trade databases (see Section 16)
- List the top 20 countries where your product is in demand

**Step 2 — Eliminate highly regulated markets**
- Remove countries with complex compliance and certification requirements
- USA, UK, EU = very high compliance (food, drug, environmental standards)
- Fewer compliances → easier entry
- This typically narrows the list to ~10 countries

**Step 3 — Assess competition**
- Of those 10, find markets where competition is lowest
- Where can you enter with minimum resistance?
- Now you're at ~5 countries

**Step 4 — Check cultural compatibility**
- Does your product suit the culture of those 5 markets?
- Example: Selling T-shirts in conservative Arabian markets = difficult
- Match product nature to cultural habits and preferences

**Result:** You now have **1–2 target markets** to start with. Lock these in and find buyers within them.

### What is an HS Code?
- **HS = Harmonized System Code** (also called Harmonized Commodity Description and Coding System)
- A universal numeric code that classifies every product in global trade
- Allows comparison across countries regardless of local product names
- Example: "Toast biscuit," "sweet biscuit," "salty biscuit" all fall under different HS codes in the international "rusk" category
- **Where to find HS codes in Bangladesh:**
  - Bangladesh Customs — First Schedule Book
  - EPB (Export Promotion Bureau) website — monthly shipment data in Excel by HS code

## 5. Choosing the Right Product

### What Makes a Product "Export-Ready"?

1. **Global demand exists** — Verify through market research tools, not assumptions
2. **Highly produced in Bangladesh** — Easier to source, lower cost base
3. **Value-added, not commodity** — Standing out in crowded markets requires differentiation

### Value Addition Strategy
- Everyone exports mango → you export mango powder or dried mango slices
- Everyone exports potato → you export potato chips
- Everyone exports jute bags → you export jute bags in the trending natural color (not dyed)
- Value addition = better margins + differentiation from bulk commodity sellers

### Market Trend Alignment
- **Check what buyers actually want**, not what you assume they want
- Use tools like Walmart product listings, trade fair catalogs, B2B platforms to understand current trends
- Example: Jute bags — market may prefer natural color, but exporters keep selling colored bags

### Innovative/Future-Proof Products
- **Eco-friendly products** — The entire world is moving toward sustainability
- Bangladeshi women artisans produce many eco-friendly handicraft products that large companies simply source and re-export
- **Gift market in the USA** — enormous demand for eco/handmade items
- **Organic food** — Growing demand globally; Gulf is an easy first market

## 6. Top Export Opportunities from Bangladesh

### 6.1 Agriculture & Agro-Processing
Bangladesh is a **Top 3 global producer** of fruits and vegetables. Yet vast quantities are wasted:

- **20–40% of agro produce is wasted** in Bangladesh before it reaches market
- Annual mango production: **24 lakh metric tons** → Only ~500 tons exported
- ৳200 crore worth of mangoes went to waste in Rajshahi in a single year

**High-opportunity agro products:**
| Product | Market | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Potato | UAE, Malaysia, Saudi | Bangladesh has surplus; Dubai price ~2x Bangladesh price |
| Vegetables (various) | Gulf countries | Gulf has almost no domestic production |
| Mango | Gulf, Malaysia, India | Needs Global GAP certification for premium markets |
| Jackfruit | Global | Bangladesh = 2nd largest producer; dozens of by-products possible |
| Hilsa fish | Diaspora markets | Cultural demand; limited by bone structure for Western markets |
| Freshwater fish (Pangas, Tilapia) | China, Southeast Asia | Top global producer; growing exports |
| Fish scales | Industrial use | Already being exported |

### 6.2 Garments & Apparel
- Bangladesh's strongest international identity
- **T-shirts** — Best starting product for new exporters (proven demand, global supply chain)
- Home textiles (bed sheets, curtains) — Growing export segment
- Gap: Bangladesh has **no internationally recognized garment brand** — opportunity exists

### 6.3 Jute & Jute Products
- Bangladesh has **the world's best quality raw jute** (acknowledged by international consultants)
- Netherlands, EU countries buy raw jute from Bangladesh, manufacture by-products, and re-export globally
- **Value-added jute products** have massive potential
- **Largest trade fair:** Ambiente Fair, Frankfurt, Germany (50–60 Bangladeshi companies attend)

### 6.4 Leather & Leather Goods
- 150+ leather export-ready factories in Bangladesh
- Challenge: Market is **controlled by Indian and Chinese syndicates**
- Opportunity: Foreign direct investment would help break the monopoly
- Despite syndicate issues, 150+ companies are already successfully exporting

### 6.5 Non-Traditional / Surprising Exports
| Product | Value | Market |
|---|---|---|
| Cow offal (tripe, organs) | ৳300 crore/year | China (Chittagong-based businesses) |
| Islamic prayer caps (Taqiyah) | ৳1,000 crore/year | Middle East |

| Kumilla Roshmolai | Growing | Bangladeshi diaspora in USA |

## 7. Finding International Buyers

### Method 1: Online Lead Generation (AI & Google)

**Using AI (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.):**
- Prompt example: *"I want to export vegetables and potatoes to Malaysia. Who are the top importers?"*
- Real result: AI returned 5 leads; the #1 was indeed the largest Bangladeshi produce buyer in Malaysia (Selangor market)
- AI can identify company names, LinkedIn profiles, websites

**Using Google Extensions:**
- Find importers using product category searches
- Tools like **Apollo.io** — extract LinkedIn contact details of importers
- Build a list of 20–50 qualified leads per market

### Method 2: Overseas Trade Associations

Every country has a trade promotion authority. Contact them **before visiting** the country:
- **Malaysia:** MATRADE (Malaysia External Trade Development Corporation)
- **Bangladesh:** EPB (Export Promotion Bureau) — Bangladesh's equivalent
- **How to approach:** Send a formal email introducing yourself, your product, and your intent to visit. They will respond and give you an appointment.

> *Real example: "I emailed MATRADE Malaysia. They gave me an in-person appointment even though they don't know me personally. This is standard."*

### Method 3: Overseas Embassies — Commercial Counselors

- **Do not contact the Ambassador** — contact the **Commercial Counselor**
- Their WhatsApp and email are often publicly available
- They actively want to help Bangladeshi exporters
- Real case: Commercial Counselor of Malaysia personally helped at the airport when entry was being questioned

### Method 4: Overseas Chambers of Commerce

- Every major city has a Chamber of Commerce
- **Dubai Chamber of Commerce** — can provide leads and contacts for UAE market
- Contact them online for a membership/appointment before visiting

### Method 5: Export Stakeholder Network

People who already have buyer relationships:
- **C&F Agents** (Clearing & Forwarding)
- **Freight Forwarding Agents**
- **Banks** (trade finance departments)
- **Audit firms**

These stakeholders have 10–20 year relationships with international buyers. They can refer your first buyer — **if you network properly**.

> *"Your first buyer will often come through these stakeholders — not through cold calling."*

**Networking rule:** Don't message "please give me a buyer." Build a relationship first, understand their work, add value — then ask.

### Method 6: B2B Data Platforms

| Platform | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Alibaba** | Very expensive; high competition |
| **Volza.com** (volza.vn) | Free version shows shipment data; highly recommended |
| **Kompass.com** | Global B2B directory |
| **Europages.com** | Excellent for European buyers |
| **UN Comtrade** | Official trade data; free |

**Volza example:** Search Bangladesh exports by HS code — shows exactly how many shipments left Bangladesh, to which countries, through which exporters.

### Method 7: Social Media & Diaspora Groups

- Search Facebook groups of Bangladeshi diaspora in Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, UAE, etc.
- Post your product in these groups with price and contact
- Real buyers (and some fraudsters) will reach out
- Filter legitimate buyers using the verification process (Section 8)

### Method 8: International Trade Fairs

**Most important fairs for Bangladesh:**

| Fair | Location | Product Focus | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gulf Food** | Dubai, UAE | Food & Agro | February, 2nd week (annual) |
| **Mihas** | Malaysia | Halal food, agro | Periodic |
| **Ambiente** | Frankfurt, Germany | Jute, leather, handicrafts | Annual |
| **Hong Kong Fair** | Hong Kong | General merchandise | Annual |

**Strategy for trade fairs:**
1. Attend as a visitor first — learn the landscape
2. Have your product samples ready
3. Prepare a 1-minute pitch (buyers give you 1 minute maximum at busy fairs)
4. Know your product's USP cold

### Method 9: Market Visit + Fair Combination

- Send a team member (or go yourself) to the target market
- Combine the visit with a trade fair in that country
- Ambiente Fair (Germany): 5,000 buyers attend — impossible to see all, but extremely valuable
- **Rule:** Only visit when a major fair is happening in that city/country

## 8. Verifying a Buyer: Avoiding Fraud

Many buyers are not genuine. Use these checks:

### Check 1: Know Their Import Duty Structure
- Ask: *"What are the import duties, VAT, and taxes for this product in your country?"*
- A genuine buyer knows this information
- A fraudulent or unqualified buyer won't know
- Example: Nepal has a free trade agreement with India for certain goods — a Nepali buyer asking for the same from Bangladesh may not actually convert

### Check 2: Ask About Compliance Requirements
- Genuine buyers know exactly what certifications and registrations they need
- Example: Saudi Arabia requires SFDA (Saudi Food and Drug Authority) registration
- If a buyer doesn't know this and you ship without it, your container will be stuck at port

### Check 3: Marketing & Distribution Plan
- Ask: *"Where and how will you distribute this product?"*
- Red flag: "I'll cover all of Saudi Arabia" — No single distributor covers a country the size of Saudi Arabia
- A real buyer has a specific territory: e.g., Riyadh metro area, or 3 specific provinces

### Check 4: Catalog Review & Product Selection
- Give the buyer your product catalog
- Ask them to name **4–5 specific products** they want to order
- If they say "give me everything" without looking — not a serious buyer
- Real buyers are specific about what they need

### Check 5: Payment Terms Test
- Require minimum **30% advance payment** before shipment
- A legitimate buyer will negotiate but will pay an advance
- A fraudulent buyer will disappear at the payment discussion
- **Never accept 10% advance** — this doesn't even cover freight costs if the deal falls apart

### Check 6: Online Presence Verification
- Check their website, social media, business registration
- In Gulf countries: **Do they have a physical signboard/office?**
- A buyer with a real business has a warehouse, delivery vehicles, staff — not just a flat and a phone

### Check 7: Reference Check
- Ask: *"Which other companies (principals) do you work with?"*
- Verify with those companies (e.g., if they claim to work with ACI or Square, call ACI/Square to confirm)

### Check 8: Embassy Verification
- Take the buyer's trade license or business ID to the local embassy for verification
- Embassies can cross-check if a business is registered and legitimate

### Check 9: Credit Report
- Request a **credit report** through your bank (costs ~$100)
- International credit rating agencies maintain records for all major businesses
- Red flag on credit report = very cautious payment terms (all advance)
- Even a buyer with a bad credit report can be worked with — on 100% advance payment only

## 9. The Role of Online Presence

**Core principle:** A buyer in Dubai cannot find you if you have no online presence. You must be findable before they can buy from you.

### Why Online Presence Matters for Export
- International buyers **Google** potential suppliers
- They check social media, LinkedIn, websites before contacting
- Without online presence, even a great product won't attract buyers
- Buyers also need to **trust you** before placing an order — your digital footprint is your credibility

### Content Strategy for Exporters
- Post regularly about your product, production process, quality standards
- Show your factory, raw materials, packaging — tell the story behind the product
- **Real result:** The expert's team gets regular international buyer inquiries purely from LinkedIn and Facebook content
- Example: An agro-content creator in Bangladesh started getting inquiries from Saudi and Gulf buyers because her content kept appearing in their feeds

### Localized Targeting
- For diaspora markets (Saudi, UAE, Malaysia), target Bangladeshi community groups with your product posts
- Buyers will reach out directly

### Online Presence Timeline
- Spend **6 months** building online presence before doing a market visit
- By then, buyers will be knocking on your door before you even arrive

### Story-Telling & Product Branding
- Explain **where your product comes from, how it's made, what makes it special**
- Example: Supima cotton (Arizona, USA) — only a tiny amount is grown → built into a premium brand story
- Bangladesh equivalents: Cox's Bazar sea salt, Rajshahi silk, Cumilla Roshmolai — all have stories
- Story = premium pricing + buyer attraction

## 10. Licensing & Documentation

### Required Licenses for Export from Bangladesh

| License | Issuing Authority | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Trade License** | Local City Corporation / Union Parishad | Basic business registration; even a Union Parishad license is valid |
| **TIN Certificate** | NBR (National Board of Revenue) | Standard |
| **BIN (VAT Registration)** | NBR | Standard |
| **Chamber Membership** | Local Chamber of Commerce | Required for export; cost varies: district chambers as low as ৳2,000 |
| **ERC (Export Registration Certificate)** | EPB (Export Promotion Bureau) | Core export license; Govt. fee: ৳11,500 + 15% VAT |

> **Important:** You do NOT need Dhaka Chamber membership (which costs more). Any recognized Chamber of Commerce in your district is sufficient.

### Shipment Documents (Typically 5–6 Required)

1. **Commercial Invoice** — Price, quantity, buyer/seller details
2. **Packing List** — Detailed contents of the shipment
3. **Bill of Lading (B/L)** — Transport document (sea); Airway Bill (air)
4. **Certificate of Origin** — Issued by Chamber of Commerce
5. **Phytosanitary Certificate** (for agro products) — Issued by Department of Agricultural Extension
6. **LC / TT payment document** — Banking instruction

For complex markets (EU, USA), additional certificates may be required:
- Global GAP Certification (food safety)
- BRC (British Retail Consortium) Certification
- Quarantine Certificates

### Agricultural Export Certifications by Market

| Market | Required Certification |
|---|---|
| Gulf (UAE, Saudi, Qatar, Oman) | Phytosanitary Certificate only (relatively easy) |
| Malaysia, Southeast Asia | Phytosanitary + some Halal certs |
| EU / UK | Global GAP, BRC, multiple quarantine certs |
| USA | FDA registration, USDA requirements, possibly HACCP |
| China | Traceability + Global GAP + no pesticide residue |

## 11. Payment Terms & Risk Management

### Standard Payment Terms

| Method | Description | When to Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Advance / TT (Telegraphic Transfer)** | Buyer pays before shipment | New buyers, small amounts |
| **LC at Sight (Letter of Credit)** | Bank guarantees payment on document presentation | Large orders, established relationship |
| **30% Advance + 70% on B/L Draft** | Split payment around shipment | Standard for moderate-trust buyers |

### Rules of Thumb

- **Minimum 30% advance** for new buyers — non-negotiable
- Never accept 10% — freight costs alone won't be covered if deal fails
- 100% advance for buyers with bad credit history
- Negotiate: *"Our accounts team has set this as standard policy"* — depersonalizes the request

### How to Negotiate Payment Terms Confidently

- Don't be afraid to state your payment terms
- Legitimate buyers will negotiate from a real position
- Fraudsters will disappear when payment is required
- Frame it as company policy, not personal preference

### Distribution of Cost Responsibilities

| Responsibility | Who Pays |
|---|---|
| Product registration in destination country | **Buyer pays** |
| Bangladesh-side licenses and compliance | **Seller pays** |
| Freight (shipping/air cargo) | Negotiated (usually seller under FOB/CIF terms) |
| Destination port handling | Buyer pays |

### Minimum Investment (Realistic Figures)

| Item | Estimated Cost |
|---|---|
| ERC License | ৳11,500 + VAT (~৳13,250) |
| Chamber Membership | ৳2,000 – ৳10,000 |
| Trade License + TIN + BIN | Already needed for any business |
| Initial product (test shipment) | ৳50,000 – ৳2,00,000 |
| Online presence (content creation) | ৳0 – minimal |
| Air courier for test sample | ৳5,000 – ৳20,000 |
| Trade fair visit | ৳50,000 – ৳2,00,000 |
| **Total to start (minimum)** | **~৳1 – 2 lakh** |

> A full container of potato: ~৳8–10 lakh product cost + ৳3–4 lakh freight to Dubai. But **you don't need to start with a full container.** Start with a courier shipment worth ৳50,000–৳1,00,000 to test the market.

### The Test Shipment Principle

1. Produce or source a small batch
2. Ship via courier (not container) to your target buyer
3. Observe: Does the buyer like it? Can they actually sell it?
4. Gather feedback on size, taste, packaging, price
5. Only then consider full container shipments

## 14. Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Not researching destination regulations
- Sending food products to Saudi Arabia without SFDA registration → container stuck at port
- Always verify destination country regulations BEFORE shipping

### Mistake 2: Choosing wrong markets
- Garments → should go to USA/EU, not Bhutan
- Vegetables → Gulf/Middle East, not EU (EU has very high compliance)
- Match product to market systematically, not by personal connection

### Mistake 3: Giving exclusive partnerships too early
- An African buyer came to the office, took the product catalog, and declared himself the "exclusive representative" without any contract
- **Rule:** Never give exclusivity on the basis of the first shipment. Exclusivity requires proven performance and proper contract.

### Mistake 4: Neglecting product customization
- Your 250g product won't sell in a market that needs 30g or 90g packs
- Either build customization capacity or choose products you can already customize

### Mistake 5: No online presence
- Buyers actively search for suppliers online
- If you're invisible digitally, you're invisible to international buyers

### Mistake 6: Expecting quick results
- Export is a 3–5 year commitment
- Asking a consultant to generate $3 million in 3 months (after 15 years of local-only business) is unrealistic

### Mistake 7: Sending 100% shipment on 10% advance
- If the buyer defaults, you've lost the product AND the freight cost
- Minimum 30% advance protects you

### Mistake 8: One-time outreach and giving up
- International buyers get 400+ messages from Bangladesh every day
- Send regular, valuable content and follow-up
- Chinese and Vietnamese suppliers chase buyers weekly — and win orders

## 15. Step-by-Step Roadmap for Beginners

### Phase 1: Prepare (Month 1–6)

- [ ] **Choose one product** — either agro, T-shirt, or jute product
- [ ] **Validate demand** — Use Trademap, OECD, Volza, UN Comtrade to confirm international demand
- [ ] **Get licensed** — Trade license + TIN + BIN + Chamber Membership + ERC
- [ ] **Open a bank account** with export-friendly facilities (go through a reference if possible)
- [ ] **Build online presence** — Create LinkedIn company page + Facebook page; post product content daily
- [ ] **Research target market** — Follow the 4-step market selection process (Section 4)
- [ ] **Identify 1–2 markets** — Recommended start: Gulf (UAE/Saudi) or Malaysia

### Phase 2: Find Your First Buyer (Month 3–9)

- [ ] **Search for Bangladeshi diaspora buyers** in your target market
- [ ] **Contact EPB** for any upcoming trade missions or fair subsidies
- [ ] **Email the Commercial Counselor** at the Bangladesh Embassy in your target country
- [ ] **Contact the overseas trade body** (MATRADE for Malaysia, etc.)
- [ ] **Generate 20 leads** using AI tools, Apollo.io, LinkedIn, Volza
- [ ] **Send connection requests + product intro** via email/WhatsApp (weekly follow-up)

### Phase 3: Test Shipment (Month 6–12)

- [ ] **Negotiate with 1 buyer** — discuss product, price, payment, compliance
- [ ] **Verify the buyer** using all 9 checks (Section 8)
- [ ] **Prepare product samples** — Always keep samples ready at your desk
- [ ] **Send courier shipment** worth ৳50,000–৳1,00,000 as a test
- [ ] **Collect feedback** — Did it arrive well? Did the buyer sell it? Any quality issues?
- [ ] **Adjust product/packaging** based on feedback

### Phase 4: Scale (Year 2–3)

- [ ] Repeat orders = proven product-market fit
- [ ] Enter 5 markets in Year 1 → scale to 10 markets in Year 2
- [ ] Start attending international trade fairs (Gulf Food in February, Ambiente in Frankfurt)
- [ ] Add value to your product (processing, packaging, branding)
- [ ] Build a dedicated export team

## 17. Mindset: From Local Player to Global Player

### The Core Shift Required

> *"We are playing in Rahmatganj or Farazganj. But we should be playing at Real Madrid or Barcelona level."*

Most Bangladeshi producers **design products for local consumption** — not for global markets. This is the fundamental barrier.

### What Global Mindset Looks Like

| Local Mindset | Global Mindset |
|---|---|
| "I'll sell in Dhaka first" | "I'll test in UAE while building local" |
